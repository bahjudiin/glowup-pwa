# 25 — Bloodwork, Metrics & Tracking

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**. This file covers *what to measure, how often, and what the numbers mean* — with the explicit caveat that results must be interpreted by a clinician.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Five numbers dominate**: **blood pressure, waist circumference, HbA1c (or fasting glucose), lipids (with non-HDL/ApoB where available), and liver enzymes (ALT)**. These are cheap, widely available, and actionable.
- **Two numbers are worth checking once and only repeating if symptomatic or indicated**: **vitamin D** (if low sun exposure) and **ferritin/CBC** (if fatigue or hair shedding).
- **HbA1c ≥6.5%, fasting glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L (126 mg/dL), BP ≥140/90 (clinic) or ≥135/85 (home), waist >94–102 cm depending on ancestry, non-HDL ≥4.9 mmol/L (190 mg/dL)** are the levels that typically trigger clinical action — *your clinician applies local thresholds.*
- **Body-composition tracking** is best done with a **weekly weight average + monthly waist + photos + strength logs**. DEXA is optional 1–2×/year; bioimpedance body-fat readings are too variable to act on.
- **Don't over-test.** Frequent testing without action is anxiety, not health. Annual testing (or 3–5 yearly if low risk) is enough for most healthy men.
- **Biotin supplements distort immunoassays** (thyroid, troponin) — stop 48–72 h before any blood draw.

## 2. What the evidence establishes

### 2.1 Which tests and why
| Test | What it detects | Frequency (healthy man) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blood pressure** (home, upper-arm) | Hypertension — the biggest silent risk in men | Every 3–6 months at home; annually at clinic | **High** (SPRINT: intensive control reduces events and mortality) |
| **Waist circumference** | Central adiposity / cardiometabolic risk | Monthly | **High** (observational) |
| **HbA1c / fasting glucose** | Prediabetes, diabetes | Every 1–3 years (annually if overweight/at risk) | **High** |
| **Lipid panel (incl. non-HDL)** | Atherogenic risk | Every 1–5 years depending on risk | **High** |
| **ALT/AST** | Fatty liver, alcohol, drug effects | Every 1–3 years; more often if overweight/drinking | **Moderate-High** |
| **Creatinine/eGFR** | Kidney function (also relevant if you take creatine or high protein) | Every 1–3 years | **High** (for kidney disease detection) |
| **CBC, ferritin** | Anaemia, iron deficiency (fatigue, hair shedding) | If symptomatic | **Moderate-High** |
| **Vitamin D (25-OH)** | Deficiency | Once; repeat only if low or risk factors | **High** (for deficiency detection) |
| **TSH** | Thyroid dysfunction (fatigue, weight change, hair loss) | If symptomatic | **High** |
| **PSA** | Prostate cancer screening | Shared decision, typically 50–70 (earlier with family history/ancestry risk) | **Moderate** (benefit/harm trade-off) |
| **Testosterone (2 × morning total T)** | Hypogonadism | Only if symptomatic (low libido, fatigue, loss of muscle) | **Moderate** |
| **Full-body MRI, biological-age/telomere tests, gut microbiome panels** | — | **Not recommended** | **Insufficient** |

### 2.2 Interpretation caveats
- **Reference ranges differ by lab and country**; a value just outside a range is not automatically a disease.
- **One abnormal test is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis** — most are repeated before action.
- **Context matters:** creatine supplementation raises serum creatinine (not kidney damage per se); hard training raises ALT/AST and CK; finasteride halves PSA; biotin distorts immunoassays.
- **Fasting vs non-fasting lipids:** non-fasting is acceptable for most screening (guideline-level).

### 2.3 Tracking body composition without over-engineering
- **Weekly weight average** (7-day mean) — the core metric.
- **Waist at navel** — monthly; tracks visceral fat better than weight alone.
- **Photos** — every 4 weeks, same light/pose.
- **Strength logs** — the best proxy for muscle retention.
- **Steps** — weekly average.
- **Sleep** — hours + subjective quality.
- **Bioimpedance body fat**: noisy (±3–5%); use trends only, never single readings.
- **DEXA**: useful 1–2×/year if affordable; not required.

## 3. Evidence quality

**High** for BP, lipids, HbA1c and waist as risk markers and for the benefit of treating hypertension/hyperlipidaemia/diabetes. **Moderate** for PSA (net benefit debated), testosterone (only in symptomatic men), and DEXA-guided decisions. **Insufficient** for biological-age tests, microbiome panels, and routine full-body imaging in asymptomatic men.

## 4. Effectiveness (what tracking actually buys)

| Metric | What changes when you track it |
|---|---|
| Weekly weight average | Enables the 2-kg correction rule (file 06) — the single most valuable tracking habit |
| Waist | Detects visceral fat change when the scale is flat (recomposition) |
| Photos | Prevents "I see no progress" despair during recomposition |
| Strength log | Detects muscle loss early during a deficit |
| Steps | The easiest lever to increase adherence |
| BP | Detects silent hypertension; verifies that lifestyle changes work |

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

**A one-day baseline, then annual:**
1. Get a **home BP monitor** and take 3 readings on 2 days (average).
2. Order a **basic panel**: CBC, glucose/HbA1c, lipids, ALT/AST, creatinine/eGFR, and vitamin D (if low sun).
3. **Measure waist** and take photos.
4. Record a **strength baseline** (e.g., 5-rep max on 3 lifts) and a **walking/running benchmark**.
5. Repeat annually (or 3–5 yearly if everything is normal and you're low risk).

## 6. Free and cheap alternatives

| Expensive test | Free/cheap substitute |
|---|---|
| Body-composition scans | Waist + photos + strength log |
| Metabolic rate testing | Formula + 2–3 weeks of weight-trend calibration |
| VO₂max lab test | A timed 1.5-mile run or 5 km walk test (rough estimate) |
| Biological-age panels | Nothing — skip |
| Continuous glucose monitor (no diabetes) | Structured meals + annual HbA1c |
| Full-body MRI | Guideline-based screening only |
| Private "longevity" blood panels | Standard panel + BP + waist + fitness test |

## 7. Practical protocol

### Home BP measurement (correct technique)
1. Sit 5 min, back supported, feet flat, arm at heart level.
2. No caffeine/exercise/smoking for 30 min.
3. Upper-arm cuff (validated device), correct cuff size.
4. Take 2–3 readings, 1 min apart; use the average.
5. Measure at the same time daily for a week when assessing — single readings mislead.

### Waist measurement
- Standing, relaxed, tape at the navel (or midpoint between lowest rib and iliac crest), at the end of a normal exhale. Don't suck in.

### The tracking dashboard (one page, updated weekly)
| Metric | Frequency | Target/rule |
|---|---|---|
| 7-day weight average | Weekly | Inside maintenance range ±1.5 kg |
| Waist | Monthly | Trend down during loss; stable in maintenance |
| Training sessions | Weekly | ≥2–3 |
| Step average | Weekly | ≥8,000 |
| Sleep average | Weekly | ≥7 h |
| Alcohol units | Weekly | ≤4 |
| BP | Monthly (or weekly if elevated) | <135/85 at home (clinician sets target) |
| Bloods | Annually | Per clinician |

### When to see a doctor promptly
- BP consistently ≥140/90 at home.
- New thirst/urination/weight loss (possible diabetes).
- Chest pain, breathlessness, fainting, palpitations.
- Unexplained weight loss, night sweats, persistent fatigue.
- Jaundice, dark urine, right-upper-quadrant pain.
- Any new or changing mole.
- Mood changes, anhedonia, or thoughts of self-harm.

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**Weekly: weight average + steps.** **Monthly: waist + BP.** **Yearly: bloods + dental + BP review.** That is a complete, cheap monitoring system.

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- This file is **not medical advice**; thresholds vary by country and by individual risk (e.g., diabetes, kidney disease, familial hypercholesterolaemia).
- **Do not start, stop or change prescription medication** based on this file.
- **Do not order tests you won't act on** — false positives lead to anxiety and unnecessary procedures.
- **Biotin** (and some other supplements) can distort lab results — disclose all supplements and stop biotin 48–72 h before bloods.
- **Creatine** raises serum creatinine; tell your clinician so kidney function is interpreted correctly (cystatin C can be used if needed).
- **Hard training** raises ALT/AST/CK; avoid heavy training 48 h before bloods if liver/muscle enzymes matter.
- **PSA**: discuss benefits and harms; do not test without a plan for what you'd do with the result.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Tracking everything and acting on nothing.**
2. **Judging progress from a single weigh-in.**
3. **Using wrist BP cuffs or poor technique** — unreliable readings.
4. **Bioimpedance body-fat as a decision metric** — too noisy.
5. **Biotin before bloods.**
6. **Not telling a doctor about creatine/finasteride/supplements.**
7. **Annual "full-body" scans instead of guideline screening.**
8. **Ignoring waist because the scale looks fine** ("skinny fat").
9. **Testing testosterone once, in the afternoon, while obese and sleep-deprived** — and being diagnosed on it.
10. **Letting the dashboard become a source of anxiety** — it is a feedback tool, not a report card.

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Buy a BP monitor** — the highest-value measurement device you can own.
2. **Measure waist monthly** and weight weekly (7-day average).
3. **Get a basic blood panel annually** (or less often if low risk).
4. **Track strength** — it is the best muscle-retention signal.
5. **Skip biological-age, microbiome, and full-body imaging** unless a clinician recommends them.
6. **Disclose every supplement** before testing, and stop biotin first.
