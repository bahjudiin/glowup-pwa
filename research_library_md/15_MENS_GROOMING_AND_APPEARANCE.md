# 15 — Men's Grooming & Appearance

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- Ranked by **actual effect on how you look**, the order is: **body composition → grooming/haircut → skin (file 14) → teeth → posture/clothing fit → everything else.** Men typically invert this and spend on products first.
- **Fat loss changes the face more than any cream or device**: it defines the jaw and cheekbones. But **very rapid or excessive weight loss can hollow the face** and make a man look older; slow loss with high protein and resistance training preserves facial volume and muscle (Low-Moderate evidence, mostly inference and clinical observation).
- **"Facial puffiness"** is mostly sleep, sodium, alcohol, allergy and (in some men) fat — not "toxins". Sleep and alcohol reduction are the effective levers.
- **Teeth and gums** are among the highest-return appearance investments: whitening, cleaning, and treating gum disease visibly improve appearance and are cheap relative to other cosmetic spending.
- **Hair loss is treatable** with real evidence — topical minoxidil, oral finasteride, and (increasingly) low-dose oral minoxidil — but this belongs in file 24 because it needs its own safety discussion.
- **Posture and clothing fit** reliably change perceived shape and attractiveness, cost little, and have no side effects — but the evidence base is mostly perceptual/behavioural, not clinical.
- **Where men waste money:** "jawline exercisers", mewing (as a claim about adult bone change), beard-growth oils with no active ingredient, "detox" facials, supplements marketed for attractiveness, and fragrance collections larger than three bottles.

## 2. What the evidence establishes

### 2.1 Face and body composition
- Facial appearance is strongly influenced by **body fat percentage and fat distribution**; facial fat is lost with overall fat loss and cannot be spot-reduced (High for the general principle).
- **Facial volume:** extreme leanness and rapid loss are associated in clinical observation with a gaunt appearance; the trade-off is real for men who get very lean (Low evidence; no RCTs).
- **Practical target:** 10–15% body fat for most men gives visible jaw/cheek definition without facial hollowing. Below ~8–10% sustained, most men look tired and feel worse.

### 2.2 Facial puffiness
- Common, modifiable causes: **short sleep, high sodium, alcohol, allergies/rhinitis, and (importantly) sleep apnoea** (Low-Moderate).
- **Morning puffiness** is largely positional/lymphatic and resolves within 1–2 hours.
- **Persistent under-eye bags** in men are usually fat herniation (structural) — no cream fixes this; surgery does. Be sceptical of eye creams claiming otherwise (Insufficient).
- **Cold compress and elevation** give temporary relief only.

### 2.3 Teeth and gums
- **Dental appearance** (colour, alignment, gingival health) is one of the most salient features in social perception research (Low-Moderate, perceptual studies).
- **Professional cleaning + whitening** are safe and effective when done by a dentist; OTC whitening strips work modestly (Moderate).
- **Gum disease** is associated with cardiovascular risk and worse glycaemic control (Low-Moderate, largely observational); treating it is good regardless.
- **Cheap wins:** brush 2×/day with fluoride, floss/interdental brushes daily, annual check, stop smoking, limit sugary drinks.

### 2.4 Grooming and hair (non-balding)
- **Haircut and beard shape** change perceived face shape more than any product; a good barber is the highest-ROI grooming spend (perceptual evidence only).
- **Scalp care:** treat dandruff/seborrhoeic dermatitis with an antifungal shampoo (ketoconazole or zinc pyrithione) — effective (Moderate).
- **Beard care:** wash, moisturise the skin beneath, trim; beard oils mostly improve feel/appearance of the hair, not growth (Low).
- **Shaving:** sharp blade, with the grain, warm water, fragrance-free moisturiser afterwards reduces irritation (Low-Moderate).

### 2.5 Posture, clothing and body language
- **Clothing fit** (shoulders, sleeve length, taper at the waist) reliably changes perceived physique; tailored/well-fitted cheap clothes beat expensive ill-fitting ones (Perceptual; Low-Moderate).
- **Posture** affects perceived confidence and, in some studies, how others judge you; it does **not** permanently reshape bone (Low).
- **Strength training** improves posture and shoulder-to-waist ratio — the only "structural" upgrade with real evidence.
- **Grooming consistency** (shaved/trimmed, clean nails, fresh clothes, deodorant) has a larger effect on first impressions than any single product.

### 2.6 Fragrance
- Fragrance improves perceived attractiveness modestly in experimental studies; effects are small and highly variable by individual and culture (Low).
- Practical: **one or two good fragrances**, applied to skin (2–4 sprays, not 10), replaced before they turn. No health benefits claimed; some people react to fragrance (contact dermatitis).

### 2.7 Hair removal / body grooming
- Trimming/shaving body hair is cosmetic and reversible; no health evidence either way (Insufficient).
- Risks: ingrown hairs, folliculitis, razor burn; use clean tools, exfoliate gently, moisturise.
- **Avoid** unregulated laser/IPL devices on darker skin tones without professional advice (burn/hyperpigmentation risk).

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

**The 90-day appearance plan:**
1. **Weeks 1–2:** haircut with a barber who will advise on face shape; beard line defined; teeth cleaning booked; buy well-fitted basics (2 pairs of trousers, 3 shirts, 1 jacket that fits the shoulders).
2. **Weeks 1–12:** run the fat-loss protocol (file 02) at 0.5–1%/week — the face changes first and most.
3. **Daily:** SPF + moisturiser; retinoid at night; 7–9 h sleep; limit alcohol/sodium if puffiness is an issue.
4. **Twice weekly:** resistance training (shoulders/back for shape).
5. **Monthly:** haircut; check photos in consistent light to see real change.

**Limitations:** genetics set the ceiling; the visible change from grooming is real but small compared to body composition. Cosmetic procedures (orthodontics, rhinoplasty, hair transplant, blepharoplasty) are legitimate but expensive and should be a considered medical decision, not a purchase.

## 7. Practical protocol

### The weekly appearance routine (≈30 min/week + 5 min/day)
- **Daily (5 min):** shower, deodorant, SPF moisturiser, brush teeth ×2, clean clothes.
- **Weekly:** trim nails, beard/shave tidy, wash bedsheets (skin/acne), one photo in consistent light.
- **Every 3–5 weeks:** haircut; beard line-up.
- **Every 6–12 months:** dental check + clean.
- **Daily, non-negotiable:** 7–9 h sleep, alcohol ≤4/week, sunscreen.

### Clothing fit rules (cheap, high-impact)
1. Shoulders must fit — everything else can be tailored.
2. Sleeve should end at the wrist bone; trousers break once, no pooling.
3. Taper at the waist; avoid boxy fits if you're lean, avoid tight fits if you're heavy.
4. Dark, plain colours; one accent. 3–4 outfits, repeated.
5. Shoes clean; a single versatile pair beats five worn ones.

### Posture
- Strength train the upper back and glutes (rows, face pulls, hinges).
- Set up screens at eye level; stand every 45–60 min.
- Skip posture "correctors" — they don't create lasting change; training does.

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**Barber every 4 weeks, SPF every morning, teeth cleaned yearly, clothes that fit, 12% body fat.** That is the entire appearance programme, and it outperforms every product purchase.

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Weight loss:** do not go below ~8% body fat for appearance; harms (hormonal, mood, sleep, immune) outweigh the cosmetic gain.
- **Teeth whitening:** avoid with untreated cavities, gum disease, or sensitive teeth; dentist first. Avoid unregulated "whitening" kits and abrasive charcoal toothpastes (enamel wear).
- **Hair loss medication** (finasteride, minoxidil): see file 24 — prescription discussion required; sexual/mood side effects are real for some men.
- **Cosmetic procedures:** choose a qualified clinician; check registration; avoid unregulated injectables, "skin lightening" injections, and beautician-performed laser on darker skin.
- **Fragrance:** patch test if you have eczema; fragrance is a common contact allergen.
- **Shaving:** do not shave over active acne or moles; change blades regularly.
- **Moles:** any changing lesion → doctor, not a cosmetic clinic.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Buying appearance products before losing fat.**
2. **Chasing extreme leanness** and looking drawn/tired.
3. **Ignoring teeth** — the cheapest large improvement available.
4. **Ill-fitting clothes** with good quality.
5. **Overspending on skincare** while smoking and sleeping 5 h.
6. **"Mewing"/jaw exercisers** expecting adult bone change (no evidence).
7. **Hair-growth supplements** instead of proven treatments (file 24).
8. **Ten sprays of fragrance.**
9. **Judging progress in bad bathroom lighting** — use consistent photos.
10. **Comparing yourself to edited photos** — the reference standard is fake.

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Get to 10–15% body fat slowly** — the largest lever, and it is free.
2. **Barber every 3–5 weeks; beard shaped.**
3. **SPF daily; retinoid nightly** (file 14).
4. **Teeth: clean yearly, whiten once, floss daily.**
5. **Three well-fitted outfits beat a wardrobe of ill-fitting ones.**
6. **Lift twice a week for shoulders and posture.**
7. **Buy nothing else** until these are automatic.
