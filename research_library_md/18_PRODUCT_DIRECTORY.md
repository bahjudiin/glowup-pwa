- **Best budget option:** psyllium powder + vitamin D + a food scale (≈$30–40 total for months of use).

## 2. What the evidence establishes (how these products were selected)

A product enters this directory only if:
1. There is **human evidence** (moderate or better) for the outcome it is sold for in this library's domains, **or** it is a mechanical tool that enables an evidence-based behaviour (scale, shoes, bands).
2. The **cost per use is low** relative to the benefit.
3. The **risk is low** at the recommended dose.
4. A **cheaper equivalent** does not exist (if it does, the cheaper one is listed first).

## 7. Practical protocol (how to buy well)

1. **Read the supplement facts panel**, not the front label. Confirm the dose matches the effective dose.
2. **Prefer single-ingredient products.** Blends hide underdosing.
3. **Prefer third-party tested** (Informed Sport/NSF Certified for Sport, USP) when available, especially if you compete.
4. **Calculate cost per serving** before buying.
5. **Buy the smallest size first** to test tolerance.
6. **Check the seller** — marketplace third-party sellers can sell counterfeit supplements.
7. **Store properly** (cool, dry; fish oil in the fridge).
8. **Check expiry dates**; discard fish oil that smells rancid.

## 9. Safety and contraindications (general purchasing safety)

- **Supplements are regulated as food**, not medicines, in most countries: no pre-market proof of efficacy, and variable quality control.
- **Biotin** in "hair/skin/nail" products interferes with lab tests (thyroid, troponin) — stop 48–72 h before bloods.
- **Kidney disease:** avoid high-dose creatine, magnesium and potassium products without medical advice.
- **Anticoagulants:** high-dose fish oil, vitamin E, garlic, ginkgo.
- **Thyroid medication:** separate fibre/calcium/iron supplements by 4 h.
- **Skin products:** patch-test new actives; avoid unregulated "peels", "whitening" injections, and at-home deep microneedling.
- **Never buy** prescription-only drugs (GLP-1s, tretinoin where prescription-only, finasteride, oral minoxidil, testosterone) from unauthorised sellers.

### 10.5 Blood-pressure monitor *(not price-verified)*

- **What to buy:** a validated **upper-arm** (not wrist) automatic monitor. Check the model against a validated-device list (e.g., validatebp.org) or your national hypertension society.
- **Why:** hypertension is silent, common in men 40+, and the single most modifiable cardiovascular risk factor after smoking.
- **Price:** typically **$25–50** — *not verified in this build.*
- **Search link (Amazon):** https://www.amazon.com/s?k=validated+upper+arm+blood+pressure+monitor
- **Verdict:** **high value**; one of the best health purchases a man can make.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. Buying supplements before buying a food scale and shoes.
2. Judging by bottle price instead of cost per serving.
3. Trusting a marketplace seller for a premium supplement (counterfeits exist).
4. Buying "advanced" forms of proven ingredients (creatine HCl, "buffered" creatine) at 3–5× the price for no added benefit.
5. Buying fish oil without checking EPA+DHA content per capsule.
6. Buying whey isolate when concentrate or food is cheaper and adequate.
7. Buying retinoid products without sunscreen.
8. Paying for subscriptions (scales, apps, trackers) with no outcome benefit.
9. Stacking 8 supplements with unknown interactions.
10. Buying anything from the §10.17 "skip" list.
