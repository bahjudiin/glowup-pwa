**Evidence convention.** This library grades every important claim and links it to PubMed. Where a claim could not be verified against the existing reference index (`22_RESEARCH_REFERENCES.md`, 141 PMIDs) it is marked **Unverified in this library** and is graded **Low** or **Very low** at best. Mechanistic findings are never presented as proof of real-world outcomes.

## 1. Executive summary

Erectile function, ejaculation control, sperm quality, attraction and testosterone are five different problems that the popular "Big D" content tends to blur into one. This file separates them and grades each.

| Section | What actually drives it | Best evidence in this library | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sexual function | Stress reduction, sleep, cardiovascular health, alcohol/smoking | Cardio-metabolic risk markers (see file 12) | Moderate |
| Premature ejaculation | Behavioural therapy; topical anaesthetics for the drug-resistant case | Not covered by this library's reference index | Low |
| Fertility | Avoid endocrine disruptors, heat, oxidative stress; semen analysis before and after | Not covered by this library's reference index | Low |
| Attraction | Body fat, muscle, sleep, grooming, hygiene | Body-fat → testosterone ([37345263](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37345263/)) | Low–Moderate |
| Low testosterone | Sleep, resistance training, body fat, micronutrients | Weight loss → testosterone ([37345263](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37345263/)); indications for testosterone therapy ([39311216](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39311216/)) | Moderate |

**The honest headline:** the highest-value interventions in this whole section — sleep, resistance training, losing visceral fat, cutting alcohol, not smoking, and screening for cardiovascular disease and sleep apnoea — are also the cheapest and the best supported. The herbal supplements are unverified here and should be treated as optional, not foundational.

## 2. Sexual function

### 2.1 Stress is the mechanism

Stress is the most commonly reported driver of erectile dysfunction and premature ejaculation. Chronic stress elevates cortisol, which competes with testosterone for receptor occupancy and suppresses gonadotropic signalling.

**Hormones that support sexual function:** testosterone, DHT, thyroid hormone, progesterone, DHEA, dopamine, acetylcholine, nitric oxide.

**Hormones that work against it:** cortisol, prolactin, excess estrogen, excess serotonin, oxidative stress.

*Grade: Moderate for the stress → function relationship; the specific hormone lists are mechanistic, not outcome-proven.*

### 2.2 Erectile function — the facts

- Erectile dysfunction is common in younger men than people assume. It is also a recognised **early marker of cardiovascular disease**, because the same endothelial and metabolic processes drive both. This library grades blood pressure and screening under file `12`.
- **Avoid:** alcohol, cigarettes, diets high in polyunsaturated fatty acids, prolonged sitting, tight underwear over the testes.
- **Sleep is the first line of defence.** Melatonin is an antioxidant and short sleep independently suppresses testosterone and impairs vascular function.
- Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) from sulfur-rich foods such as garlic and onion is a second vasodilator pathway alongside nitric oxide. This is a mechanistic observation, not an outcome trial.

*Grade: Moderate for the cardiovascular-risk-marker relationship; Low for the food-based vasodilation claims.*

### 2.3 Herbal support — graded

| Intervention | Claim in the ebook | Grade in this library | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ashwagandha | Reduces stress ~20%, supports blood flow and testosterone | Low | Stress-reduction effect has small-trial support. **Safety note:** ashwagandha-associated liver injury is now a recognised adverse event ([42367407](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42367407/)) — it is not benign. |
| Tribulus terrestris | Lowers prolactin and estrogen, raises dopamine; ≥120 mg protodioscin | Very low | Mostly small, short studies. |
| Mucuna pruriens | L-DOPA precursor; raises dopamine and testosterone, lowers serotonin | Very low | Dopaminergic side effects (nausea, agitation, blood-pressure changes) are real. |
| Butea superba | Raises DHT | Very low | No good outcome data. |
| Tongkat ali | Aromatase inhibitor and PDE-5 inhibitor | Very low | Not verified in this library. |
| Epimedium (horny goat weed) | Natural PDE-5 inhibitor | Very low | Not verified in this library. |
| Maca | Oxygenation and endurance; 2–4 g/day over 12 weeks | Very low | Subjective endpoints in small trials. |
| Ginkgo | Antioxidant, circulation, counters excess serotonin | Very low | Not verified in this library. |

### 2.4 Vasodilatory foods

- Cacao, beets, pomegranate and watermelon are rich in inorganic nitrate or polyphenols; the nitric-oxide pathway is real, the clinical effect size is not established here.
- Garlic plus vitamin C increasing nitric oxide production is a biochemical observation.
- Ginger acting on the 5-HT3 receptor to reduce gut serotonin is mechanistic.

*Grade: Low.*

### 2.5 What to do first

1. Sleep 7–9 hours, consistently.
2. Train 3–5 times a week with one zone-2 session.
3. Protein, vegetables, minimal alcohol.
4. Check blood pressure, fasting glucose, lipids.
5. Screen for sleep apnoea if you snore or wake unrefreshed (file `11`).
6. Only then evaluate supplements.

## 3. Premature ejaculation

### 3.1 Mechanism

Premature ejaculation is described as low dopamine with elevated serotonin or adrenaline — a narrow window between arousal and climax. This is the conventional model.

- Acetylcholine (egg yolks, liver, royal jelly) supports dopamine synthesis.
- Taurine supports dopamine and GABA together.
- Magnesium regulates serotonin and calms the nervous system.
- Caffeine, alcohol and sleep debt raise sympathetic tone.

*Grade: Low — mechanistic, not outcome-proven. The specific food list is not verifiable in this library.*

### 3.2 Protocol

1. **Sleep first.** Short sleep raises serotonin and lowers dopamine and reproduces the pattern on its own.
2. **Train the exhale.** Slow nasal breathing with a long exhale lowers sympathetic activation.
3. **Start-stop and squeeze technique** — 3 sets of 10, three times a week for four weeks.
4. **Reduce alcohol.**
5. **Topical anaesthetics** (lidocaine/prilocaine) — the highest-evidence pharmacological option for premature ejaculation. **Not covered by this library's reference index**; graded Moderate on external grounds and flagged as needing a clinician.

> Medical note: premature ejaculation that is new or sudden, or that comes with pain, blood in the semen or testicular changes, needs a urologist, not a supplement stack.

## 4. Fertility and sperm health

### 4.1 What controls sperm production

- **LH** signals the testes to produce testosterone.
- **FSH** signals Sertoli cells to drive spermatogenesis.
- **Xenoestrogens** — plastics, phthalates and other endocrine disruptors — are associated with reduced sperm counts. Avoidance is free and the highest-leverage action in this section.

### 4.2 Key enhancers — graded

| Intervention | Claim in the ebook | Grade in this library |
|---|---|---|
| Ginger | ~+16% sperm count; improved LH/FSH/testosterone | Low — single small study, not independently verified here |
| Vitamin E | Protects sperm membranes from oxidative damage | Moderate — well-established biochemistry, low-dose trial support |
| Vitamin K2 | Supports testosterone and Leydig cell function | Low |
| Shilajit | ~+37.6% sperm quality, ~+61.4% sperm count, ~+23.5% testosterone | Very low — single small study; **not independently verified in this library** |
| Cistanche | Restores kisspeptin signalling | Very low — not verified in this library |
| Royal jelly | ~+20% testosterone over three months | Very low — not verified in this library |
| Maca | Sperm count and semen quality | Very low — not verified in this library |
| Raw onion and garlic | Sperm number and motility | Very low — not verified in this library |

**Read that table honestly.** Every one of those percentage figures comes from a single small trial and none of them were independently reproduced in this library's reference index. They are not reliable expectations.

### 4.3 Baseline for both partners

1. Semen analysis and hormone panel for both partners, before anything else.
2. Check for varicocele — common, correctable, frequently missed.
3. Two to three months of lifestyle work, then re-test. The spermatogenic cycle is about 74 days, so changes show up in a quarter, not tomorrow.
4. Keep the testes cool: no prolonged hot baths, no laptops on the lap, no tight synthetic underwear.

> Sperm quality falls sharply after 35 and more steeply after 40. If you want children, this is not a task to defer.

## 5. Attraction and androgen signs

### 5.1 Observable correlates

- **Hair** — healthy hair is driven by testosterone and DHT. Androgenetic alopecia is the most common treatable cause of hair loss in men; the treatments with the best evidence in this library are topical minoxidil ([12196747](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12196747/)), finasteride ([9777765](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9777765/)) and topical ketoconazole ([31858672](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31858672/)).
- **Face** — a broad jawline and high cheekbones read as higher testosterone.
- **Voice** — lower pitch reflects greater DHT exposure during puberty.
- **Body** — broad shoulders, lean waist, visible vascularity.
- **Grip** — forearm strength correlates with testosterone.

### 5.2 Pheromones

Androsterones in sweat are associated with a musky odour and increased perceived attractiveness. Exercise-induced sweating raises them; an animal-based diet raises musky body odour; over-washing with harsh chemicals damages the skin microbiome that produces the signal.

*Grade: Low. Human pheromone research is genuinely contested; treat "pheromones" as a hygiene-and-diet effect rather than a chemistry engine.*

### 5.3 Practical levers

1. **Lose body fat.** Visceral fat is the strongest single dietary driver of low testosterone; weight loss reliably raises testosterone in obese men ([37345263](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37345263/)). At 12–15% body fat and below, testosterone typically normalises for most men.
2. **Train heavy and compound.** Squat, hinge, press, pull.
3. **Fix sleep.** Seven to nine hours; a single night of five-hour sleep measurably suppresses testosterone.
4. **Cut alcohol, cigarettes and chronic stress.**
5. **Presentation compounds the biology** — grooming, posture, a fitted haircut, clean skin.

> These signals are real but modest. Behaviour, status and hygiene move perceived attraction further than any single hormone.

## 6. Fixing low testosterone

### 6.1 Boost LH and dopamine

Ginger, taurine, cistanche, mucuna and manganese are presented as supporting the GnRH → LH → FSH → testosterone cascade. Adequate sleep is the largest single input to that cascade.

### 6.2 Correct the deficiencies first

Testosterone is nutrient-dependent. Vitamin A, K2, E, D and B complex; zinc, selenium and iodine; boron and magnesium. Shilajit is included for mitochondrial and hormonal support.

### 6.3 Manage serotonin

Magnesium regulates serotonin; ginkgo and ginger are listed as counteracting excess serotonin; L-theanine reduces anxiety.

### 6.4 Avoid the disruptors

- **Xenoestrogens** — plastics, phthalates, endocrine disruptors.
- **EMFs** — claimed to increase calcium influx and impair gonadal function. Mitigation: magnesium, grounding, blue-light glasses. *Grade: Very low. There is no established human evidence that ordinary household EMFs impair gonadal function. The mitigation advice is harmless; the causal claim is not supported.*
- Alcohol, cigarettes and chronic sleep debt.

### 6.5 The order that works

1. Sleep 7–9 hours, consistently.
2. Body fat down to 12–15%.
3. Heavy compound training, 3–4 times a week.
4. Diet: enough protein, enough fat, vegetables, minimal alcohol.
5. **Test before you treat** — total and free testosterone, SHBG, LH, FSH, thyroid, morning draw.

> Medical note. Testosterone therapy is a prescription intervention with a defined indication set ([39311216](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39311216/)) and a cardiovascular-safety evidence base to respect (TRAVERSE; see `22_RESEARCH_REFERENCES.md`). Do not self-prescribe: exogenous testosterone suppresses your own axis, shrinks testes and can permanently affect fertility.

## 7. Claims not verified in this library

Stated plainly so the app never overclaims:

1. Every percentage figure for shilajit, royal jelly, maca, ginger and tongkat ali.
2. Tongkat ali as an aromatase inhibitor and PDE-5 inhibitor.
3. Epimedium as a PDE-5 inhibitor.
4. Tribulus lowering prolactin and estrogen.
5. H₂S from garlic and onion as an erectile-function intervention.
6. Topical anaesthetics for premature ejaculation — graded Moderate on external grounds but absent from this library's reference index.
7. EMF exposure impairing gonadal function.
8. Body-odour "pheromones" as a reliable attraction mechanism.

## 9. Disclaimer

Research synthesis for information only — **not medical advice**. Erectile dysfunction, premature ejaculation and fertility problems are frequently the first presentation of diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, endocrine disease or sleep apnoea, and all of those are treatable. See a clinician for persistent sexual-function problems, for anything that is new or sudden, for fertility planning, and before starting any hormone-affecting supplement. Never self-prescribe testosterone. Stop and seek care for chest pain, fainting, confusion, palpitations, testicular pain or swelling, or blood in the semen.
