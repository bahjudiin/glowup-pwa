# 11 — Sleep & Recovery

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Sleep is the cheapest performance-enhancing, appetite-suppressing, muscle-preserving intervention available**, and it is the most commonly ignored variable in fat-loss plans.
- Extending sleep in short-sleeping overweight adults reduced objectively measured energy intake by **~270 kcal/day** with no change in activity (Tasali 2022 — Moderate-High). That is a larger effect than most supplements and many diets produce.
- **Sleep restriction changes *what* you lose**: in a controlled deficit, 5.5 h vs 8.5 h of sleep reduced fat loss by ~55% and increased lean-mass loss by ~60% (Nedeltcheva 2010 — Moderate).
- Short sleep raises ghrelin and lowers leptin → more hunger (Spiegel 2004 — Moderate). Both short and long sleep are associated with higher all-cause mortality in a U-shaped relationship (Cappuccio 2010 — Moderate-High, observational).
- **Target: 7–9 hours with a fixed wake time.** Consistency of timing matters almost as much as duration.
- Snoring, waking unrefreshed, morning headaches, or witnessed apnoeas in an overweight man with a large neck → **screen for obstructive sleep apnoea**; untreated OSA undermines every other intervention in this library.
- Recovery is not a supplement or a gadget: it is **sleep, protein, calories, and managed training volume**.

## 2. What the evidence establishes

### 2.1 Sleep and body weight
- Sleep extension (to ~8.5 h) in adults habitually sleeping <6.5 h reduced energy intake by **−270 kcal/day**, with weight loss over 2 weeks (Tasali 2022, JAMA Intern Med — Moderate-High).
- Sleep restriction during a calorie deficit shifted substrate use toward lean mass: fat loss fell by ~55% and fat-free mass loss rose by ~60% (Nedeltcheva 2010, Ann Intern Med — Moderate; small n).
- Short sleep increases hunger, ghrelin and appetite, especially for energy-dense foods (Spiegel 2004 — Moderate).

### 2.2 Sleep and mortality/health
- Meta-analysis of prospective studies: both short (<7 h) and long (>8–9 h) sleep duration are associated with higher all-cause mortality (Cappuccio 2010, Sleep — Moderate-High, observational).
- Short sleep is associated with impaired glucose tolerance, higher blood pressure, and greater cardiovascular risk (Moderate, largely observational).

### 2.3 Sleep and training
- Sleep deprivation impairs strength performance, reduces time to exhaustion, and increases perceived effort (Moderate).
- Sleep extension in athletes improves reaction time, sprint performance and mood (Low-Moderate).

### 2.4 Insomnia: what works
- **CBT-I (cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia) is first-line** and outperforms medication long-term (High, guideline-level). Free/low-cost digital CBT-I programmes exist and are effective.
- **Melatonin** has a small effect on sleep-onset latency (a few minutes), best used for circadian timing problems/jet lag; doses 0.5–3 mg are as effective as higher doses (Moderate).
- **Magnesium, glycine, valerian, tart cherry, "sleep blends"** have weak/inconsistent evidence (Low).

### 2.5 Napping and caffeine
- A short nap (10–30 min, early afternoon) can restore alertness; long/late naps impair night sleep.
- Caffeine has a ~5–6 h half-life; a 4pm coffee still has ~quarter of its effect at midnight. Cut caffeine 8–10 h before bed.

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

**The 7-day sleep reset:**
1. **Fixed wake time** every day (including weekends) — the single most powerful lever.
2. **Caffeine cutoff** 8–10 h before bed; no caffeine after midday if you're sensitive.
3. **Light:** bright outdoor light within 30 min of waking; dim light and screens off 60 min before bed.
4. **Temperature:** cool room; shower before bed (the post-shower temperature drop aids sleep onset).
5. **Alcohol:** none within 3 h of bed (it reduces deep sleep and fragments the second half of the night).
6. **Food:** finish eating 2–3 h before bed (also helps reflux and weight control).
7. **Bed = sleep only** (no scrolling, no work); if awake >20 min, get up and do something boring in dim light.

**Limitations:** this will not fix sleep apnoea, restless legs, chronic pain or primary insomnia — those need medical care.

## 7. Practical protocol

### Evening routine (60 minutes before bed)
- Set a "screens off" alarm 60 min before bed.
- Prepare tomorrow's food and clothes (removes morning decisions).
- Light stretching or 10-min walk.
- Cool, dark, quiet room; phone charging outside the bedroom.

### Morning routine (first 60 minutes)
- Same wake time, alarm across the room.
- Bright light: step outside or use a bright lamp.
- Water, protein breakfast, 10-min walk (also improves glucose control).

### Measuring
- Log **bed time, wake time, and how you feel (1–5)** — no wearable required.
- Weekly average sleep duration target ≥7 h.
- If you snore loudly, gasp, or wake with a dry mouth/headache → **ask for a sleep study**.

### Recovery rules for training
- Persistent fatigue, rising resting heart rate, declining lifts, poor sleep → **deload 1 week at 50–60% volume**.
- Do not add training volume while sleeping <6 h.
- Recovery tools ranked: sleep > food/protein > stress management > walking > everything else (massage, cold plunges, compression) — the last group is optional and unproven for recovery.

---

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**Two rules:** (1) same wake time every day; (2) no caffeine after midday and no alcohol within 3 h of bed. Add a dark, cool room. That captures most of the benefit with zero equipment.

---

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Sleep apnoea**: loud snoring + daytime sleepiness + large neck circumference in an overweight man is a red flag. Untreated OSA raises cardiovascular risk and sabotages fat loss and energy. Get assessed; treatment (CPAP) is highly effective.
- **Melatonin**: generally safe short-term; may interact with anticoagulants, immunosuppressants, and antihypertensives. Avoid in pregnancy. Do not drive after high doses.
- **Sedative hypnotics** (benzodiazepines, Z-drugs) carry dependence, fall and cognitive risks — not a first-line solution; discuss with a clinician.
- **Alcohol as a sleep aid** is counterproductive: it reduces sleep quality and increases apnoeas.
- **Restless legs/periodic limb movements, chronic insomnia, depression**: medical assessment; iron status is worth checking.
- **Shift work**: use light control and consistent anchors; seek advice rather than fighting it with supplements.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Treating sleep as the variable to sacrifice when busy** — it costs you fat loss, muscle and health.
2. **Varying wake time by 2+ hours on weekends** ("social jet lag").
3. **Late caffeine** disguised as pre-workout.
4. **Alcohol as a sleep aid.**
5. **Eating large meals right before bed** — worse reflux, worse sleep, higher intake.
6. **Buying sleep gadgets before fixing light, caffeine and timing.**
7. **Ignoring snoring/apnoea** for years.
8. **Training harder to compensate for poor sleep** — the opposite of what's needed.
9. **Screens in bed** — delays sleep onset even with "night mode".
10. **Expecting supplements to fix a broken schedule.**

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **7–9 h sleep, fixed wake time, seven days a week.**
2. **Caffeine cutoff 8–10 h before bed; alcohol ≥3 h before bed and limited.**
3. **Dark, cool, quiet room; phone outside.**
4. **Finish eating 2–3 h before bed.**
5. **Screen for sleep apnoea** if you snore or wake unrefreshed — it changes everything.
6. **Use CBT-I (free digital versions exist) instead of sleeping pills.**
7. Spend $0–30 total. Sleep is the cheapest intervention in this library.
