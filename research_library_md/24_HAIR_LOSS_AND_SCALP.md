# 24 — Hair Loss & Scalp Care

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**. Hair loss is the most treatable common cosmetic concern in men — and the area where "natural" products waste the most money.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Male androgenetic alopecia (AGA) is driven by genetics + androgens** (DHT sensitivity of scalp follicles). It is progressive but **treatable** if you act early; lost follicles can be replaced only by surgery.
- **Two treatments have strong evidence:** **topical minoxidil** (5% foam/solution) and **oral finasteride 1 mg** (prescription). Both are modest in effect, need to be continued indefinitely, and work best in early stages (High/Moderate-High).
- **Other options with weaker evidence:** topical dutasteride/finasteride (prescription, emerging), low-dose oral minoxidil (prescription, off-label, effective but requires monitoring), ketoconazole shampoo (modest adjunct — Weak/Moderate), microneedling (adjunct, Moderate-Low), low-level laser therapy (Weak), PRP injections (Weak-Moderate, expensive).
- **What doesn't work:** biotin (unless deficient), saw palmetto (weak at best), caffeine/rosemary shampoos (insufficient), "hair growth" supplements, onion juice, biotin shampoos, and every "DHT-blocking" cosmetic.
- **Safety is a real discussion, not a footnote:** finasteride causes sexual and mood side effects in a minority, and a persistent syndrome is reported (aetiology debated); finasteride tablets are **teratogenic** (crushed/broken tablets must not be handled by pregnant women); minoxidil is **highly toxic to cats**; oral minoxidil can cause hypotension, oedema and tachycardia.
- **Non-balding scalp care is simple:** treat dandruff/seborrhoeic dermatitis with an antifungal shampoo, wash as often as needed, don't over-treat.
- **Act early, choose one proven treatment, and commit to 12 months** before judging — this is the entire evidence-based strategy.

---

## 2. What the evidence establishes

### 2.1 Topical minoxidil
- 5% topical minoxidil is superior to 2% and to placebo for male AGA in a 48-week RCT (Olsen 2002, JAAD — Moderate-High).
- Mechanism: prolongs anagen (growth phase) and increases follicle size; it does **not** stop the androgen pathway.
- Typical results: modest regrowth + slowed progression; **shedding in the first 4–8 weeks is expected** and is not a sign of failure.
- Requires indefinite use; stopping causes loss of gains within months.

### 2.2 Finasteride
- Oral finasteride 1 mg/day slowed hair loss and increased hair count over 2 years in a large RCT (Kaufman 1998, JAAD — High for efficacy).
- Mechanism: inhibits type II 5α-reductase, lowering scalp DHT.
- **Sexual side effects** (reduced libido, erectile dysfunction, ejaculatory changes) are reported in ~2–4% in trials, typically reversible on stopping; a **persistent** post-finasteride syndrome is reported in a minority and remains an area of active debate (Giatti 2024 — Low-Moderate; the existence of persistent symptoms is documented in case series, the mechanism is not settled).
- Depression/mood changes are reported; discuss if you have a history of mood disorder.
- Requires a prescription in most countries; PSA interpretation is affected (finasteride roughly halves PSA — tell any doctor testing you).

### 2.3 Other options
| Treatment | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ketoconazole 2% shampoo** | **Weak-Moderate** (systematic review — Fields 2020) | Cheap adjunct, 2×/week; also treats seborrhoeic dermatitis |
| **Microneedling (0.5–1.5 mm)** | **Moderate-Low** as an adjunct to minoxidil | In-clinic or careful home use; infection risk; not for use on inflamed scalp |
| **Low-level laser therapy (combs/caps)** | **Low** | Expensive; small trials; modest at best |
| **PRP injections** | **Low-Moderate** | Expensive, repeated, variable protocols |
| **Topical finasteride/dutasteride** | **Low-Moderate** (emerging) | Prescription; may reduce systemic exposure |
| **Low-dose oral minoxidil (0.25–5 mg)** | **Moderate** (off-label) | Effective; requires prescription + BP monitoring; risks: hypotension, oedema, tachycardia, pericardial effusion (rare) |
| **Dutasteride 0.5 mg** | **Moderate-High** (off-label for AGA) | More potent DHT suppression; longer half-life, similar side-effect discussion |
| **Hair transplant (FUE/FUT)** | **High** for appropriate candidates | Expensive; best when medically stabilised first |
| **Biotin, saw palmetto, rosemary oil, caffeine shampoo, "hair vitamins"** | **Insufficient to Low** | Not recommended as primary treatment |

### 2.4 What actually predicts success
- **Early intervention** (before follicles miniaturise completely).
- **Consistent daily use** for ≥6–12 months.
- **Realistic expectations:** stabilisation is the main win; regrowth is usually partial.
- **Combination** (minoxidil + finasteride) outperforms either alone.

### 2.5 Non-AGA hair loss (worth knowing)
- **Telogen effluvium** (diffuse shedding after illness, crash dieting, surgery, stress): usually self-limiting over 3–6 months; **crash diets and very low protein/iron are common causes** — directly relevant to file 20.
- **Alopecia areata** (patchy, smooth): autoimmune; see a dermatologist.
- **Scarring alopecias**: urgent dermatology.
- **Nutritional causes:** iron deficiency (check ferritin), severe protein/calorie restriction, zinc deficiency.

| Supplements | No reliable effect |

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

1. **Confirm the diagnosis** (pattern, family history, photos). If the pattern is atypical, patchy, itchy/scarring, or rapid — see a dermatologist before treating.
2. **Start topical minoxidil 5% once or twice daily** (evidence-based, OTC in many countries).
3. **Discuss oral finasteride 1 mg** with a clinician; start if appropriate after discussing sexual/mood side effects.
4. **Add ketoconazole 2% shampoo 2×/week** if you have dandruff/itch (cheap, low risk).
5. **Take photos monthly** in the same lighting; judge at 6 and 12 months.
6. **Do not stop at 3 months** because of "no change" or initial shedding.
7. **If you want a transplant**, stabilise medically for 12 months first.

**Limitations:** both proven treatments require indefinite use; results are modest; finasteride's side-effect profile is a genuine trade-off; minoxidil is messy and toxic to pets.

## 7. Practical protocol

### Daily
- Minoxidil 5% foam: once daily (twice if tolerated) on a dry scalp; wash hands after; allow 4 h before washing hair.
- Finasteride 1 mg once daily (if prescribed).
- Normal shampooing is fine — minoxidil works better on a clean scalp.

### Weekly
- Ketoconazole 2% shampoo 2×/week (leave 3–5 min).
- Photos in consistent light (monthly).

### Quarterly
- Assess photos and side effects; continue if stable.
- If side effects on finasteride: discuss dose reduction/alternatives with your clinician — **do not** stop abruptly and expect to keep gains from combination therapy.

### Scalp care (everyone, balding or not)
- Wash hair as often as needed with a gentle shampoo; dandruff is common and treatable.
- Avoid tight hairstyles/traction; avoid harsh chemical treatments on an irritated scalp.
- Sun protection on a balding scalp (skin cancer risk is real — file 14).

---

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**Minoxidil 5% once daily + finasteride 1 mg daily (if prescribed).** Two minutes a day. That is the entire evidence-based hair protocol.

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Finasteride:** prescription only. Reported sexual dysfunction, reduced libido, mood changes; persistent symptoms reported in a minority. **Teratogenic** — pregnant or potentially pregnant women must not handle crushed or broken tablets. Affects PSA interpretation. Discuss with a clinician if you have depression or are trying to conceive.
- **Minoxidil (topical):** contact dermatitis, scalp irritation, unwanted facial hair if it drips; **extremely toxic to cats and dogs** — store safely and don't let pets lick your pillow/hands.
- **Oral minoxidil:** prescription only; risks include hypotension, fluid retention, tachycardia, and (rarely) pericardial effusion; contraindicated in certain cardiac conditions; requires monitoring.
- **Dutasteride:** similar considerations to finasteride; long half-life means side effects may take longer to resolve.
- **LLLT/PRP/transplant:** choose a qualified, registered clinician; avoid unregulated clinics and "hair mesotherapy" injections with unapproved substances.
- **Red flags needing medical review:** rapid diffuse shedding, patchy hair loss, scalp pain/itch/scaling with scarring, eyebrow/body hair loss, associated fatigue/weight change (thyroid), or iron deficiency symptoms.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Starting too late** (waiting until the crown is bald).
2. **Stopping at 3 months** — before the 6–12 month window.
3. **Using supplements instead of proven treatments.**
4. **Buying an LED cap before trying minoxidil.**
5. **Not telling a doctor you take finasteride** before a PSA test.
6. **Crash dieting** and triggering telogen effluvium.
7. **Ignoring scalp inflammation/dandruff.**
8. **Combining 5 unproven topicals** and getting contact dermatitis.
9. **Assuming a transplant removes the need for medication** — untransplanted hair keeps thinning.
10. **Not photographing progress** — memory is a poor judge.

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Confirm it's AGA**; rule out thyroid/iron/telogen effluvium if the pattern doesn't fit.
2. **Minoxidil 5% + finasteride (if appropriate)** — the evidence-based pair.
3. **Commit to 12 months** and photograph monthly.
4. **Add ketoconazole shampoo** if you have dandruff/itch.
5. **Skip supplements, LED caps and PRP** as first-line options.
6. **Avoid crash diets** — they cause reversible hair loss in men who don't have AGA.
7. **Protect a balding scalp with sunscreen.**
