# 03 — Body Recomposition & Muscle Retention

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Body recomposition** (losing fat while gaining or preserving muscle) is real, but its magnitude depends on who you are. It is most achievable in men who are **untrained, higher body fat, detrained, or returning after a layoff**, and least achievable in lean, advanced trainees (Moderate).
- The recipe is not exotic: **resistance training + high protein + a modest deficit + adequate sleep**. Those four variables account for most of the variance in whether you keep your muscle.
- In a deficit, **protein needs are higher than at maintenance**: aim 2.0–2.6 g/kg/day (or ~2.3–3.1 g/kg fat-free mass in lean, trained men).
- **Resistance training is the muscle-retention intervention.** Cardio is an energy-expenditure tool; it does not substitute for lifting.
- Muscle loss during dieting is smaller than folklore suggests when protein and training are adequate — in well-controlled trials it can be near zero, and in higher-body-fat beginners net lean mass can *increase*.
- The main threats to muscle in a diet are: **too large a deficit, too little protein, no resistance training, poor sleep, and excessive cardio volume**.

## 2. What the evidence establishes

### 2.1 Recomposition is possible, and conditional
- A narrative review of recomposition evidence concludes simultaneous fat loss and muscle gain is achievable, particularly in untrained and higher-body-fat individuals, and that the effect shrinks as training status and leanness increase (Barakat et al., *Strength Cond J* 2020 — Low-Moderate; narrative review, cited via search link below).
- The best trial evidence comes from controlled overfeeding/deficit studies: young men in a **40% deficit with 2.4 g/kg protein plus intense training gained lean mass while losing fat** (Longland 2016, AJCN — Moderate).
- Physique-sport reviews converge on **slow-to-moderate deficits + very high protein + heavy training** as the recomposition protocol (Helms 2014, JISSN — Low-Moderate).

### 2.2 Protein: the dose-response
- Meta-regression across 49 trials: muscle and strength gains from protein plateau at **~1.6 g/kg/day**, with the 95% CI extending to **2.2 g/kg** (Morton 2018, BJSM — High). Above ~2.2 g/kg there is no additional *hypertrophy* benefit in a surplus — but in a **deficit** the picture changes.
- In energy restriction, higher protein consistently outperforms lower protein for fat-free mass retention across reviews (Cava 2017; Weinheimer 2010 — Moderate).
- Protein also has the highest satiety-per-calorie of the macronutrients and a small thermic advantage — useful in a deficit (see file 05).

### 2.3 Resistance training: the retention signal
- Meta-analysis in obese older adults: caloric restriction alone lost lean mass; **adding resistance training prevented it** (Sardeli 2018, Nutrients — Moderate).
- Systematic review of energy restriction ± exercise: **exercise (especially resistance) attenuates fat-free mass loss**; aerobic-only programmes attenuate it less reliably (Weinheimer 2010 — Moderate).
- **Practical implication:** training is not optional in a deficit if you care what you look like at the end.

### 2.4 What actually drives hypertrophy
- **Mechanical tension/progressive overload** is the primary driver; volume (sets per muscle per week) and effort (proximity to failure) are the main modulators. In a deficit, **keep loads heavy** (≈5–10 RM) and cut volume rather than intensity if recovery suffers.
- **Protein distribution:** 3–5 feedings of ~0.4 g/kg (≈30–40 g) is a reasonable, evidence-consistent pattern; total daily protein matters more than exact timing (Moderate).
- **Training frequency:** 2×/week per muscle group is at least as good as 1× when volume is equated (Moderate-High from volume/frequency meta-analyses).
- **Cardio interference:** high-volume endurance training can blunt hypertrophy; moderate cardio (walking, 2–3 sessions of 20–30 min) does not meaningfully interfere (Low-Moderate).

### 2.5 Sleep and recovery
- Sleep restriction during a deficit shifted loss toward lean tissue and reduced fat loss (Nedeltcheva 2010, Ann Intern Med — Moderate).
- Sleep extension in overweight adults reduced spontaneous energy intake by ~270 kcal/day (Tasali 2022, JAMA Intern Med — Moderate-High). Sleep is a body-composition intervention, not a luxury.

### 2.6 Age
- Older men need **more protein and more resistance training**, not less. Creatine has additional support in older adults for lean mass and strength alongside resistance training (Chilibeck 2017, Open Access J Sports Med — Moderate).

| Claim | Grade |
|---|---|
| Resistance training preserves lean mass in a deficit | **Moderate** (consistent meta-analytic direction) |
| Protein ≥1.6 g/kg supports muscle gain; deficit needs more | **High** (for the plateau); **Moderate** (for the deficit-specific number) |
| Recomposition occurs in untrained/higher-BF men | **Moderate** |
| Recomposition in lean advanced trainees is slow/rare | **Low-Moderate** (inferred; hard to randomise) |
| Sleep affects the fat:lean ratio of weight lost | **Moderate** |
| Creatine adds lean mass/strength with training | **High** (see file 16) |

## 4. Effectiveness (expected magnitude)

| Scenario | Expected 12–16 week outcome |
|---|---|
| Untrained, 25–30% BF, 20% deficit, 2 g/kg protein, 3× RT | **−6 to −10 kg fat, +1 to +3 kg lean mass** (net scale change smaller than the fat change) |
| Trained, 15% BF, similar protocol | −4 to −7 kg fat, lean mass ≈ unchanged (±1 kg) |
| Lean, advanced, 10–12% BF | Fat loss with **lean mass at risk**; recomposition unlikely; expect a slow deficit and possible strength dips |
| No resistance training, low protein | 20–30% of weight lost can be lean mass (Moderate) |

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

For recomposition specifically, the fastest safe route is **not** the largest deficit — it is the **largest deficit you can run while keeping training performance and protein intake intact** (typically 15–25%).

**Ranked by leverage:**
1. Resistance training 3–4×/week, progressive overload, 10–20 hard sets per muscle per week.
2. Protein 2.0–2.6 g/kg/day, spread over 3–4 meals (≥30 g each).
3. Deficit 15–25% (not 40%) unless you are obese and supervised.
4. Sleep 7–9 h.
5. Walking 8,000–12,000 steps; cardio as a tool, not an identity.
6. Creatine monohydrate 3–5 g/day (cheap, high-certainty, see file 16).
7. Optional: 2.5–5 g leucine-rich protein around training is not necessary if total protein is high.

**Limitations/risks:** recomposition is slow and hard to verify on a scale; water/glycogen shifts mask fat loss; over-aggressive deficits destroy the training quality that makes recomposition possible.

## 7. Practical protocol

### Minimal effective training programme (3×/week, ~40 min)
| Session | Movements |
|---|---|
| A | Squat pattern (or leg press), bench/overhead press, row, hinge (Romanian deadlift), 1 core |
| B | Hinge (deadlift variation), vertical pull (pull-up/lat pulldown), incline press, lunge/split squat, 1 carry/core |

- **Sets/reps:** 2–4 sets of 5–10 reps, 1–3 reps in reserve; add load or a rep each week.
- **Progression:** when you hit the top of the rep range on all sets, add the smallest available increment.
- **Deload:** every 6–10 weeks, halve the volume for one week if performance stalls.

### Protein: how to actually hit 2 g/kg
For an 85 kg man → ~170 g/day. Template:
- Breakfast: 3 eggs + 250 g Greek yoghurt (~40 g)
- Lunch: 200 g chicken/fish/lean beef + legumes (~55 g)
- Snack: 300 ml milk + 30 g whey or 200 g cottage cheese (~30 g)
- Dinner: 200 g protein source + vegetables (~50 g)

### In a deficit, protect muscle with these rules
1. Never let protein drop below 1.8 g/kg.
2. Keep at least 2 hard sets per muscle per week even when tired.
3. Don't add cardio to compensate for a bad diet — fix the diet.
4. If strength drops >10% over 3 weeks, the deficit is too aggressive.

---

## 8. Low-maintenance version

- **Two full-body sessions per week** (minimum effective dose for retention) of 5 movements, 3 sets each.
- **Protein at every meal** — one palm-and-a-half of animal/soy protein or a shake if needed.
- **Creatine 5 g/day, forever** — the only supplement in this file worth defaulting to.
- **Walk daily.** Nothing else is required to keep muscle while losing fat.

---

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Kidney disease:** high protein requires medical supervision. In healthy kidneys, high protein intake has not been shown to cause damage (Moderate).
- **Gout:** high-purine protein sources (organ meats, shellfish, some fish) may trigger flares; dairy and plant proteins are favourable.
- **Heart failure, uncontrolled hypertension:** avoid heavy Valsalva straining; get clearance.
- **Injury/pain:** sharp joint pain, numbness, or pain that worsens across sessions = stop the movement pattern and get assessed.
- **Avoid** anabolic steroids/SARMs. The documented harms (dyslipidaemia, hypertension, cardiomyopathy, hypogonadism, infertility, hepatic injury) outweigh the cosmetic benefit, and legality varies by country. This library does not provide dosing guidance for controlled substances.
- **Men over 45 with symptoms** (chest pain, unusual breathlessness) need medical assessment before intense training.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Cutting calories hard and skipping training** — fastest way to lose muscle.
2. **Protein at dinner only** — total is what matters most, but a 40 g breakfast makes the target achievable.
3. **Chasing weight on the scale rather than waist + strength + photos** — recomposition can look like "nothing happening" for 6 weeks.
4. **Swapping lifting for HIIT** — cardiovascular benefit, worse muscle retention.
5. **Believing you need a "bulk"** after a diet — most men at 15–20% BF do better with slow recomposition.
6. **Buying BCAAs/HMB/glutamine/test boosters** — none adds meaningfully to adequate protein.
7. **Training to failure on every set in a deficit** — recovery cost exceeds the stimulus.
8. **Ignoring sleep** — it changes *what* you lose, not just how fast.

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. Resistance train **2–4×/week**, progressively, forever.
2. Protein **2.0–2.6 g/kg/day** in a deficit; **1.6–2.2 g/kg** at maintenance.
3. Keep the deficit **moderate** (15–25%) — recomposition is a patience strategy, not a sprint.
4. Sleep 7–9 h; walk 8–12k steps.
5. Creatine 5 g/day.
6. Judge progress by **waist, photos, and strength**, never by scale weight alone.
