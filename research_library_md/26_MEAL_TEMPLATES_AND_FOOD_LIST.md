# 26 — Meal Templates, Food Lists & Shopping

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**. This is the practical "what do I actually eat" file — designed for repetition, low cost, high protein and high fibre.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Repetition is a feature.** Men who succeed long-term eat a small rotation of meals. Variety is where adherence dies.
- Every template below hits **35–55 g protein per meal** and **8–15 g fibre**, and uses cheap, widely available foods.
- **Cost:** the core templates cost roughly **$4–8/day** in most high-income countries and less in many others (local prices vary; see file 09 for cost-per-gram-protein logic).
- **The 4-item shopping rule:** every week buy **(1) a protein for every meal, (2) frozen vegetables, (3) fruit, (4) a fibre base (oats/legumes)**.
- **Restaurant/travel rules** matter more than home cooking for many men: protein-first ordering, no liquid calories, one starch, skip desserts and starters.
- **No foods are banned.** The templates are a default, not a religion; 2–4 flexible meals per week are part of the plan (file 19).

---

## 2. What the evidence establishes

- High-protein, high-fibre, low-energy-density meals increase satiety per calorie (files 05, 08, 09 — **Moderate-High**).
- Meal consistency and repeated breakfast are associated with successful weight maintenance (NWCR data — **Low-Moderate**).
- Minimally processed diets reduce spontaneous intake versus ultra-processed diets at the same "diet quality" (Hall 2019 — **Moderate-High**).
- Legumes, oats and vegetables are the cheapest sources of fibre and provide good protein density (USDA FoodData Central — **High** for composition data).

---

## 3. Evidence quality

**Moderate-High** for the nutritional principles; the specific recipes are engineering, not evidence. Their value is adherence.

---

## 4. Effectiveness (expected magnitude)

Following these templates for 12 weeks with no other change (no counting):
- Protein intake typically rises from ~0.9 g/kg to **1.6–2.2 g/kg** — the single biggest dietary improvement most men can make.
- Fibre rises from ~15 g to **30–40 g/day**.
- Spontaneous intake falls by **200–500 kcal/day** for many men (Moderate).

---

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

Pick **one** breakfast, **two** lunches, **three** dinners and **one** snack from the lists below. Eat those for 2 weeks. Then rotate one item per week. Buy the same shopping list each week. Add a 20% deficit only after the pattern is automatic (usually 2–3 weeks).

---

## 6. Free and cheap alternatives

Cheapest protein per gram (file 09): **lentils/beans, milk, eggs, chicken thighs, canned fish**. Cheapest fibre: **oats, cabbage, carrots, frozen vegetables, legumes**. Cheapest calories overall: **rice, potatoes, oats, oil** — these are not the enemy; they are the budget.

If money is very tight: **eggs + oats + lentils + frozen vegetables + milk + rice + seasonal fruit** is a complete, high-protein, high-fibre diet for a few dollars a day.

## 7. Practical protocol

### Breakfasts (pick 1–2)
| Template | Protein | Fibre | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3 eggs + 200 g Greek yoghurt + 40 g oats + berries** | ~40 g | ~8 g | The default |
| **Overnight oats**: 60 g oats + 250 ml milk + 200 g yoghurt + 1 scoop whey + cinnamon | ~45 g | ~9 g | 2 min prep the night before |
| **Cottage cheese bowl**: 250 g cottage cheese + fruit + 30 g nuts | ~35 g | ~6 g | No cooking |
| **Egg wrap**: 3 eggs + wholegrain tortilla + spinach + salsa | ~30 g | ~7 g | Portable |
| **Tofu scramble**: 250 g firm tofu + vegetables + 2 slices wholegrain bread | ~30 g | ~8 g | Vegetarian |

### Lunches/dinners (pick 3–4)
| Template | Protein | Fibre | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chicken + rice + frozen veg**: 200 g chicken, 200 g cooked rice, 250 g vegetables, 1 tbsp olive oil | ~50 g | ~10 g | The workhorse |
| **Lentil/chickpea stew**: 250 g cooked legumes + 150 g rice + vegetables + spices | ~25 g | ~15 g | Cheapest meal |
| **Canned fish plate**: 2 tins sardines/tuna + 2 slices bread + salad + olive oil | ~45 g | ~8 g | No cooking |
| **Beef/pork stir-fry**: 180 g lean meat + 300 g mixed vegetables + 100 g noodles | ~45 g | ~9 g | 12 min |
| **Egg-based dinner**: 4 eggs + 300 g vegetables + 150 g potatoes | ~30 g | ~10 g | Cheap |
| **Yoghurt + legume salad**: 200 g yoghurt + 200 g chickpeas + cucumber/tomato/herbs | ~30 g | ~10 g | No cooking |
| **Tofu/tempeh bowl**: 250 g tofu + 150 g rice + vegetables + sesame oil | ~30 g | ~8 g | Vegetarian |

### Snacks (pick 1)
- 300 ml milk + fruit (~15 g protein)
- 200 g cottage cheese (~25 g)
- 2 boiled eggs (~12 g)
- 30 g whey in water (~25 g)
- 1 tin tuna (~25 g)
- Handful of nuts + fruit (small; calorie-dense)

### The 7-day rotation (example)
| Day | Breakfast | Lunch | Dinner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Eggs + yoghurt + oats | Chicken + rice + veg | Lentil stew |
| Tue | Overnight oats | Canned fish plate | Beef stir-fry |
| Wed | Cottage cheese bowl | Chicken + rice + veg | Egg + veg + potato |
| Thu | Eggs + yoghurt + oats | Lentil stew | Chicken + vegetables |
| Fri | Overnight oats | Chickpea salad + yoghurt | Canned fish plate |
| Sat | Flexible meal (eat out) | Protein + veg + 1 starch | Home template |
| Sun | Eggs + yoghurt + oats | Chicken + rice + veg | Batch-cook lentils for the week |

### The weekly shopping list (one page)
**Protein:** eggs (18–24), milk (2–3 L), plain yoghurt or cottage cheese (1 kg), chicken (1.5 kg) or tofu, canned fish (4–6 tins), lentils/chickpeas (500 g dry or 4 tins), whey (optional).
**Fibre/veg:** oats (1 kg), frozen mixed vegetables (1.5 kg), cabbage/carrots, tomatoes/cucumber, onions/garlic, seasonal fruit (2 kg), potatoes or rice (1–2 kg).
**Fats/flavour:** olive oil, spices, salt, vinegar, mustard, herbs.
**Skip:** juice, soft drinks, crisps/snacks, breakfast cereals with added sugar, "protein" bars, ice cream, alcohol (or 1–2 units max).

### Eating out (rules that work anywhere)
1. **Order a protein main first** — grilled/baked/roasted, not fried or creamy.
2. **Vegetables or salad as the side**, starch second.
3. **Water or sparkling water**; no sugary drinks; max 1–2 alcoholic drinks.
4. **Skip starters, bread basket and dessert** — or share one dessert, deliberately.
5. **Eat slowly**, stop at 8/10 fullness; box the rest.
6. **Don't "save" calories by skipping meals before eating out** — you will overeat.

### Travel
- Airport/supermarket: yoghurt, milk, bananas, pre-cooked chicken, tinned fish, nuts, wholegrain sandwiches.
- Hotel breakfast: eggs, yoghurt, fruit, cold meats — skip pastries and juice.
- Keep the **step target** and **protein**; let the rest be flexible.

### Batch cooking (Sunday, 60–90 min)
1. Cook 1 kg of a protein (chicken thighs/beef mince/lentils).
2. Cook 1 kg of a starch (rice/potatoes).
3. Roast or steam 1.5 kg of vegetables.
4. Portion into 4–5 containers; freeze half.
5. Boil 8 eggs for snacks.

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**Two meals you repeat and one you vary.** Example: eggs+yoghurt+oats every morning; chicken+rice+frozen veg every lunch; a rotating high-protein dinner. That's 30 minutes of cooking per day and no decisions.

---

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Food safety:** cook meat/fish thoroughly, refrigerate leftovers within 2 h, reheat to steaming, avoid reheating rice more than once.
- **Allergies:** check labels for milk, soy, egg, gluten, fish; substitute isolates accordingly.
- **Kidney disease:** protein targets in this file do not apply — follow your clinician/dietitian.
- **Gout:** limit organ meats/shellfish/beer; dairy and legumes are generally protective.
- **IBD/IBS:** high-fibre templates may need modification (clinician-guided).
- **Diabetes on insulin/sulfonylureas:** carbohydrate amounts in these templates may require dose adjustment with your clinician.
- **Eating disorders:** rigid meal plans can be harmful; seek professional support rather than following templates.

---

## 10. Products and tools

Optional and cheap: food scale, meal-prep containers, a large non-stick pan, a rice cooker (if rice is a staple), a freezer (batch cooking). No supplements required — protein powder is a convenience item only (file 18).

---

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Trying 20 new recipes in week 1** — decision fatigue, waste, abandonment.
2. **No protein at breakfast** — hunger all day.
3. **Buying "healthy" snacks** (protein bars, granola, smoothies) that are calorie-dense.
4. **Not batch cooking** — takeaway wins by default.
5. **Drinking calories.**
6. **Under-buying vegetables** — buy more than you think you need, frozen if fresh spoils.
7. **Cooking one portion at a time** — doubles effort.
8. **No plan for eating out** — one restaurant meal can erase a week's deficit if unplanned.
9. **Believing you must eat "clean" 100%** — 80–90% is the target.
10. **Spending money on supplements instead of protein and vegetables.**

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Pick 6 meals and repeat them** for 2 weeks.
2. **Every meal: protein first, vegetables second, starch third.**
3. **Buy the same shopping list weekly.**
4. **Batch cook once a week.**
5. **Have restaurant and travel rules written down.**
6. **Keep 2–4 flexible meals per week** — planned, not accidental.
7. **Spend on protein and vegetables, not on supplements.**
