# 12 — Men's Health & Longevity

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- For a man without a diagnosed disease, **the highest-value longevity interventions are unglamorous and nearly free**: don't smoke, keep blood pressure low, stay fit/strong, maintain a healthy waist, sleep, limit alcohol, eat mostly plants and adequate protein, and treat dental and mental health.
- Ranked by evidence for **reducing all-cause mortality**, the top five are: **not smoking, cardiorespiratory fitness, blood-pressure control, physical activity (including strength training), and body composition/waist circumference** (High).
- **Fitness is the most under-rated**: each 1-MET increase in aerobic capacity ≈ 13% lower all-cause mortality (Kodama 2009 — High); strength training 30–60 min/week ≈ 10–17% lower mortality (Momma 2022 — Moderate-High).
- **Blood pressure control** has the largest randomised evidence base of any single physiological target: intensive control (<120 mmHg) reduced major CV events and mortality in SPRINT (2015 — High).
- **Smoking** is the single largest modifiable risk: quitting before 40 recovers ~90% of the decade of life lost to continued smoking (Jha 2013 — High).
- **Alcohol:** the largest global analysis concluded that **no level of consumption improves health** (GBD 2018 — Moderate); the honest recommendation is "less is better, none is best", not "one glass for the heart".
- **Testosterone declines modestly with age and substantially with obesity and poor sleep.** Weight loss, sleep, and resistance training raise it naturally; medication is appropriate only for *symptomatic, biochemically confirmed* hypogonadism under medical supervision (Moderate).
- Almost nothing sold as "longevity" has human outcome evidence. File 13 grades each popular intervention.

## 2. What the evidence establishes

### 2.1 What actually moves mortality risk

| Dental health | Observational; shared risk factors | **Low-Moderate** |
| Sun protection | Green 2011; Hughes 2013 | **High** |

### 2.2 Cardiorespiratory fitness and strength
- Each 1-MET increase in fitness → **13% lower all-cause mortality, 15% lower CVD mortality** (Kodama 2009, JAMA — High).
- In 122,007 treadmill tests, elite fitness had a mortality hazard of roughly **0.20** vs the least fit, with no evidence of harm at the top (Mandsager 2018 — High).
- Muscle-strengthening 30–60 min/week → **10–17% lower all-cause mortality**, with a J-shaped dose-response (Momma 2022 — Moderate-High).

### 2.3 Blood pressure
- SPRINT: targeting systolic <120 mmHg reduced major cardiovascular events by ~25% and all-cause mortality by ~27% vs <140 mmHg (SPRINT 2015, NEJM — High), at the cost of more adverse events (hypotension, acute kidney injury, electrolyte abnormalities).
- Exercise lowers resting BP by ~4–5 mmHg on average; dietary sodium reduction, weight loss, and reduced alcohol add to this (High for sodium and weight).
- **Practical:** know your BP. A $25 home monitor is one of the highest-value health purchases a man can make.

### 2.4 Smoking, alcohol, other substances
- Smoking: quitting before 40 recovers ~90% of the life-expectancy loss; quitting at any age helps (Jha 2013 — High).
- Alcohol: GBD analysis found the health-loss-minimising intake was **zero** (GBD 2018 — Moderate). Alcohol is a Group 1 carcinogen; it also adds calories, disrupts sleep and increases injury risk.
- **Cannabis/smoking anything** harms the airway; vaping's long-term risk is unknown (Insufficient).
- **Anabolic steroids/SARMs:** documented harms include hypertension, dyslipidaemia, left-ventricular hypertrophy, hypogonadism, infertility and hepatic injury. Not recommended (Moderate).

### 2.5 Metabolic health
- **Waist circumference** is a better practical marker than BMI: >94 cm (37 in) in men is the common European threshold for increased risk; >102 cm (40 in) substantially increased. Asian populations use lower thresholds (guideline consensus — Moderate).
- **HbA1c, fasting glucose, lipids, blood pressure, and liver enzymes** are the five numbers worth knowing.
- Losing **5–10% of body weight** improves blood pressure, glucose, lipids, sleep apnoea and joint pain (High, guideline-level).

### 2.6 Testosterone (men's endocrine health)
- Testosterone declines ~1%/year after 30, and more with obesity, poor sleep, and inactivity. **Weight loss raises testosterone** — a meta-analysis/nomogram quantified gains from weight loss (Ken-Dror 2024, Andrology — Moderate).
- Testosterone therapy is indicated for **symptomatic hypogonadism with confirmed low levels** (two morning measurements). In older men (T Trials) it improved sexual function, mood and bone density modestly, with some increase in haematocrit and (in one trial) cardiovascular signal questions; the TRAVERSE trial was designed to address cardiovascular safety (Snyder 2016, NEJM — Moderate; Cruickshank 2024, HTA evidence synthesis — Moderate).
- **Free interventions first:** sleep, weight loss, resistance training, alcohol reduction. Then get tested. Avoid "testosterone boosters" (file 23).

### 2.7 Mental health, social connection and stress
- Depression and anxiety worsen adherence to everything and independently affect mortality. Exercise has a modest antidepressant effect (Moderate).
- Social isolation is consistently associated with higher mortality (Moderate, observational); this is a real health behaviour, not a soft one.
- Chronic stress drives intake, alcohol use, poor sleep and (via blood pressure) cardiovascular risk.

### 2.8 Screening (men, general guidance — follow local/national programmes)
- **Blood pressure** — annually (or at every visit).
- **Lipids and HbA1c** — every 1–5 years depending on risk (more often if abnormal).
- **Colorectal cancer screening** — typically from age 45–50 (US) or 50–60 (many other systems).
- **Prostate discussion** — individualised (PSA shared decision, typically 50–70).
- **Dental check** — 6–12 monthly.
- **Vision/hearing** — periodically.
- **Vaccinations** — per national schedule.
- **Depression screen** — PHQ-2/9 in primary care.
- **Skin** — check changing moles; sunscreen daily (file 14).

## 3. Evidence quality

High for: smoking, fitness, BP control, activity, diet pattern, sun protection. Moderate for: alcohol, sleep, strength dose, testosterone (for symptoms). Low-Moderate for: dental, social, stress interventions. Insufficient for most "longevity" supplements (file 13).

## 4. Effectiveness (expected magnitude)

| Intervention | Realistic effect |
|---|---|
| Quit smoking (age <40) | Regain ~9–10 years of life expectancy |
| Move from low to high fitness | ~50–70% lower all-cause mortality in cohort data |
| 150–300 min/wk activity + 2 strength sessions | ~20–35% lower all-cause mortality (observational) |
| BP from 150/95 → 125/80 | Large reduction in stroke and CV events |
| Lose 5–10% body weight | Better glucose, BP, lipids, sleep apnoea, joints |
| 30–60 g/day fibre | Lower CVD, diabetes, colorectal cancer risk |
| Daily sunscreen | 24% less skin-ageing progression; ~50% lower melanoma incidence at 10-year follow-up (HR 0.50; 95% CI 0.24–1.02) |

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

**The "boring but enormous" list, in implementation order:**
1. **Don't smoke; don't vape.** If you smoke, that is priority #1 over every supplement in this library.
2. **Know your numbers:** BP, waist, fasting glucose/HbA1c, lipids. A home BP monitor + one blood panel per year.
3. **Train 150–300 min/week + 2 strength sessions.** Fitness is the mortality lever you control.
4. **Get waist under ~94 cm** (or ≤90 cm if of South Asian descent).
5. **Sleep 7–9 h; fixed wake time.**
6. **Alcohol: ≤0–4 units/week.**
7. **Eat: protein + fibre + vegetables + olive oil/fish/nuts** — Mediterranean-style.
8. **Daily sunscreen.**
9. **Dental check + treat depression/anxiety if present.**
10. **Then** — and only then — consider supplements (file 16).

## 7. Practical protocol

### The annual health review (one hour, once a year)
1. **Measure:** weight, waist, BP (average of 3 readings), resting heart rate.
2. **Bloods:** full blood count, glucose/HbA1c, lipids, liver and kidney function, vitamin D if indicated, PSA discussion if 50+.
3. **Behaviour audit:** smoking, alcohol units/week, sleep hours, training sessions/week, step average, fibre/protein intake.
4. **One change** for the year — the weakest link above.

### The daily minimum
- 10,000 steps, 1 protein-anchored meal pattern, 7.5 h sleep, no smoking, ≤1 drink, sunscreen, 10-min walk after dinner.

### If you are 40+ and untrained
- Get a check-up; start with walking and 2 light full-body sessions/week; add load over 8–12 weeks.
- Do **not** start with HIIT, heavy lifting and a crash diet simultaneously.

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**Five non-negotiables:** no smoking · BP checked · 8,000+ steps · 2 strength sessions · 7+ h sleep. Add sunscreen and a waist target. That is a complete longevity programme, and it costs nothing.

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Do not start vigorous exercise** without clearance if you have known cardiovascular disease or symptoms.
- **BP measurements**: use a validated upper-arm cuff, seated, 5 min rest, average of 2–3 readings on 2 occasions.
- **Testosterone**: only with confirmed diagnosis; risks include erythrocytosis, acne, infertility/azoospermia, possible prostate effects, and expense; it is a controlled substance in many countries.
- **5-ARIs (finasteride/dutasteride)**: sexual and mood side effects are real, including a persistent syndrome in a minority; discuss before starting (file 24).
- **Screening harms**: false positives, overdiagnosis, and unnecessary procedures — follow guideline-based programmes, not "full-body scans".
- **Mental health:** if you have thoughts of self-harm, seek urgent help.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Optimising supplements before smoking, BP, sleep and fitness.**
2. **Not knowing your blood pressure** — the most common silent risk in men 40+.
3. **Believing "one glass of red wine is healthy"** — the evidence now says otherwise.
4. **Buying biological-age tests** instead of measuring waist and VO₂-ish fitness.
5. **Ignoring mental health and social connection** while chasing biomarkers.
6. **Skipping screening** because you "feel fine".
7. **Starting an extreme protocol at 45** after 20 sedentary years.
8. **Using testosterone clinics that diagnose on a single borderline test.**
9. **Assuming fitness from gym attendance** — track steps and strength, not intentions.
10. **Waiting for symptoms** — hypertension, dyslipidaemia and prediabetes are silent.

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Stop smoking. Limit alcohol (ideally to zero).**
2. **Measure BP, waist, glucose, lipids yearly.**
3. **Train for fitness and strength** — 150–300 min/week + 2× strength.
4. **Get and keep your waist down.**
5. **Sleep 7–9 h.**
6. **Eat Mediterranean-style with high protein and fibre.**
7. **Sunscreen daily, dental check yearly, treat depression/anxiety.**
8. **Skip the longevity industry** until the free basics are done — and after they are done, you will find you need very little of it.
