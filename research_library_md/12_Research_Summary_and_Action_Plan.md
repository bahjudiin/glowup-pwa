# 12 — Research Summary and Action Plan

*Phase 2 module, final file. Synthesises files `01`–`11`. Reference list: `11_Evidence_and_References.md`. Phase-1 synthesis: `00_MASTER_SUMMARY.md`.*

---

## 1. What this module set out to answer

> "Make fat loss easier, cheaper, faster where safe, and sustainable with minimal daily effort — without repeating previous research, and using foods and products actually obtainable in Mogadishu."

**Answer in one paragraph:** the highest-value interventions are not products and are not fasting schedules. They are, in order: **enough protein, enough food volume from cheap vegetables, enough sleep, resistance training twice a week, and no hyper-palatable food in the house.** Everything else — fasting windows, fibre supplements, products, timing tricks — changes *how easy it is to keep the first five going*. Their value is adherence, not magic. The evidence for this ordering is consistent across independent literatures (satiety, sleep, energy density, protein, resistance training, maintenance), and it survived every attempt in this research to find something cheaper or faster that actually works.

## 2. The ten strongest findings of the module

| # | Finding | Evidence | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Protein is the most powerful food-level hunger lever.** ~30% protein produced **−441 kcal/day** spontaneous intake with no deliberate restriction | Controlled feeding study ([Weigle 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16002798/)) + RCTs | `02` §3.3 |
| 2 | **Energy density drives passive overeating.** Identical-macro ultra-processed vs unprocessed diets differed by **+508 kcal/day** | Inpatient RCT ([Hall 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31105044/)) | `01`, `02` §3.2 |
| 3 | **Short sleep shifts loss away from fat and toward muscle** (0.6 vs 1.4 kg fat lost; +60% FFM loss) | Controlled inpatient trial ([Nedeltcheva 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20921542/)) | `05` §2.1 |
| 4 | **Extending sleep cuts spontaneous intake ~270 kcal/day** | RCT ([Tasali 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35129580/)) | `05` §2.1 |
| 5 | **Intermittent fasting is not superior to any other way of eating less**, and 16:8 can cost lean mass | Meta-analysis ([Enríquez Guerrero 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33293678/)); 12-month RCT ([Liu 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35443107/)); 12-week RCT ([TREAT 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32986097/)) | `03` §4.1 |
| 6 | **Resistance training + adequate protein preserves lean mass in a deficit**; slower loss preserves more | RCTs ([Garthe 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21558571/)); meta-analysis ([Morton 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)) | `06` |
| 7 | **Boiled/baked potato had the highest satiety score of 38 foods tested at equal calories**, and it is cheap here | Satiety index ([Holt 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7498104/)) | `01` §8 |
| 8 | **Soup/liquid form changes intake**: soup preloads reduce meal intake; caloric liquids are poorly compensated | RCTs ([Flood 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17574705/); [Cassady 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22258267/)) | `01`, `02` §3.5 |
| 9 | **Hunger hormones stay altered after weight loss**, so maintenance is genuinely harder than loss | Prospective cohort ([Sumithran 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22029981/)) | `08` §3.4 |
| 10 | **Regain is the default without continued structure** (−4.7% vs −2.1% at 8 years; rapid regain on stopping medication) | RCTs ([Look AHEAD](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24307184/); [SURMOUNT-4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38078870/)) | `08` §6 |

## 3. The 20 highest-value actions, ranked

Ranking criteria, applied jointly: **evidence quality → effectiveness → affordability → safety → ease**. Rank 1 is the best combination of all five, not the most effective single intervention in isolation.

| # | Action | Why it ranks here | Evidence | Cost | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Eat 25–40 g protein at every main meal** (≈110–140 g/day; eggs, chicken, goat, fish, lentils) | Best-evidenced hunger lever; also protects muscle; locally cheap | High | Low | Low |
| **2** | **Make half of every plate non-starchy vegetables, eaten first** (400 g; cabbage, tomato, eggplant, onion, carrot) | Largest volume per calorie; drives the energy-density effect; cheapest food here | High | Very low | Low |
| **3** | **Fix sleep: same wake time, 7–9 h in bed, last caffeine 8 h before bed** | Free; changes intake by ~270 kcal/day and protects lean mass | High | Free | Medium |
| **4** | **Two resistance sessions per week, progressive, at home** | The dominant muscle-preservation signal; free | High | Free | Medium |
| **5** | **Remove hyper-palatable/ultra-processed food from your home** | Removes the ~500 kcal/day passive-overeating environment; also saves money | High | Saves money | Low |
| **6** | **Close the kitchen 3 h before bed (12:12–14:10 window)** | The least restrictive fasting schedule with meaningful results; prevents late-night eating | Moderate–high | Free | Low |
| **7** | **Stop drinking calories** (juice, soda, sweetened tea/coffee) | Liquids are poorly compensated; instant deficit with no hunger cost | High (mechanism) | Saves money | Low |
| **8** | **Start every main meal with soup/broth or 400–500 ml water** | Reduces intake; free; renal-safe if you don't overdo it | Moderate (RCTs) | Near free | Low |
| **9** | **Eat your slowest meal in 20+ minutes; sit down, no phone** | Eating rate has sustained effects on intake | Moderate–high | Free | Low |
| **10** | **Get fibre to 25–35 g/day, increasing by ~5 g/week** (lentils, oats, cabbage, okra, guava) | Appetite + bowel function + metabolic health; cheap | High | Low | Low |
| **11** | **Weigh daily and judge only the 7-day average; measure waist weekly** | Prevents quitting on water-weight noise; costs nothing | Moderate | Free | Very low |
| **12** | **Walk 7,000–10,000 steps/day** | Supports energy balance without adding hunger/training cost | Moderate | Free | Low |
| **13** | **Batch-cook protein and vegetables twice a week** | Removes the "too tired to cook" failure mode; the highest-leverage behavioural habit found | Moderate (behavioural) | Low | Medium |
| **14** | **Use potato, oats-as-porridge, and lentils/beans as default starches** | Highest satiety per calorie among cheap starches | High (satiety index, pulses) | Very low | Low |
| **15** | **Buy a digital kitchen scale (~$8–20) and weigh oil and grains** | Fixes the largest hidden calorie source; one-time cost | Moderate (behavioural) | ~$10 | Very low |
| **16** | **Set a maintenance band (±2 kg) and a written trigger rule** | Converts maintenance from willpower into a procedure; the difference between result and regain | Moderate | Free | Very low |
| **17** | **Screen for sleep apnoea (STOP-BANG) if you snore and are tired** | Untreated OSA sabotages energy, appetite and blood pressure; treatable | Moderate–high | Free screening | Very low |
| **18** | **Add psyllium 5 g/day only if constipated or fibre intake is genuinely low** | Good evidence for constipation/LDL; small for weight; cheap | High (for bowel) | ~$0.16/day | Very low |
| **19** | **Take creatine monohydrate 3–5 g/day only if training consistently** | Modest, real training/lean-mass support | Moderate | ~$0.16–0.21/day | Very low |
| **20** | **Plan one week at maintenance every 8–12 weeks (diet break)** | Adherence tool for hunger/fatigue; not a metabolic accelerator | Moderate (adherence) | Free | Low |

**Deliberately *not* in the top 20** (and where they ranked): 16:8 fasting (middle — equivalent results, higher cost/difficulty); whey protein (low — food is cheaper here); electrolyte products (low — a problem most people don't have); fat burners, appetite-suppressant supplements, "natural GLP-1" products, detox teas, waist trainers, body-fat scales, CGMs (all bottom — rejected).

## 4. What the module found that is genuinely *cheap*

A full day can be built on: **eggs, cabbage, tomato, onion, eggplant, potato, lentils, seasonal fruit, and local fish or chicken**, with **water, tea, sleep, walking and a floor to exercise on.** Every one of the top 10 actions costs nothing or very little, and none requires an imported product.

**Estimated** (not verified) local prices from third-party sources put several of these items at roughly $0.50–2.00/kg or per unit — see `01` §2 for why those numbers must be treated as rough and re-checked locally.

**Total justifiable spend for the whole module: roughly $20–50**, concentrated in a kitchen scale (~$10) and optionally psyllium (~$11–21).

## 5. What to ignore (this saves money and attention)

| Ignored | Why |
|---|---|
| Fat burners / thermogenics | No durable effect; stimulant risk |
| Appetite-suppressant supplements; "natural Ozempic"/"GLP-1 booster" products | No clinical evidence of drug-like effects |
| Detox/cleansing teas, salt-water flushes, colon cleanses | Laxative effect and electrolyte risk; no benefit |
| Extended fasting (36 h+) as a routine | No advantage; refeeding, electrolyte, gallstone and muscle risks |
| 20:4 / OMAD | Harder, worse protein intake, no better results |
| Body-fat scales, smart rings, CGMs for fat loss | Inaccurate or unproven for this purpose |
| Reverse dieting, "metabolism reset" protocols, metabolic testing | No RCT support as distinct methods |
| Whey protein while eating eggs and chicken | You are paying for convenience you don't need |
| Electrolyte powders on a normal diet | Solving a problem you don't have |
| Meal replacements, "zero-calorie" noodles, fibre gummies | Worse satiety per shilling than the foods in `01` |

## 6. What is still unknown or uncertain (stated, not papered over)

1. **Long-term effects of any fasting schedule beyond 12 months** — the trials are short.
2. **Whether diet breaks truly improve long-term fat loss** or only adherence — suggestive, not settled.
3. **Human relevance of fasting-induced autophagy** — biomarker-level only; no outcome data; no hour thresholds.
4. **Magnitude of adaptive thermogenesis** in ordinary (non-extreme) dieters — variable across studies.
5. **Behavioural treatment of stress/emotional eating** — the literature is thin and heterogeneous; this module gives behavioural steps, not effect sizes.
6. **Somali-specific intake, body composition and price data** — essentially absent from the indexed literature; every local number here is Estimated or Unknown.
7. **Effect of chronic heat on appetite, hydration and training capacity** — under-studied, and relevant to Mogadishu.
8. **Whether any food pattern can match pharmacotherapy** — no evidence that it can; this module does not claim it.

## 7. The 30-day plan (putting the top actions in order)

**Week 1 — environment and protein (actions 1, 5, 7, 14)**
- Buy: eggs, chicken or fish, lentils, cabbage, tomato, onion, eggplant, potato, seasonal fruit.
- Remove one hyper-palatable food from the house permanently.
- Add protein to every meal; switch all drinks to water/plain tea.
- No fasting, no supplements, no weighing yet. Just these three things.

**Week 2 — volume and sleep (actions 2, 3, 8)**
- Half-plate vegetables at both main meals; soup or water first.
- Set a fixed wake time and 7–9 h in bed; last caffeine 8 h before bed.
- Start weighing daily — but read only the weekly average.

**Week 3 — training and kitchen close (actions 4, 6, 12, 13)**
- Two 25-minute bodyweight sessions; add one rep or one second per week.
- Kitchen closes 3 h before bed.
- Walk 7–10k steps; batch-cook protein twice this week.

**Week 4 — measure, adjust, and lock in (actions 9, 10, 11, 15, 16)**
- Add fibre gradually to 25–35 g/day.
- Eat one meal a day in 20+ minutes, no phone.
- Buy a kitchen scale; weigh oil and grains for one week.
- Write your maintenance band and trigger rule **now**, before you need it.
- Review: weekly average weight, waist, sessions done, sleep hours. Change **one** thing for next month.

**Do not** add fasting windows beyond 12:12, supplements, or extra cardio in the first 30 days. There is nothing in this month that requires them.

## 8. Summary

### 8.1 Strongest verified findings
Protein, food volume/energy density, sleep, resistance training and removing hyper-palatable food are the five levers with high-quality human evidence. Fasting schedules, fibre supplements and products are adherence tools with small or absent independent effects. Appetite physiology turns against you after weight loss, which is why maintenance needs a permanent system rather than a finish line.

### 8.2 Most practical actions
Rank 1–10 of §3 — all of them free or nearly free, all achievable in Mogadishu with local food.

### 8.3 Cheapest effective options
Eggs, cabbage, tomato, eggplant, potato, lentils, local fish, seasonal fruit; water; sleep; walking; bodyweight training; a notebook. **Under $10 of tools is enough to run the entire module.**

### 8.4 What is unnecessary
Fasting beyond 12:12–14:10 for most people; whey; electrolyte products; psyllium unless constipated; creatine unless training; and every product class listed in §5.

### 8.5 What is unsupported or misleading
- Any claim that a food reproduces GLP-1 drug effects.
- "Fasting for autophagy/longevity" as a human outcome claim.
- "Metabolic damage" and "reverse dieting."
- "You must fast 16 hours to burn fat."
- Precise hour thresholds for cellular processes.
- Any invented local price or product spec — all prices here are labelled Verified/Estimated/Unknown, and **no local price in this module is Verified**.

### 8.6 Important risks
Fasting on insulin/sulfonylureas or BP medication without medical advice; refeeding syndrome in genuinely malnourished people; over-drinking water or unsupervised electrolyte/potassium use; eating-disorder escalation from rigid fasting rules; pushing through warning signs (dizziness, palpitations, fainting, confusion, chest pain) — which this module never recommends; extreme restriction in response to a plateau.

### 8.7 Concise action plan (one paragraph)
Eat protein at every meal, fill half the plate with cheap vegetables and eat them first, sleep a fixed 7–9 hours, do two short resistance sessions a week, keep hyper-palatable food out of the house, close the kitchen three hours before bed, drink only water and unsweetened tea, walk daily, weigh daily but judge weekly, and write down a 2 kg maintenance band with a trigger rule **before** you reach your goal. Buy a kitchen scale and nothing else for the first month.
