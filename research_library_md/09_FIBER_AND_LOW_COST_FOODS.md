# 09 — Fibre & Low-Cost Foods

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Fibre is the cheapest, most under-used tool in fat loss.** At 25–30+ g/day it is associated with lower all-cause mortality, lower cardiovascular disease, lower type-2-diabetes risk, lower body weight and better lipid profiles (Reynolds 2019, Lancet — High).
- Most men eat **15–20 g/day**. Closing that gap costs **$0.30–0.80/day** using legumes, oats, frozen vegetables, cabbage, carrots and apples — and it is **more effective for satiety than any fibre supplement**.
- **Psyllium** is the best-evidenced fibre supplement (satiety, glycaemic control, cholesterol, constipation) and costs roughly **$0.15–0.20 per serving** at verified prices (file 18).
- **Glucomannan** has weaker evidence and more side effects; **inulin/chicory** cause more bloating; **"fibre blends"** are usually expensive versions of the same thing.
- The cheapest high-protein, high-fibre foods in nearly every market are: **eggs, milk, plain yoghurt, lentils, beans, chickpeas, oats, frozen vegetables, cabbage, carrots, canned fish, chicken thighs**.
- **Do not increase fibre quickly.** Add ~5 g/week with more water to avoid bloating and abandoning it.

---

## 2. What the evidence establishes

### 2.1 Outcomes
- Dose-response meta-analysis (185 cohorts, 58 trials): greatest risk reduction at **25–29 g/day** of total fibre, with additional benefit above 30 g; higher fibre intake associated with **lower body weight, systolic BP and total cholesterol** (Reynolds 2019 — High).
- Whole-grain intake shows similar dose-response benefits (same analysis — High).
- Viscous/soluble fibres (psyllium, oats/β-glucan, pectin, guar) slow gastric emptying and blunt postprandial glucose; insoluble fibres (wheat bran, cellulose) mainly aid stool bulk (Moderate).

### 2.2 Satiety
- Soluble fibre supplementation increases perceived satiety and reduces short-term energy intake (Salleh 2019, Foods — Moderate, small studies).
- **Psyllium** specifically increased satiety in controlled studies (Brum 2016 — Moderate) and improves glycaemic control and LDL.
- **Glucomannan**: meta-analysis found modest weight reduction (under 1 kg beyond placebo) with meaningful lipid effects and frequent GI side effects (Sood 2008 — Low-Moderate).
- Whole foods outperform supplements for satiety per calorie because they add **water and volume** (file 05).

### 2.3 Fibre and the gut
- Higher fibre intake is associated with greater microbial diversity, but **probiotic supplements are not required** to achieve this (Low-Moderate; file 16).
- Fermentable fibres (inulin, resistant starch) can cause gas — dose gradually.

| Change | Expected effect |
|---|---|
| 15 → 30 g/day fibre | Better fullness; ~1–3 mmHg lower systolic BP; small LDL reduction; modest weight benefit |
| 5 g psyllium before the hungriest meal | Small reduction in intake; improved glycaemic response; better bowel regularity |
| Replacing refined starch with legumes at one meal/day | Higher satiety per calorie, ~5–8 g extra fibre |
| Glucomannan 3 g/day | <1 kg extra loss over 8 weeks; frequent bloating |

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

**Week 1:** add 1 portion of legumes or oats daily (+5–8 g fibre).
**Week 2:** add 300 g vegetables daily (+5–8 g).
**Week 3:** add 2 portions of fruit daily (+5–8 g).
**Week 4:** if still short, add **5 g psyllium** in a full glass of water before the hungriest meal.
**Throughout:** increase water intake; expect 1–2 weeks of mild gas.

That sequence costs almost nothing and captures the full health and satiety benefit.

## 7. Practical protocol

### A one-day high-fibre, high-protein, low-cost menu (~$4–7/day in most high-income markets)
- **Breakfast:** 80 g oats + 300 ml milk + 1 banana + cinnamon (~12 g fibre, ~22 g protein)
- **Lunch:** 200 g lentils/chickpeas + 200 g rice + 250 g frozen vegetables + olive oil (~14 g fibre, ~25 g protein)
- **Snack:** 200 g plain yoghurt + apple (~5 g fibre, ~12 g protein)
- **Dinner:** 3 eggs + 300 g cabbage/carrot stir-fry + 2 slices wholegrain bread (~10 g fibre, ~25 g protein)
- **Total:** ~41 g fibre, ~84 g protein — scale portions up for a larger man, and add a cheaper protein anchor (chicken thighs, canned fish, milk) to hit 150+ g protein.

### Fibre top-up rules
- **Psyllium:** 1 tsp–1 tbsp (≈3–5 g) in 300 ml water, once or twice daily, always with plenty of fluid.
- **Never take psyllium dry** or immediately before bed.
- Increase fibre **and** water together; otherwise constipation worsens.

---

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**Buy the same five things every week:** oats, frozen mixed vegetables, lentils (canned or dry), eggs, and a fruit. That combination alone gets most men from 15 g to 30+ g of fibre and covers cheap protein. Add psyllium only during hunger emergencies.

---

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Increase gradually** (≈5 g/week) — rapid increases cause gas, bloating, cramps.
- **Contraindicated/medical advice needed:** bowel obstruction or stricture, severe dysphagia, recent abdominal surgery, inflammatory bowel disease flare, gastroparesis.
- **Psyllium/glucomannan** can obstruct if taken with insufficient fluid; avoid with swallowing disorders.
- **Fibre supplements can reduce absorption of some medications** (e.g., levothyroxine, lithium, some antibiotics) — separate by 2–4 hours.
- **Iron/calcium/zinc:** high phytate intake can reduce mineral absorption; this is rarely clinically significant in mixed diets, but relevant for vegetarians — consider timing and vitamin C with meals.
- **Irritable bowel syndrome:** a general high-fibre increase can worsen symptoms; a clinician-guided approach (including low-FODMAP if appropriate) is needed.
- **Do not use fibre supplements as a meal replacement** or to compensate for purging/bingeing.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Buying a "fibre blend" powder** instead of psyllium or oats.
2. **Jumping from 10 g to 40 g in a week** — bloating, then abandonment.
3. **Taking psyllium with too little water** — constipation or choking risk.
4. **Assuming fibre supplements replace vegetables** — they don't provide volume, potassium or micronutrients.
5. **Ignoring fibre because it isn't "macros"** — it is the most reliable cheap satiety tool after protein.
6. **Buying premium "gut health" products** (probiotics, digestive enzymes) instead of eating 30 g of fibre.
7. **Not counting liquid fibre** — smoothies with fruit are fine but far less satisfying than whole fruit.

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. Get **25–40 g/day of fibre from food**; that alone is a meaningful health intervention.
2. Keep **psyllium powder** (not capsules) as the cheap supplement for hunger and regularity.
3. Build meals from **legumes, oats, frozen vegetables, cabbage/carrots, eggs and dairy** — cheap, high-fibre, high-protein.
4. Add fibre **slowly**, with water.
5. Skip glucomannan, fibre blends and "gut" supplements unless a clinician recommends them.
