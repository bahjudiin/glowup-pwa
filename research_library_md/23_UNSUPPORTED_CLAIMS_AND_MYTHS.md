# 23 — Unsupported Claims & Myths

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**. Every entry below is something a man is likely to be sold, told, or to believe. Verdicts are graded against the evidence reviewed in this library.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- Most money spent on fat loss, anti-aging and appearance buys **narrative, not effect**.
- The myths below survive because they are **mechanistically plausible, hard to falsify in a single person's experience, and profitable**.
- **The four questions** that kill 95% of bad claims: *(1) Is there a human trial with a clinical outcome? (2) What is the effect size in real units? (3) Who funded it? (4) Does the seller profit from my belief?*
- **What actually works is boring and cheap** — and it is also the only thing with consistent, replicated human evidence.

---

## 2. What the evidence establishes (myth-by-myth)

### Fat loss & metabolism

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence & explanation |
|---|---|---|
| "You're in starvation mode — your metabolism is broken" | **False as commonly stated** | Metabolic adaptation is real but modest and largely explained by a smaller body + less movement (Hall 2008; Leibel 1995). A true "broken metabolism" is a medical condition (hypothyroidism), not a plateau. **Grade: High** that adaptation is smaller than claimed |
| "Spot-reduce belly fat with crunches/wraps" | **False** | Fat is mobilised systemically; abdominal fat falls with an overall deficit. Waist trainers cause water loss and discomfort. **High** |
| "Keto/fasting gives a metabolic advantage" | **False when calories & protein are matched** | Meta-analyses show comparable loss (Ge 2020; Cioffi 2018; Gibson 2015). Keto can help adherence for some men. **High/Moderate** |
| "Fasted cardio burns more fat" | **Misleading** | Fat oxidation during exercise is higher fasted, but 24-h fat balance and total loss are equivalent. **Moderate** |
| "You must eat 6 small meals to 'stoke metabolism'" | **False** | Meal frequency has no meaningful effect when calories/protein are matched. **Moderate-High** |
| "Carbs make you fat" | **False** | Excess calories make you fat; carbs are not uniquely fattening (Ge 2020; Johnston 2014). **High** |
| "Eating after 8pm turns calories into fat" | **Mostly false** | Late eating is associated with higher total intake and worse glucose tolerance, not with a calorie multiplier. It matters because of *what and how much*, plus sleep. **Moderate** |
| "Sweating = burning fat" | **False** | Sweat is thermoregulation/water loss. **High** |
| "Muscle turns into fat when you stop training" | **False** | Different tissues; muscle atrophies while fat accumulates. **High** |
| "HIIT is superior for fat loss" | **Overstated** | Equal calories → equal loss; HIIT is time-efficient but harder to recover from in a deficit. **Moderate** |
| "You can out-train a bad diet" | **False in practice** | Exercise is easy to out-eat; diet drives weight change. **High** |
| "Detoxes, teas, and cleanses remove toxins and fat" | **False** | The liver and kidneys detoxify; these products cause water/electrolyte loss and GI distress. Some (senna-based) cause dependence. **High** that they don't work |
| "Apple cider vinegar / lemon water burns fat" | **Unsupported** | Small glycaemic effects; no meaningful weight effect. **Low/Insufficient** |
| "Cheat days reset your metabolism" | **False** | A high-calorie day can support adherence and glycogen, but doesn't "reset" metabolism. **Low** |
| "Fat burners / thermogenics work" | **No meaningful effect, real risk** | Caffeine has a trivial effect; stimulant blends carry cardiovascular and adulteration risk. **Moderate-High** against |
| "hCG injections melt fat" | **False** | The 500-kcal diet does the work; hCG adds nothing. **High** against |
| "You need 1 g of protein per pound — exactly" | **Not exactly** | 1.6–2.2 g/kg (≈0.7–1.0 g/lb) covers hypertrophy; deficits need more. **High** |
| "Protein damages healthy kidneys" | **False** | No evidence of harm in healthy kidneys; true in CKD. **Moderate-High** |
| "Creatine damages kidneys / causes hair loss / is a steroid" | **False** | Safe in healthy people; a 12-week RCT found no hair effect (Lak 2025); it is not hormonal. **High** |
| "You must eat protein within 30 minutes of training" | **Overstated** | Total daily protein dominates; the "anabolic window" is hours wide. **Moderate-High** |
| "BCAAs are essential for muscle" | **False** | Complete protein is superior; BCAAs are redundant. **High** |

### Fasting

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Autophagy starts at 16 hours in humans" | **Unsupported** — no validated human measurement or threshold; animal/cell data only. **Very low** |
| "Fasting triggers stem-cell regeneration in humans" | **Unsupported** — rodent extrapolation. **Very low** |
| "Fasting extends human lifespan" | **Unknown** — no human data. **Insufficient** |
| "Fasting preserves muscle better than dieting" | **False as a general rule** — matched protein/training matters more. **Moderate** |
| "You must fast 20+ hours to get benefits" | **False** — longer isn't better; adherence and total intake matter. **Moderate** |

### Supplements & longevity

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "NMN/NR reverses ageing" | **Unsupported** — raises NAD+, no human outcome data. **Insufficient** |
| "Resveratrol activates sirtuins and extends life" | **Unsupported in humans**; no benefit observed in cohort data. **Low** |
| "Metformin is a longevity drug for healthy people" | **Unsupported** — no completed trial shows lifespan benefit in non-diabetics; DPP benefit is in prediabetes. **Insufficient** |
| "Rapamycin will extend your lifespan" | **Unknown in humans**; immunosuppression risk. **Insufficient / ⛔** |
| "Taurine/spermidine/AKG are proven longevity compounds" | **Animal/marker data only. Insufficient** |
| "Testosterone boosters raise your T" | **False** for tribulus/fenugreek/DAA/ZMA in men with normal T. **High** against |
| "Testosterone is the reason you're fat and tired" | **Overstated** — obesity and sleep loss lower T; weight loss raises it (Ken-Dror 2024). Treat the cause first. **Moderate** |
| "Ashwagandha is a safe wonder-herb" | **Caution** — documented liver injury cases. **Low but real risk** |
| "Biotin grows hair/nails" | **Only in deficiency**; interferes with lab tests. **Moderate against** |
| "Collagen builds muscle / fixes wrinkles" | **Not for muscle** (incomplete protein); small skin effects at best. **Low-Moderate** |
| "Multivitamins make you live longer" | **False** — COSMOS and meta-analyses show no mortality/CVD/cancer benefit in well-fed adults. **High** against |
| "Vitamin D prevents cancer and heart disease" | **False in replete adults** — VITAL and D-Health were null. **High** against |
| "Omega-3 prevents heart disease in healthy people" | **False for primary prevention** (VITAL null); high-dose prescription EPA is a different story. **High** |
| "Biological age tests tell you how fast you're ageing" | **Not validated for individual decisions. Insufficient** |
| "IV vitamin drips / hyperbaric oxygen reverse ageing" | **No outcome data; risks and cost. Insufficient / ⛔** |

### Appearance & grooming

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Jawline exercisers/mewing reshape adult bone" | **False** — no evidence of adult skeletal change; jaw exercisers risk TMJ pain. **High** against |
| "Facial exercises restore youth" | **Weak** — one small study showed modest fullness change; no bone change. **Low** |
| "LED/red-light masks reverse skin ageing" | **Weak** — small, short, often industry-funded trials. **Low** |
| "Eye creams remove under-eye bags" | **No** — most bags are fat herniation; surgery is the fix. **Low/Insufficient** |
| "You can 'detox' your skin / purge toxins" | **False** — skin doesn't detoxify in that sense. **High** against |
| "Skin-lightening injections are safe" | **Dangerous** — unregulated glutathione/mercury products have caused harm. **⛔** |
| "Beard oil grows your beard" | **Unsupported** — improves feel/appearance only. **Insufficient** |
| "Posture correctors fix posture" | **False long-term** — strength training does. **Low** |
| "Sunscreen blocks vitamin D so you shouldn't wear it daily" | **Misleading** — normal exposure plus diet covers needs; sunscreen use does not cause deficiency in practice. **Moderate** |
| "Tooth whitening ruins enamel" | **Not with dentist-supervised or peroxide-based OTC products**; abrasive charcoal toothpastes are the actual problem. **Moderate** |

### Clothing, confidence and psychology

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "You must feel motivated before you start" | **Backwards** — action precedes motivation. **Moderate** (behavioural) |
| "Willpower is the main determinant" | **False** — environment and structure dominate. **Moderate-High** |
| "One slip ruins the diet" | **False** — the weekly average is what matters. **Moderate** |
| "You should never feel hungry when dieting" | **False but a warning sign** — some hunger is expected; severe, persistent hunger means the plan is wrong. **Moderate** |
| "Extreme leanness is the goal" | **Harmful** — below ~8–10% body fat, hormonal/sleep/libido/immune costs rise. **Moderate** |

## 3. Evidence quality

The verdicts above are graded individually. For the "false" verdicts, most rest on **High** evidence (meta-analyses or large trials). The "unsupported/insufficient" verdicts are the honest category of *we don't know* — they are not claims of harm, only of absent human outcome evidence.

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

Replace every myth-based purchase with the **short list that actually works**: protein, fibre, a calorie deficit, resistance training, walking, sleep, sunscreen, a retinoid, creatine, and — if deficient — vitamin D. Files 02–14.

---

## 6. Free and cheap alternatives

Free myth-checking: search the claim on **PubMed** and read whether the top result is a *trial* or a *review*, and whether the participants were humans. Two minutes replaces most paid advice.

---

## 7. Practical protocol

Before buying anything: state the claim in one sentence, find one human RCT or meta-analysis that tests the *outcome you want*, and check the dose used vs the dose in the product. If you can't find one, don't buy it.

---

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**One rule:** if a product's marketing depends on a mechanism you can't verify in a human trial, it is a story, not a treatment.

---

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Some myths are dangerous, not just wasteful:** skin-lightening injections, unregulated GLP-1s, DNP, stimulant fat burners, "detox" laxatives, and self-prescribed hormones have caused deaths and serious harm.
- **Some myths mask treatable disease:** "cortisol belly", "hormone imbalance", and "broken metabolism" can delay diagnosis of hypothyroidism, Cushing's syndrome, sleep apnoea, or depression.
- **Some myths promote disordered eating:** "hunger is weakness", "cheat days", "detox after overeating". If food rules are causing distress, seek help.

---

## 10. Products and tools

**Never buy:** fat burners, testosterone boosters, BCAAs, glutamine, HMB, detox teas/cleanses, hCG, "fat cavitation", body wraps, waist trainers, jawline exercisers, posture correctors, LED masks, microcurrent devices, NAD precursors, resveratrol, spermidine, collagen (for muscle), "greens" powders, biological-age tests, IV drips, skin-lightening injections, unregulated peptides/SARMs.

**Do buy (if anything):** file 18's short list.

---

## 11. Common mistakes

1. Arguing about diet composition while never creating a deficit.
2. Buying the "next thing" before fixing sleep, protein and training.
3. Confusing "felt better" with "worked" (many interventions have placebo effects).
4. Reading only the headline of a study, not the population or dose.
5. Believing animal studies transfer to humans by default.
6. Trusting anyone who earns money when you believe them.
7. Using "detox" logic to recover from overeating.
8. Chasing anabolic shortcuts (steroids/SARMs) and paying with health.
9. Treating social media physiques as the reference standard.
10. Letting a myth become an identity ("I'm keto", "I'm a faster") — identity makes correction impossible.

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Adopt the four-question filter** and apply it to everything.
2. **Spend nothing on the "never buy" list.**
3. **Put the money into food, shoes, a gym, and a food scale.**
4. **Re-read this file** whenever a new product seems compelling.
5. **Accept the boring answer:** deficit, protein, lifting, walking, sleep, sunscreen, patience.
