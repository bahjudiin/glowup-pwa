# 13 — Anti-Aging & Longevity Interventions (Graded)

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**. Every popular "longevity" product/intervention is graded here: evidence, risk, cost, and verdict.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **There is no supplement, device or drug with human evidence that it extends lifespan in healthy people.** Everything sold as "anti-aging" either (a) changes a biomarker, (b) extends lifespan in rodents, or (c) is pure marketing.
- The interventions with **real human outcome evidence** for living longer/healthier are behavioural and free: **not smoking, fitness, strength training, blood-pressure control, fibre/Mediterranean-style diet, sleep, limited alcohol, sun protection, vaccines/screening** (file 12).
- Among *purchasable* items, only a small number are worth money — and only for specific, non-longevity reasons: **creatine** (strength/lean mass), **vitamin D** (if deficient), **omega-3** (if you don't eat fish), **fibre/psyllium** (satiety/lipids), **sunscreen + retinoid** (appearance; file 14).
- **The expensive "longevity stack" (NMN/NR, resveratrol, spermidine, metformin, rapamycin, taurine, AKG, spermidine, exosomes, peptides)** ranges from "biomarker change with unknown value" to "documented harm risk". None is recommended in this library.
- **Distinguish four different questions:** Does it change a biomarker? Does it work in mice? Does it improve a human clinical outcome? Does it extend human lifespan? Almost all products pass only the first two.

## 3. Evidence quality

- **High:** that smoking cessation, fitness, BP control, activity and diet pattern affect mortality.
- **Moderate:** that vitamin D, omega-3 and metformin help *specific populations* (deficient, hypertriglyceridaemic, prediabetic).
- **Low-Moderate:** magnesium, multivitamin, collagen, berberine, testosterone (for symptoms), sauna, cold exposure.
- **Insufficient:** NMN/NR, resveratrol, spermidine, taurine, glycine, rapamycin for human lifespan, HBOT, plasma/exosomes, biological-age tests.

## 4. Effectiveness (what you can realistically expect)

| Category | Realistic benefit |
|---|---|
| Creatine + resistance training | +1–3 kg lean mass, +10–30% strength over 3–6 months |
| Vitamin D if deficient | Correction of deficiency; improved bone/immune function |
| Omega-3 if low fish intake | ~20–30% lower triglycerides; small BP reduction |
| Fibre/psyllium | Better satiety, lipids, glycaemia |
| Metformin (prediabetes) | ~31% lower progression to diabetes |
| NMN/NR | NAD+ up ~50–60%; **zero demonstrated clinical benefit** |
| Rapamycin, HBOT, plasma, exosomes | No demonstrated benefit; documented risks |

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

**If you want to "do anti-aging" properly and cheaply, do it in this order:**
1. **Behaviour first** (file 12): smoking, BP, fitness, waist, sleep, alcohol, diet, sunscreen.
2. **Measure first:** blood pressure, waist, HbA1c/glucose, lipids, vitamin D (if low sun exposure), ferritin/B12 if symptomatic.
3. **Fix deficiencies only** (vitamin D, iron, B12) — that is what supplements are for.
4. **Add only these purchases** if they fit your goals: creatine (performance), psyllium (satiety/lipids), fish or fish oil (if no fish), sunscreen + retinoid (appearance).
5. **Skip everything else** in the table above until there is human outcome evidence. Revisit in 5 years.

## 6. Free and cheap alternatives to expensive longevity protocols

| Expensive protocol | Claim | Free/cheap reality |
|---|---|---|
| NAD+ IV drips ($300–1,000) | "Reverses ageing" | No outcome evidence; NMN/NR raise NAD+ far cheaper and still with no outcome evidence — so buy **neither** |
| Longevity clinic "protocol" ($3,000–20,000/yr) | Personalised optimisation | A BP cuff, a blood panel, a gym, and file 12 cover the actionable elements |
| Metformin off-label | "Longevity drug" | DPP benefits are for prediabetes; lifestyle outperformed metformin for diabetes prevention (58% vs 31%) |
| Rapamycin | "mTOR aging" | No human mortality data; immunosuppression risk |
| HBOT chamber | "Telomere lengthening" | Surrogate endpoint; unvalidated; expensive; small risk |
| Cold plunge + sauna studio membership | "Hormesis" | A cold shower and a hot bath are free versions of the same unproven idea |
| Peptide stacks (BPC-157, TB-500, "GH secretagogues") | Recovery/anti-aging | Unapproved, unregulated, contamination risk, no human outcome data |

**The metabolically honest statement:** lifestyle interventions are not "as good as" a longevity drug — there *is* no validated longevity drug. Lifestyle is the only category with human outcome evidence, and it happens to be free.

## 7. Practical protocol (if you want a rational stack)

**Tier 1 — mandatory, free:** file 12 behaviours.
**Tier 2 — cheap, evidence-supported:**
- Creatine monohydrate **3–5 g/day**, any time (no loading needed).
- Vitamin D3 **1,000–2,000 IU/day** if limited sun exposure or documented low level; recheck after 3 months.
- Psyllium **3–5 g/day** if fibre intake <25 g/day.
- Fish 1–2 servings/week **or** 1–2 g/day combined EPA+DHA if you don't eat fish.
**Tier 3 — optional, low priority:** magnesium glycinate 200–400 mg (if diet poor/cramps), multivitamin.
**Tier 4 — do not buy:** NMN/NR, resveratrol, spermidine, taurine, berberine (unless advised), rapamycin, peptides, HBOT, exosomes, "biological age" tests.

**Before starting anything in Tier 2:** check for interactions if you take medications (pharmacist is free). Do not combine high-dose fish oil with anticoagulants without advice.

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**Creatine 5 g + vitamin D 2,000 IU (if low sun) + fish twice a week.** Total cost typically under $10–15/month. Everything else in the longevity market is optional or unproven.

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Creatine:** safe in healthy people; ensure hydration; caution in kidney disease (discuss with clinician); not a cause of hair loss in a 12-week RCT (Lak 2025).
- **Vitamin D:** do not exceed ~4,000 IU/day long-term without monitoring; toxicity causes hypercalcaemia and kidney stones.
- **Omega-3:** high doses increase bleeding risk and atrial fibrillation risk; stop before surgery as advised.
- **Berberine:** inhibits CYP enzymes — interacts with many drugs (statins, immunosuppressants, diabetes drugs). Avoid in pregnancy.
- **Metformin:** contraindicated in significant kidney impairment; causes B12 deficiency long-term; not for self-prescribed "longevity".
- **Rapamycin/everolimus, GH, peptides, TRT:** prescription-only categories with real harms. Do not obtain from unregulated sources.
- **Cold exposure:** avoid with uncontrolled hypertension, arrhythmia, cardiovascular disease; never alone in open water.
- **Anything injected** (IV drips, "stem cells", exosomes) purchased outside a regulated medical setting carries infection and contamination risk.
- **Ashwagandha:** documented cases of drug-induced liver injury — avoid or monitor (file 23).

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Buying the stack before fixing sleep/fitness/waist.**
2. **Confusing biomarker change with health benefit** (NAD+, telomeres, IGF-1, "autophagy score").
3. **Extrapolating mouse lifespan data to yourself** (rapamycin, taurine, 17-α-estradiol, AKG).
4. **Taking metformin without prediabetes** and expecting longevity.
5. **Using growth hormone or peptides** for body composition — harms exceed any benefit.
6. **Paying for hyperbaric oxygen or "biological age" testing.**
7. **Stacking many supplements with overlapping/unknown interactions.**
8. **Ignoring the boring inputs because they are not sold to you** — the free behaviours dominate.
9. **Believing "natural" means safe** (ashwagandha hepatotoxicity, berberine interactions, high-dose vitamin D toxicity).
10. **Not telling your doctor what you take** — supplements interfere with lab tests (biotin is a classic).

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **There is no validated longevity pill.** Act accordingly.
2. Spend on: **creatine, vitamin D if low, fish/fish oil if no fish, psyllium, sunscreen, retinoid, a BP monitor, shoes.**
3. Reject: **NAD precursors, resveratrol, spermidine, rapamycin, GH/peptides, HBOT, exosomes, biological-age tests, IV drips.**
4. Use metformin only for a diagnosis, with a clinician.
5. Re-run the "four questions" (§1) on every new longevity product for the rest of your life.
