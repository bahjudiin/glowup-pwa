# 10 — Exercise & NEAT

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Exercise is the single best-evidenced intervention in this entire library for living longer and better** — better than any supplement, and comparable to or better than many medications for cardiovascular risk (High).
- For **fat loss**, exercise alone is a weak tool (it is easy to out-eat); for **maintaining fat loss** and **preserving muscle**, it is close to essential (Moderate-High).
- **Three components, ranked by value per minute:**
  1. **Resistance training 2–4×/week** — muscle, strength, bone, glucose control, mortality.
  2. **Walking / daily step volume (8,000–12,000 steps)** — the cheapest way to raise energy expenditure and the easiest habit to keep.
  3. **Vigorous cardio 1–2×/week (or 75–150 min/week)** — cardiorespiratory fitness, the strongest single predictor of mortality in cohort data.
- **Cardiorespiratory fitness (VO₂max) is one of the strongest known predictors of all-cause mortality**: each 1-MET increase in fitness ≈ **13% lower all-cause mortality** (Kodama 2009 — High), and the least-fit group had a far higher mortality risk than the most-fit (Mandsager 2018 — High).
- **Muscle-strengthening activity for just 30–60 min/week is associated with ~10–17% lower all-cause mortality** (Momma 2022 — Moderate-High), with a J-shaped curve — more is not linearly better.
- **NEAT** (non-exercise activity thermogenesis — fidgeting, standing, walking, chores) is the most variable component of daily energy expenditure and the easiest to increase by rule ("10,000 steps", "stand for calls", "stairs").
- Exercise programmes produce modest weight loss (~2–3 kg) but large improvements in **visceral fat, blood pressure, insulin sensitivity, lipids, mood, sleep and fitness** — which is why "exercise for health, diet for weight" is the correct framing.

## 2. What the evidence establishes

### 2.1 Mortality and fitness
- Meta-analysis of 33 cohort studies: each **1-MET** higher maximal aerobic capacity → **13% lower all-cause mortality** and **15% lower CVD mortality** (Kodama 2009, JAMA — High).
- In 122,007 patients undergoing treadmill testing, **elite fitness** carried a far lower mortality risk than low fitness, with no upper harm threshold; the effect size rivalled or exceeded traditional risk factors (Mandsager 2018, JAMA Netw Open — High).
- **Steps:** meta-analysis of 15 cohorts — mortality risk decreases up to **~8,000–10,000 steps/day** for adults under 60 and **~6,000–8,000** for adults 60+, then plateaus (Paluch 2022, Lancet Public Health — High).
- **Resistance training** for 30–60 min/week is associated with **10–17% lower all-cause mortality** and lower CVD/cancer mortality; higher volumes show a J-shape (Momma 2022, BJSM — Moderate-High; Shailendra 2022 — Moderate).
- **Sedentary time** is independently associated with higher mortality, partly offset by activity (Rahmati 2025 umbrella review — Moderate; Shivgulam 2026 — Moderate).

### 2.2 Guidelines
- WHO 2020: **150–300 min/week of moderate** or 75–150 min/week of vigorous aerobic activity, **plus 2+ days/week of muscle-strengthening**, plus reduced sedentary time (Bull 2020 — High, guideline).
- ACSM position stand for weight control: **150–250 min/week** of moderate activity for weight loss prevention of regain; **200–300+ min/week** for weight maintenance after loss (Donnelly 2009 — Moderate).

### 2.3 Exercise and body composition
- Diet vs exercise-induced weight loss in men: similar total weight loss, but **exercise produced greater visceral fat reduction and better insulin sensitivity** (Ross 2000, Ann Intern Med — Moderate).
- Resistance training during a deficit **preserves lean mass** (Sardeli 2018; Weinheimer 2010 — Moderate).
- **NEAT** varies by up to ~2,000 kcal/day between individuals and changes with overfeeding/underfeeding (Levine 1999, Science — Low-Moderate). It is not fully volitional, but you can raise its floor.

### 2.4 Blood pressure and cardiometabolic health
- Network meta-analysis of 270 RCTs: exercise training lowers resting BP by ~**4–5 mmHg systolic** on average, with **isometric exercise** and combined training among the most effective modes (Edwards 2023, BJSM — High).
- Exercise improves insulin sensitivity, HbA1c, triglycerides and HDL independently of weight loss (High).

### 2.5 What exercise does *not* do well
- Produce large weight loss on its own (compensatory intake and reduced NEAT blunt it; Moderate).
- "Spot-reduce" fat (no).
- Replace a bad diet (no).
- Prevent regain without dietary structure (no) — but it is strongly associated with successful maintenance (Moderate).

## 4. Effectiveness (expected magnitude)

| Intervention | Expected effect |
|---|---|
| 150 min/week moderate cardio | ~2–3 kg weight loss over 6 months (alone); −5 to −8 mmHg systolic BP in hypertensives; large fitness gains |
| 2–3 resistance sessions/week | +1–3 kg lean mass over 3–6 months in novices; +20–40% strength; better glucose control |
| 8,000–10,000 steps/day | Substantially lower mortality risk; ~200–400 kcal/day expenditure |
| Isometric wall-sit/handgrip training | ~5–8 mmHg systolic BP reduction (Moderate) |
| 300 min/week + diet after weight loss | Best-supported regain prevention |

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

**The minimum effective exercise programme (30–45 min, 3×/week, plus walking):**

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon | Full-body strength: squat, press, row, hinge, core (3 sets each, 6–10 reps) |
| Tue | 30–45 min brisk walk (or 20 min intervals) |
| Wed | Rest / walk |
| Thu | Full-body strength (different movement variations) |
| Fri | 30–45 min brisk walk |
| Sat | Optional: sport, hike, or a third strength session |
| Sun | Rest / walk |

Plus: **8,000–12,000 steps daily**, stairs instead of lifts, standing for phone calls.

**Progression:** add load or reps weekly on the main lifts; add 10 min/week to walking until you hit the step target.

**Limitations/risks:** injury from sudden volume jumps; overtraining when combined with an aggressive deficit; cardio interfering with hypertrophy if volume is extreme; time cost (the main real barrier).

## 7. Practical protocol

### Beginner progression (first 8 weeks)
- **Weeks 1–2:** 2 strength sessions/week, 2 sets per movement, light loads; 20 min walk daily.
- **Weeks 3–4:** 3 sessions/week, 3 sets; 30 min walk daily.
- **Weeks 5–8:** 3 sessions, 3–4 sets, add load weekly; 8,000 steps/day.

### Strength template (movement patterns, not exercises)
1. **Squat** (back squat, front squat, goblet, leg press)
2. **Hinge** (deadlift, RDL, hip thrust, kettlebell swing)
3. **Horizontal push** (bench, push-up, dumbbell press)
4. **Horizontal pull** (row — barbell, dumbbell, cable, inverted)
5. **Vertical push** (overhead press)
6. **Vertical pull** (pull-up, lat pulldown)
7. **Carry/core** (farmer carry, plank, hanging leg raise)

Choose 5 per session; alternate A/B. 3 sets × 6–10 reps, 1–3 reps in reserve.

### Cardio prescriptions
- **Base:** 150–300 min/week brisk walking (zone 2-ish, conversational).
- **Vigorous:** 1×/week 4×4 min hard / 3 min easy intervals (or 20 min of hard cycling).
- **Blood-pressure mode:** 3×/week isometric wall sits (4 × 2 min) or handgrip training (Moderate-High evidence).
- **Interference rule:** keep hard cardio away from leg days if hypertrophy is a priority.

### NEAT rules (no willpower required)
- 10,000 steps before dinner or it doesn't count.
- Stand/walk during phone calls.
- Park at the far end; stairs always; one walking meeting per day.
- A 10-minute post-meal walk (also blunts glucose response).

---

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**Two strength sessions + daily walking.** That is 90% of the mortality, strength and muscle-retention benefit at ~90 minutes/week. Add one brisk 30-min walk a day; everything else is optional.

---

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Get clearance** before vigorous exercise if you have known heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension, arrhythmia, or symptoms (chest pain, fainting, unusual breathlessness).
- **Start below your capacity.** Most injuries come from week-1 enthusiasm, not from the programme.
- **Joint pain:** modify range/load; sharp pain = stop and assess.
- **Hypertension:** avoid maximal Valsalva straining and heavy overhead work until BP is controlled.
- **Hernia, recent surgery, pregnancy (household), osteoporosis:** individualised guidance.
- **Heat/hydration:** in hot climates (e.g., coastal East Africa), train early or late, drink water, and watch for heat exhaustion.
- **Do not use stimulants to power training** if you have cardiovascular risk factors.
- **Overtraining signs:** persistent fatigue, rising resting HR, sleep disruption, performance decline → deload a week.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Doing only cardio** — loses weight, loses muscle, no strength benefit.
2. **Doing only lifting with no steps/walking** — misses the easiest energy expenditure lever.
3. **Programme-hopping every 3 weeks** — no progression, no results.
4. **Training to failure every session** — recovery cost > benefit, especially in a deficit.
5. **Adding exercise to "earn" food** — a fast route to disordered eating and compensatory overeating.
6. **Ignoring steps because "I go to the gym"** — a sedentary 20 h/day outweighs a 45-min session.
7. **Chasing soreness as proof** — soreness ≠ stimulus.
8. **Buying gadgets instead of walking** — the watch doesn't move your legs.
9. **Sudden volume spikes** — the main cause of injury.
10. **Not doing any vigorous cardio ever** — VO₂max is the mortality predictor you can actually move.

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Lift 2–4×/week, progressively, forever.**
2. **Walk 8,000–12,000 steps/day.**
3. **Add 1–2 vigorous cardio sessions/week (or 75–150 min/week).**
4. Prefer **the cheapest equipment you will actually use**.
5. Judge progress by **strength numbers and step average**, not by the scale alone.
6. Treat exercise as **the health intervention**, and the diet as **the weight intervention**.
