# 08 — Plateaus and Weight Maintenance

*Phase 2 module, file 8 of 12. Muscle-loss checks in `06` §8; overall system in `10`.*

---

## 1. Purpose

Two problems end most fat-loss attempts: **a two-week stall is mistaken for failure**, and **the loss is regained within a year**. This file separates real plateaus from noise, explains what actually changes as you get lighter, and gives an intentionally *low-effort* maintenance system — one designed to survive a normal life rather than to require daily obsession.

---

## 2. Is it a plateau, or is it noise?

### 2.1 The maths of scale noise
Daily weight swings of **0.5–2 kg** come from water, glycogen, sodium, stool and food in transit (`07` §5). Fat loss at a realistic rate is **0.3–1 kg/week**, i.e. **40–140 g/day** — an order of magnitude smaller than the noise. Any judgement based on fewer than 10–14 days of data is mostly measuring water.

### 2.2 A working definition
> **A genuine plateau = no downward trend in the 7-day average weight AND no reduction in waist circumference over 3 consecutive weeks, while adherence was actually maintained.**

### 2.3 Diagnostic checklist before changing anything

| Question | If yes | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Has waist circumference changed in 3 weeks? | Waist shrinking while weight is flat = **recomposition**, not a plateau | Change nothing |
| Has strength/clothing fit improved? | Same as above | Change nothing |
| Has adherence actually been consistent (protein, portions, no unlogged oil/snacks/liquids)? | Often the real answer is **"no"** | Fix adherence for 2 weeks first |
| Did you recently start harder training or increase salt/carbs? | Water retention masking fat loss | Wait 7–10 days |
| Are you sleeping < 6 h or very stressed? | Cortisol/water + intake increase | Fix sleep first (`05`) |
| Have you lost > 5–8% of bodyweight already? | Real reduction in expenditure | Recalculate (`08` §3.4) |

**The most common real cause of a "plateau" is unmeasured intake** — oil, bread, tea with sugar, juice, "tastes" while cooking, portions that grew gradually. A one-week honest food log (not a permanent habit) usually resolves the question.

## 3. What actually changes as you lose weight

### 3.1 You are a smaller body
Energy expenditure falls simply because a lighter body needs less energy — in fat mass and in lean mass. This is **not** "damage": if you lose 10 kg, your maintenance intake drops roughly in proportion. Expect ~15–25% lower maintenance after a substantial loss (approximate; individual variation is wide).

### 3.2 Adaptive thermogenesis (metabolic adaptation beyond size)
- A systematic review found that **adaptive thermogenesis does occur after weight loss** in adults — i.e. measured resting expenditure falls *more* than predicted from the loss of mass alone — but that the magnitude varies widely between studies and individuals ([Nunes 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33762040/)).
- The most striking long-term data come from "The Biggest Loser" cohort: **persistent metabolic adaptation 6 years after** the competition ([Fothergill 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27136388/)). This is a small, extreme, non-representative sample — a real phenomenon, not a template for what happens to someone losing 8 kg.
- **Honest framing:** adaptation is real, usually modest (tens to a couple of hundred kcal/day), partly reversible with adequate intake and training, and **never large enough to explain a total stall on its own.**

### 3.3 Spontaneous activity falls
As you diet, you fidget less, walk less, stand less. This can quietly remove 100–300 kcal/day of expenditure. **The counter is deliberate steps** (7–10k/day), not more cardio.

### 3.4 Hunger rises and stays raised
After ~10% weight loss, ghrelin rises and leptin falls, and hunger remains elevated at 1 year ([Sumithran 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22029981/)). **This is the main reason maintenance is harder than loss**, and it is physiology, not weakness. The available responses are: high protein, high volume, adequate sleep, and (for some) structure such as fasting windows or meal timing.

### 3.5 Water and cycle fluctuation
A genuinely flat 2–3 weeks can be water. Women's cyclical water shifts are predictable and should be anticipated, not reacted to.

### 3.6 "Starvation mode" — what is and isn't true
| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Your metabolism shuts down and you stop losing fat" | **False.** Expenditure falls modestly, but a deficit still causes fat loss. |
| "Adaptive thermogenesis exists" | **True**, magnitude variable ([Nunes 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33762040/)). |
| "You must do a reverse diet to fix your metabolism" | **Unsupported as a requirement.** Slowly raising calories to maintenance is sensible; the elaborate "reverse diet" protocol has no RCT support as a fat-loss tool. |
| "Diet breaks accelerate fat loss" | **Mixed.** MATADOR showed intermittent energy restriction improved loss *efficiency* ([Byrne 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28925405/)), and rationale reviews support the concept ([Sainsbury 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30511512/)), but this is not consistently reproduced. Diet breaks are best justified as **adherence tools**, not metabolic ones. |
| "Metabolic damage is permanent" | **False.** |

## 4. Interventions if it is a real plateau (in order)

1. **Verify adherence for 14 days** — weigh oil, count bread, no sugar in tea, log drinks. (Most plateaus end here.)
2. **Recalculate maintenance for your new weight** and reduce intake by **~10–15%**, not by 500 kcal.
3. **Add 1,500–2,500 steps/day** rather than adding cardio sessions.
4. **Check protein and sleep** — both are usually the broken parts (`06`, `05`).
5. **Only then** consider structure changes: fewer meals, a narrower eating window, or a 1–2 week diet break at maintenance.
6. **Do not** respond by slashing calories to a very low intake. Extreme restriction accelerates lean-mass loss and binge risk, and it is not a plan you can maintain.

**How long before changing the plan?** At least **3 weeks** of confirmed adherence with no waist or weight trend. Changing the plan every 10 days guarantees you never learn what works.

## 5. Calorie cycling, diet breaks, refeeds and reverse dieting — honest verdicts

| Strategy | What it is | Evidence verdict | Who might benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diet break** (1–2 weeks at maintenance every 8–12 weeks) | Deliberate pause in the deficit | **Plausible adherence benefit**; small metabolic rationale; MATADOR's intermittent approach improved efficiency ([Byrne 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28925405/)) but replication is limited | People with diet fatigue, poor sleep, or escalating hunger |
| **Intermittent energy restriction (5:2 / ADF, weekly structure)** | Big deficit on some days, maintenance on others | Comparable results to continuous restriction; may suit "all-or-nothing" personalities | People who prefer clear rules |
| **Refeed day** (1 day at maintenance with more carbs) | Short break | **Weak evidence**; mostly psychological and glycogen/water (which then confuses the scale) | Athletes before competition; otherwise optional |
| **Calorie cycling** (different intakes on different days) | Spreads the same weekly average | **No advantage** when weekly intake is matched | People who want a big evening meal some days |
| **Reverse dieting** (gradually raising calories by 50–100 kcal/week) | Slow return to maintenance | **No RCT support as a distinct method.** The sensible version — return to maintenance gradually rather than jumping — is just good practice | Anyone finishing a diet |
| **"Metabolism reset" programmes** | Multi-month, very low then high | **Marketing.** No evidence. | Nobody |

**The one thing that matters:** the *weekly average intake* and whether you can keep doing it. Everything above is a tool for adherence, not a metabolic trick.

## 6. Maintenance: why regain happens and what actually prevents it

### 6.1 The evidence on regain
- In Look AHEAD, intensive lifestyle intervention produced **−4.7% vs −2.1%** at 8 years — i.e. substantial regain occurred even in the best-resourced programme, and yet the intervention group was clearly better off ([Look AHEAD 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24307184/)).
- With medication, stopping treatment causes rapid regain: in SURMOUNT-4, participants who stopped tirzepatide regained most of the loss while the continuing group kept losing ([SURMOUNT-4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38078870/)). This is biology, not "the drug stopped working."
- Mechanisms of regain include persisting reductions in leptin/energy expenditure and increased ghrelin ([Sumithran 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22029981/)), plus behavioural relaxation; recent reviews synthesise predictors and mechanisms ([Tejera 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42547731/)).

### 6.2 What predicts better maintenance
Consistently in the literature: **continued self-monitoring (weighing), continued physical activity, continued high protein intake, and a defined maintenance plan** — not "going back to normal." The people who maintain are the people who keep doing *some* version of the behaviour indefinitely.

### 6.3 The low-effort maintenance system (15 minutes/week)
1. **Weigh daily (or ≥ 4×/week), use the weekly average.** One number per week.
2. **Waist weekly**, same spot, same time.
3. **Maintenance band:** define a **2 kg band** around your maintenance weight (e.g. 82–84 kg). Inside the band: **do nothing different.**
4. **Trigger rule:** 2 consecutive weekly averages above the band → return to a modest deficit (10% below maintenance) for 2–4 weeks. No drama, no shame, no "restart."
5. **Protein anchor:** keep the protein target you used while losing (110–140 g/day). The single most protective habit.
6. **Steps:** keep 7–10k/day.
7. **Two resistance sessions per week** — non-negotiable for keeping the body composition you paid for.
8. **Monthly review, not daily:** one page: weekly averages, waist, sessions completed, sleep. If two of those slipped, fix those two.

### 6.4 What to avoid in maintenance
- **Obsessive daily monitoring with emotional reactions** — weighs the same information but destroys adherence.
- **"I'll be good all week and relax at weekends"** — a 2-day weekend surplus can erase a 5-day deficit.
- **Dropping structure entirely** at goal weight ("I'm done") — the plan after the diet is the difference between results and a story.
- **Long-term very-low-calorie eating** to keep a weight that your habits do not support.

## 7. Summary

### 7.1 Strongest verified findings
1. **Adaptive thermogenesis is real but variable and modest**, and rarely sufficient to explain a stall on its own ([Nunes 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33762040/)); extreme cases show persistence at 6 years ([Fothergill 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27136388/)).
2. **Hunger hormones remain unfavourable after weight loss at 1 year** ([Sumithran 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22029981/)).
3. **Regain is the default without continued structure**: −4.7% vs −2.1% at 8 years in Look AHEAD ([24307184](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24307184/)); rapid regain on stopping medication ([SURMOUNT-4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38078870/)).
4. **Intermittent energy restriction can improve loss efficiency** in at least one RCT ([MATADOR](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28925405/)) — a legitimate adherence tool, not a metabolic necessity.
5. **"Metabolic damage"/"starvation mode" as popularly described is not supported.**

### 7.2 Most practical actions
Define a plateau properly (3 weeks + waist + adherence); verify intake before changing anything; recalculate maintenance at your new weight; use a 2 kg maintenance band with a trigger rule; keep protein, steps and strength training forever; review monthly, not daily.

### 7.3 Cheapest effective options
Weekly average weighing, a tape measure, a step target, and two bodyweight sessions per week. Total cost: zero.

### 7.4 What is unnecessary
Reverse-diet protocols, "metabolism reset" programmes, expensive body-composition scans every week, continuous glucose monitors for fat loss in non-diabetics, daily calorie apps forever, and refeed days as a metabolic strategy.

### 7.5 What is unsupported or misleading
- "You've stalled because your metabolism is damaged" — usually intake or noise.
- "You must eat more to lose more" / "reverse dieting fixes your metabolism" — no RCT support.
- "Diet breaks accelerate fat loss" — modest and inconsistent evidence; frame them as adherence tools.
- "Once you reach your goal you can eat normally again" — the habits, not the diet, are what you keep.
- Any specific claim that a stall means "your body is holding on to fat."

### 7.6 Important risks
Slashing calories in response to a stall (lean-mass loss, binge risk); over-monitoring (anxiety, disordered eating); abandoning all structure at goal weight; using stimulants or "fat burners" to break a plateau (**do not**); interpreting a water fluctuation as failure and quitting.

### 7.7 Concise action plan
1. Start a **7-day rolling average** today, and read only that.
2. Write down your **maintenance band** (±2 kg) and your **trigger rule**.
3. If weight is flat for 3 weeks with a flat waist and genuine adherence: cut **10%** of intake, add **2,000 steps**.
4. Schedule a **1-week diet break at maintenance** every 8–12 weeks if hunger or fatigue is climbing.
5. At goal: keep protein, steps, and 2 weekly training sessions — **permanently**.
6. Review monthly on one page. Ignore daily numbers otherwise.
