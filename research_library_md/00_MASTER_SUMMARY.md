## 1. Executive summary

If you read nothing else, read this page.

**The hierarchy of what actually works** (by evidence quality × effect size × cost):

1. **A sustained calorie deficit with high protein** — the only mechanism of fat loss.
2. **Resistance training** — the only reliable way to keep muscle while losing fat, and one of the strongest predictors of living longer.
3. **Walking / daily step volume** — the cheapest energy-expenditure and mortality lever that exists.
4. **Sleep 7–9 h** — reduces spontaneous intake by ~270 kcal/day and changes *what* you lose (fat vs muscle).
5. **Fibre + food volume + no liquid calories** — the cheap, safe appetite-control stack.
6. **Sunscreen + a retinoid** — the only two skincare items with strong evidence.
7. **Creatine** — the only supplement worth defaulting to.
8. **Self-weighing + a written 2-kg correction rule** — the single most powerful maintenance habit.

**The honest numbers:** lifestyle achieves **5–8%** weight loss sustained with effort; semaglutide achieves **~15%**, tirzepatide **~21%**, surgery **25–30%** — and all of them are followed by regain when stopped unless a maintenance system exists. **Nothing cheap replaces a GLP-1 drug; nothing expensive replaces protein, training, sleep and maintenance behaviour.**

**The biggest waste of money in this field:** fat burners, testosterone boosters, NAD precursors, resveratrol, LED masks, jaw exercisers, "detox" products, hCG, biological-age tests, and unregulated peptides. A typical man spends **$1,000–4,000/year** on these for approximately zero benefit.

**The single most important insight in the whole library:** losing weight is a project; **keeping it off is a permanent, low-effort background process** — a weight range, a weekly weigh-in, a written correction rule, protein, two lifting sessions a week, and a daily walk.

## 2. What the evidence establishes — answers to the twelve master questions

### 2.1 What are the most effective methods for rapid fat loss?
A **20–25% energy deficit**, **protein 2.0–2.6 g/kg/day**, **3–4 resistance sessions/week**, **10,000–12,000 steps/day**, **7–9 h sleep**, and a **repeated meal structure**. Expected rate: **0.5–1.0% of body weight per week** — faster than that costs muscle, sleep and adherence (Garthe 2011; Hall 2008; Longland 2016). Diet *composition* matters far less than the deficit and adherence (Ge 2020; Johnston 2014). **Files 02, 20.**

### 2.2 What is the best-supported approach to preserve muscle?
**Resistance training + high protein + a moderate (not extreme) deficit + adequate sleep.** Protein at **2.2–2.6 g/kg** in a deficit (or 2.3–3.1 g/kg fat-free mass in lean men); resistance training 2–4×/week with heavy loads; keep the deficit at 15–25%; sleep 7–9 h (muscle-loss risk rises sharply when sleep is short). **Files 03, 08, 11.**

### 2.3 Which fasting schedule is most practical and effective?
**16:8 with the eating window ending 2–3 h before bed** (or **14:10** if 16:8 hurts protein intake, training or sleep). Fasting is **not superior** to calorie restriction when protein and calories are matched (Lowe 2020; Cioffi 2018); and the largest 12-month trial found only a non-significant 1.8 kg difference favouring TRE (Liu 2022, P=0.11) and improves adherence for men who dislike breakfast. **Early TRE (8am–4pm)** has the best metabolic signals and the worst social fit. **20:4 and alternate-day fasting** are harder and no better. **Autophagy-hour claims have no human evidence.** **File 04.**

### 2.4 Which methods reduce appetite most reliably?
Ranked: **protein (biggest)** → **fibre/viscous fibre (psyllium)** → **food volume & low energy density** → **sleep** → **no liquid calories** → **eating slowly** → **water before meals** → **caffeine (small)**. Together they typically remove **300–700 kcal/day** of spontaneous intake. **No OTC product is remotely equivalent to a GLP-1 drug.** **File 05.**

### 2.5 What helps prevent weight regain for years?
**Continued physical activity (200–300 min/week plus resistance training), frequent self-weighing, high protein intake, continued (even minimal) behavioural contact, and early correction of small regains.** Look AHEAD maintained **4.7% vs 2.1%** weight loss at 8 years with continued intervention; DPP cut diabetes incidence by 58% even with partial regain. **The single best mechanism is a written rule: a maintenance range of ±1.5 kg and a 2-week correction triggered at +2 kg.** **Files 06, 07, 21.**

### 2.6 What are the highest-value anti-aging interventions?
**Not smoking, blood-pressure control, cardiorespiratory fitness, strength training, avoiding a large waist, 7–9 h sleep, high fibre/Mediterranean-style diet, limited alcohol, and daily sunscreen.** Each 1-MET of fitness ≈ 13% lower all-cause mortality (Kodama 2009); strength training 30–60 min/week ≈ 10–17% lower mortality (Momma 2022); intensive BP control cut major CV events and mortality (SPRINT 2015). **No supplement or device has human evidence of extending lifespan.** **Files 12, 13.**

### 2.7 What are the best low-cost glow-up interventions for men?
**Ranked:** (1) **fat loss to 10–15% body fat** (the face changes first and most); (2) **daily sunscreen SPF 30–50** (24% less skin-ageing progression; HR 0.50 for melanoma at 10-year follow-up); (3) **a nightly retinoid** (adapalene OTC or tretinoin Rx); (4) **a good barber every 3–5 weeks**; (5) **teeth cleaning/whitening**; (6) **well-fitted clothes**; (7) **2 lifting sessions/week for shoulders and posture**. Total: ~$25–45/month. **Files 14, 15, 24.**

### 2.8 Which supplements are genuinely worthwhile?
**Creatine monohydrate 3–5 g/day** (High evidence, ~$0.16–0.21/day) · **vitamin D3 1,000–2,000 IU if low sun exposure/deficient** · **omega-3 only if you don't eat oily fish** · **psyllium if fibre <25 g/day** · **magnesium and a multivitamin as cheap optional extras**. Nothing else. **File 16.**

### 2.9 Which expensive products can be skipped?
Fat burners, testosterone boosters, BCAA/EAA, glutamine, HMB, NAD precursors (NMN/NR), resveratrol, spermidine, collagen (for muscle), "greens" powders, probiotic blends, LED/red-light masks, microcurrent devices, jawline exercisers, posture correctors, hCG injections, "detox" teas, body wraps, IV vitamin drips, hyperbaric oxygen, exosomes/stem-cell injections, biological-age and telomere tests, metabolic-rate testing, and compounded/grey-market GLP-1 drugs. **Files 13, 17, 23.**

### 2.10 What are the biggest myths?
"Starvation mode/"broken metabolism" · "spot reduction" · "keto/fasting gives a metabolic advantage" · "detox" · "you must eat 6 meals" · "protein damages kidneys" · "creatine causes hair loss" · "autophagy starts at hour 16" · "NMN/resveratrol reverse ageing" · "metformin is a longevity drug" · "testosterone boosters work" · "mewing/jaw exercisers reshape adult bone" · "eye creams fix under-eye bags" · "LED masks reverse skin ageing" · "one glass of wine is healthy". **File 23** grades each.

### 2.11 What is the simplest daily routine?
**Protein at every meal · two lifting sessions a week · 8,000–12,000 steps · 7–9 h sleep · no liquid calories · sunscreen in the morning · weigh weekly.** About **20 minutes of active effort per day**. **File 19.**

### 2.12 What is the most important maintenance strategy?
**A written, self-correcting feedback loop:** a maintenance range (goal ±1.5 kg), a weekly 7-day weight average, and a pre-planned **2-week correction** triggered when the average sits 2 kg above the range for 2 consecutive weeks — while keeping **protein, lifting, walking and sleep** unchanged forever. **Files 06, 07, 21.**

## 3. Evidence quality — how to read the library

Each file grades its claims **High / Moderate / Low / Very low / Insufficient**, and states why. The distribution across this library:

| Grade | Strength of the claim class | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | Multiple large RCTs/meta-analyses, direct outcomes | Protein and hypertrophy; sunscreen and photoaging; fibre and disease risk; semaglutide/tirzepatide efficacy and regain on stopping; smoking and mortality |
| **Moderate** | RCTs that are small, short, or use surrogates | Time-restricted eating; psyllium and satiety; self-weighing and maintenance; sleep and fat:lean ratio |
| **Low** | Observational, small uncontrolled, indirect | Red-light devices; collagen; sauna; oral minoxidil (off-label) |
| **Insufficient** | No adequate human data | NMN/NR outcomes; rapamycin in healthy humans; human autophagy thresholds; most "longevity" compounds |

**Method note:** every PMID and citation string in this library was retrieved from PubMed's own metadata via the NCBI E-utilities API; product prices were read from live iHerb listings on 1 Nov 2026; where a product page could not be verified, a marketplace **search link** is given instead of a fabricated URL. Full audit trail: **file 22**.

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach (the 16-week version)

| Setting | Prescription |
|---|---|
| **Deficit** | 20–25% below estimated maintenance (weight kg × 22–30, calibrated against 2–3 weeks of real data) |
| **Protein** | 2.2–2.6 g/kg/day, ≥30–40 g at breakfast |
| **Training** | 4 sessions/week (upper/lower split) or 3 full-body; 3–4 sets of 6–10, 1–3 reps in reserve |
| **Cardio/NEAT** | 10,000–12,000 steps + 1–2 interval sessions |
| **Sleep** | 7–9 h, fixed wake time |
| **Supplements** | Creatine 5 g; vitamin D if low sun; psyllium if fibre low |
| **Rate** | 0.75–1.0% body weight/week → **9–16 kg in 12–20 weeks** for a 95–110 kg man |
| **Exit** | Reverse diet +100–200 kcal/week, maintenance range written before the diet ends |

## 6. Free and cheap alternatives — the highest-value free interventions

1. **Walking** (8–12k steps) — mortality + expenditure, $0.
2. **Bodyweight training** (push-ups, squats, hinges, rows on a table/door) — muscle retention, $0.
3. **Sleep routine** (fixed wake time, caffeine cutoff, dark room) — ~270 kcal/day intake reduction, $0.
4. **Protein-from-food substitution** (eggs/milk/lentils instead of powder) — cheaper per gram.
5. **Fibre from food** (oats, legumes, frozen vegetables) — cheaper than any supplement.
6. **The 2-kg rule + weekly weighing** — the best maintenance tool, $0.
7. **Sunscreen discipline + no smoking** — the two biggest appearance/health levers.
8. **Food-environment design** (what's in the house, what's visible, what's pre-portioned) — free behaviour change.
9. **PubMed** — free verification of every claim; replaces paid "research" services.
10. **A written page** with your rules — costs nothing, outperforms most subscriptions.

## 7. Practical protocol (the complete daily/weekly/monthly system)

### Morning (10 min)
- **Same wake time**; bright light within 30 min; 250–500 ml water.
- **Protein breakfast** (30–50 g) — the highest-leverage meal decision of the day.
- **Sunscreen SPF 30–50** (face + neck), moisturiser if dry.
- 10-min walk or commute-by-foot.
- *No caffeine after midday if you're sleep-sensitive.*

### Eating window (14:10 or 16:8, optional)
- First meal ~11:00–12:00; last meal ends **2–3 h before bed**.
- **Every meal: protein first, vegetables second, starch third.**
- **500 ml water** before main meals; **zero liquid calories**.
- **2 meals + 1 snack** in the window (or 3 meals in 14:10); protein ≥40 g per main meal.
- 10-min walk after the largest meal.

### Fasting window
- Water, black coffee, plain tea only. No sweetened drinks, no "fasting" products.
- If you feel ravenous, dizzy, or obsessive about food → **shorten the window to 14:10 or eat normally**; the schedule serves you, not the reverse.

### Training (3–4 × 40 min/week)
- **A:** squat, press, row, hinge, core. **B:** hinge, vertical pull, incline press, lunge, carry.
- 3–4 sets × 6–10 reps, 1–3 reps in reserve; add load or reps weekly.
- **Deload** (half volume) every 6–10 weeks or when performance stalls.
- **1–2 cardio sessions**: 30–45 min brisk walk, or 4×4 min intervals.
- **Daily steps: 8,000–12,000.**

### Evening (60 min before bed)
- Last meal ≥2–3 h before bed; **kitchen closed**.
- No alcohol (or ≤1 drink and ≥3 h before bed).
- Screens off 45–60 min before bed; phone charging outside the bedroom.
- Prepare tomorrow's food/clothes (removes morning decisions).
- **Retinoid** (2–7 nights/week as tolerated) + moisturiser.

### Weekly (10 min)
- **Weigh daily; use the 7-day average.** Inside range → do nothing.
- **2–4 resistance sessions**; average ≥8,000 steps.
- **One grocery run** with the 4-item list (protein, frozen vegetables, fruit, fibre base).
- **One 5-minute review:** weight average, sessions, steps, alcohol units, sleep average.
- **2–4 flexible meals** allowed — planned, not accidental.

### Monthly (20 min)
- **Waist measurement + photos** (same light, same pose).
- **BP check** (home monitor, correct technique).
- Restock creatine/psyllium/vitamin D; **barber**; check the maintenance range.
- Act on the **2-kg rule** if triggered (2-week correction, never a crash diet).

### Long-term maintenance (forever)
- **Non-negotiables:** protein at every meal, 2+ lifting sessions/week, 8k+ steps/day, 7+ h sleep, ≤4 alcohol units/week, sunscreen daily.
- **Written range** (goal ±1.5 kg) and **written trigger** (+2 kg for 2 weeks → 2-week correction).
- **Annual:** blood pressure, basic bloods, dental check, fitness benchmark.
- **Never stop weighing.** Never crash-correct. Never restart from zero.
- **Reframe:** holding 70–80% of the loss for 5 years is an excellent outcome; chasing the lowest number ever seen is how men end up back at their starting weight.

## 8. Low-maintenance version (the whole library in five lines)

1. **Protein at every meal.**
2. **Lift twice a week.**
3. **Walk 30 minutes a day.**
4. **Sleep 7–9 hours; no liquid calories; sunscreen every morning.**
5. **Weigh weekly; correct at +2 kg for two weeks.**

That is ~20 minutes a day and captures most of the proven benefit in these 27 files.

## 9. Safety and contraindications (library-wide)

- **Get medical clearance** before an aggressive deficit or new vigorous training if you have cardiovascular disease, diabetes on insulin/sulfonylureas, kidney/liver disease, uncontrolled hypertension, or symptoms (chest pain, fainting, unusual breathlessness).
- **Do not self-prescribe** GLP-1 drugs, testosterone, finasteride, oral minoxidil, tretinoin, metformin, rapamycin or peptides. Do not buy them from unregulated sellers.
- **Do not fast** with a history of eating disorders, type 1 diabetes, insulin/sulfonylurea-treated diabetes, or when underweight.
- **Stop and seek help** for: fainting, palpitations, persistent insomnia, hair shedding, loss of libido, binge episodes, obsessive food thoughts, or weight loss >1.5%/week sustained.
- **Supplements:** disclose everything to your doctor; stop biotin 48–72 h before blood tests; avoid ashwagandha (liver injury reports); avoid stimulant "fat burners".
- **Skin:** any new/changing/bleeding mole or non-healing spot → doctor, not a cosmetic clinic.
- **Mental health:** if food rules, body image or mood are causing distress, that is a clinical issue, not a discipline problem.

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Run the 16-week protocol (§5) or the low-maintenance version (§8)** — both are evidence-based; the second is more likely to be followed.
2. **Protect muscle:** protein 2.0–2.6 g/kg, lift 2–4×/week, keep the deficit moderate, sleep.
3. **Buy almost nothing.** A food scale, shoes, creatine, psyllium, sunscreen, adapalene, vitamin D if low, a BP monitor.
4. **Write the maintenance rules before you need them** (range + 2-kg trigger).
5. **Measure BP and waist**; skip biological-age tests.
6. **Do the free things first, forever** — walking, lifting, sleeping, protein, fibre, sunscreen, not smoking.
7. **Accept the honest ceiling of lifestyle (5–8%)** and, if you need more and qualify, use medication *with* this protocol rather than instead of it.
8. **Re-read files 06, 07 and 23 every few months** — maintenance and myth-resistance are skills that decay.

## 14. Strongest findings (one-paragraph synthesis)

The evidence converges on a single conclusion: **behaviour beats biology-peddling, and structure beats willpower.** Energy balance determines fat loss; protein and resistance training determine what that loss is made of; sleep and food volume determine how bearable the deficit is; and self-monitoring plus a written correction rule determines whether the result survives its first year. Expensive pharmacology is genuinely more powerful than lifestyle for *weight loss* — 15–21% versus 5–8% — but it does not repeal physiology: stopping reverses the loss, and the muscle-sparing and maintenance behaviours in this library become *more* important, not less. Meanwhile, the entire supplement-and-device industry offers, at best, small biomarker changes with no demonstrated human outcome benefit. The highest-value interventions in this library are free: walking, lifting, sleeping, protein, fibre, not smoking, sunscreen, and a weekly weigh-in with a plan for what to do about it.

## 15. Evidence gaps

1. **Long-term (>2 year) randomised maintenance trials** with body-composition endpoints — almost nonexistent.
2. **Direct drug vs intensive-lifestyle trials** measuring lean mass, strength and function (not just weight).
3. **Human autophagy measurement** — no validated method, therefore no verified hour thresholds.
4. **Cosmetic device evidence** — small, short, mostly industry-funded.
5. **Optimal rate of loss** — one small athlete RCT (Garthe 2011) plus convention.
6. **The 2-kg trigger rule** — physiologically plausible, no RCT.
7. **Long-term safety of GLP-1/multi-agonists** (>5 years) and of stopping after long-term use.
8. **Protein needs in older, dieting men** — extrapolated.
9. **Which men maintain best on which structure** — no validated personalisation.
10. **Maintenance after bariatric surgery** — regain predictors are poorly characterised.
11. **"Diet breaks" and refeeds** — plausible, thin evidence.
12. **Male-specific skin/hair interventions** — most dermatology trials skew female.
