# 16 — Supplements: Evidence Review

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Almost no supplement is necessary for a man who eats reasonably and trains.** The short list that survives evidence review is: **creatine, vitamin D (only if deficient or low sun exposure), omega-3 (only if you don't eat fish), fibre/psyllium, and — for specific conditions — iron/B12 if deficient.**
- **Creatine monohydrate is the best-value supplement in existence for a man**: high-certainty evidence for strength and lean mass with training, excellent safety profile, and ~$0.16–0.21/day (verified price, file 18).
- **Vitamin D, omega-3 and multivitamins have failed their big outcome trials** in generally healthy adults: VITAL found no reduction in cancer or cardiovascular disease for either vitamin D or omega-3; D-Health found no mortality benefit for vitamin D; COSMOS found no reduction in cancer or CVD for a multivitamin. These are **deficiency-correction tools, not longevity drugs**.
- **Protein powder is food, not a supplement** — useful only if it helps you hit protein cheaply/conveniently.
- **Fat burners, testosterone boosters, BCAAs, glutamine, HMB, collagen (for muscles), "greens" powders, probiotic blends, and NAD precursors are not worth buying** for the outcomes in this library.
- **Two safety points that matter**: (1) **biotin** interferes with common lab immunoassays (including thyroid and cardiac troponin tests) and has caused misdiagnosis; (2) **"natural" does not mean safe** — ashwagandha has documented liver injury, green tea extract has caused hepatotoxicity, and high-dose vitamin D causes hypercalcaemia.
- **Supplement quality is not guaranteed.** Choose products with third-party testing where available, single-ingredient products, and brands with published certificates of analysis.

## 2. What the evidence establishes (supplement by supplement)

### ✅ Creatine monohydrate
- **Evidence:** ISSN position stand concludes creatine is safe and effective for increasing strength, lean mass and exercise performance (Kreider 2017 — High). Benefits are clearest with resistance training; older adults also benefit (Chilibeck 2017 — Moderate).
- **Dose:** 3–5 g/day, any time; loading (20 g/day × 5 days) is optional and not required.
- **Safety:** no evidence of kidney damage in healthy people; ~1 kg water gain possible; GI upset if taken in large single doses.
- **Myths dispelled:** it does **not** cause hair loss (a 12-week RCT found no effect — Lak 2025) and is not a steroid.
- **Verdict: ✅ buy** (single-ingredient monohydrate; ~$0.16–0.21/day).

### 🟡 Vitamin D3
- **Evidence:** VITAL (n=25,871) found **no** reduction in invasive cancer or cardiovascular events (Manson 2019 — High). D-Health found **no mortality benefit** (Neale 2022 — High). USPSTF recommends against routine supplementation to prevent fractures in community-dwelling adults (2018 — High). An individual-patient-data meta-analysis suggested a possible reduction in **cancer mortality** with daily dosing (Kuznia 2023 — Moderate).
- **When it helps:** documented deficiency, minimal sun exposure, dark skin at high latitude, obesity, older age, malabsorption.
- **Dose:** 1,000–2,000 IU/day; recheck after 3 months; avoid >4,000 IU/day without monitoring.
- **Verdict: 🟡 conditional** — cheap and safe; not a longevity drug.

### 🟡 Omega-3 (EPA/DHA)
- **Evidence:** VITAL found **no** reduction in major cardiovascular events in generally healthy adults (Manson 2019 — High). **REDUCE-IT** found a 25% relative risk reduction with **prescription icosapent ethyl 4 g/day** in patients with high triglycerides on statins (Bhatt 2019 — High) — a different product, dose and population. Dose-response meta-analysis suggests benefit at higher doses (Bernasconi 2021 — Moderate). High-dose omega-3 increases **atrial fibrillation** risk (Abuknesha 2026 — Moderate-High).
- **Dose:** 1–2 g/day combined EPA+DHA if no fish intake; eat fish 1–2×/week instead where affordable.
- **Verdict: 🟡 conditional.**

### 🟡 Magnesium
- **Evidence:** meta-analysis shows a small reduction in blood pressure, mainly at higher doses and in people with hypertension (Argeros 2025 — Moderate). Sleep/anxiety/cramp evidence is weak (Low).
- **Dose:** 200–400 mg elemental magnesium (glycinate/citrate) if diet is poor; avoid in kidney disease.
- **Verdict: 🟡 optional/cheap.**

### 🟡 Multivitamin
- **Evidence:** COSMOS found **no** reduction in cancer or cardiovascular disease (Sesso 2022 — High); a meta-analysis found no mortality benefit (Macpherson 2013 — Moderate). A small cognitive signal exists in COSMOS-Mind (Low-Moderate).
- **Verdict: 🟡 cheap insurance only** if your diet is chaotic.

### ⚪/⛔ Not recommended
| Supplement | Why not |
|---|---|
| **BCAAs / EAAs** | Redundant if protein intake is adequate; inferior to whole protein |
| **Glutamine** | No benefit for healthy trainees; no effect on muscle/immunity outcomes |
| **HMB** | Small effects at best; irrelevant with adequate protein |
| **Testosterone "boosters"** (tribulus, fenugreek, D-aspartic acid, ZMA) | No meaningful testosterone or body-composition effect |
| **Fat burners / thermogenics** | Negligible effect; stimulant and adulteration risk |
| **Garcinia, raspberry ketone, forskolin, CLA, green coffee** | No meaningful effect; some hepatotoxicity reports (green tea extract) |
| **NMN / NR (NAD+)** | Raise NAD+, no clinical outcome; expensive (file 13) |
| **Resveratrol** | No outcome benefit; possible harm signals |
| **Spermidine** | Minimal effect on polyamines; no outcome data |
| **Ashwagandha** | Small stress/sleep studies; **documented liver injury** cases |
| **Biotin (for hair/skin/nails)** | No benefit absent deficiency; **interferes with lab tests** |
| **Collagen (for muscle)** | Not a complete protein; only small skin effects (file 14) |
| **Probiotic blends** | No weight/longevity outcomes; useful for specific GI indications |
| **"Greens" powders** | Expensive vegetables; fibre and micronutrients cheaper from food |
| **BCAA + ketone + pre-workout stacks** | Cost without benefit; caffeine alone is equivalent |
| **DHEA / "hormone support"** | No outcome evidence; hormonal effects |
| **Zinc megadoses for testosterone** | Only corrects deficiency; high doses cause copper deficiency |

### Safety-specific notes
- **Biotin** at high doses distorts immunoassays (thyroid, troponin) — stop 48–72 h before blood tests (Bhimireddy 2026 — Low-Moderate but well documented in lab medicine).
- **Ashwagandha** liver injury is documented in case series/scoping reviews (McIntyre 2026 — Low but consistent).
- **Pre-workouts** often contain proprietary blends, excessive stimulants, and occasionally banned substances; if you compete in tested sport, avoid.
- **Iron** should only be taken if a blood test shows deficiency — excess iron is harmful.

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

**The complete supplement stack worth owning (a man who eats well and lifts):**
1. **Creatine monohydrate 5 g/day** — every day, no loading, no cycling.
2. **Vitamin D3 1,000–2,000 IU/day** — only if low sun exposure or tested low.
3. **Psyllium 3–5 g/day** — if fibre <25 g/day.
4. **Omega-3 1–2 g/day** — only if you don't eat fish.
5. **Multivitamin** — optional, cheap insurance.

Total: roughly **$10–20/month**. That is the entire evidence-based stack. Nothing else in the supplement market is required for fat loss, muscle, skin or longevity in a healthy man.

## 7. Practical protocol

**Buying rules:**
1. **Single ingredient**: "creatine monohydrate", "vitamin D3", "psyllium husk" — not blends.
2. **Third-party testing** where available (e.g., Informed Sport/NSF for athletes; USP for pharmacy brands).
3. **Ignore front-of-label claims**; read the supplement facts panel and check the dose matches the effective dose.
4. **Cost per effective dose**, not cost per bottle.
5. **Don't stack more than 4–5 products.** More products = more interactions, more expense, more chance of contamination.

**Timing (mostly irrelevant):**
- Creatine: any time, daily consistency matters.
- Vitamin D: with a fat-containing meal.
- Omega-3: with meals to reduce reflux.
- Psyllium: before the hungriest meal, with a full glass of water.

**Testing before supplementing (worth doing):**
- Vitamin D (25-OH), ferritin/CBC, B12 if vegetarian, TSH if symptomatic, HbA1c/lipids annually. Treat deficiencies; don't guess.

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Kidney disease:** creatine, high protein and magnesium all need medical advice.
- **Bleeding risk/anticoagulants:** high-dose fish oil, vitamin E, ginkgo, garlic supplements.
- **Thyroid medication:** fibre supplements and calcium/iron reduce absorption — separate by 4 h.
- **Diabetes medication:** berberine, cinnamon, chromium and other "glucose support" supplements can cause hypoglycaemia.
- **Psychiatric medication:** St John's wort has major interactions (e.g., with SSRIs, oral contraceptives, immunosuppressants) — avoid.
- **Biotin:** stop 48–72 h before blood tests.
- **Pregnancy (household):** high-dose vitamin A/retinol supplements are teratogenic — check labels.
- **Contamination:** sports supplements are a known source of banned substances; use third-party tested products if you compete.
- **Do not buy** "research chemicals", SARMs, peptides, or injectable supplements online.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Buying 10 supplements** instead of eating protein, fibre and vegetables.
2. **Testosterone boosters** instead of sleep, weight loss and lifting.
3. **Fat burners** instead of a deficit.
4. **Biotin before blood tests.**
5. **Megadose vitamin D** without testing.
6. **Proprietary blends** with unknown doses.
7. **Assuming FDA/EMA approval means efficacy** — supplements are regulated as food, not drugs, in most countries; efficacy claims are not pre-approved.
8. **Buying "grass-fed"/"organic" versions of things with no evidence** (e.g., organic creatine).
9. **Taking BCAAs while under-eating protein.**
10. **Ignoring interactions with prescription medication.**

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Creatine 5 g/day** — the one supplement worth defaulting to.
2. **Vitamin D only if deficient/low sun.**
3. **Omega-3 only if no fish; otherwise eat fish.**
4. **Psyllium if fibre is low.**
5. **Test, don't guess:** a yearly blood panel beats a supplement cabinet.
6. **Everything else in the supplement aisle is optional at best and a waste at worst.**
7. **Stop biotin 48–72 h before any blood test.**
