# 14 — Skincare & Glow-Up (Evidence-Based)

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Only three things have strong human evidence for improving how skin ages: sun protection, retinoids, and not smoking.** Everything else is either supportive (cleansing, moisturising, treating acne), modest (niacinamide, azelaic acid, vitamin C), or unproven (most devices, most "actives", almost all supplements).
- **Daily sunscreen is the single highest-value skincare intervention that exists.** In the Nambour trial, daily sunscreen use slowed skin ageing by ~24% over 4.5 years vs discretionary use (Hughes 2013 — High), and in 10-year follow-up of the same cohort, daily sunscreen was associated with **about half the melanoma incidence** (11 vs 22 invasive melanomas; HR 0.50, 95% CI 0.24–1.02) — a large effect with a confidence interval that just touches 1.0, so best read as **strong but imprecise** (Green 2011 — Moderate-High).
- **Topical retinoids are the best-evidenced "anti-aging" ingredient.** Tretinoin restores collagen formation in photodamaged skin (Griffiths 1993 — Moderate-High) and improves fine wrinkles (Weiss 1988 — Moderate-High). **Adapalene 0.1% is available over the counter** in many countries and is a reasonable OTC retinoid; retinol is weaker but better tolerated (McDaniel 2017 — Low-Moderate).
- **A basic routine of cleanser + moisturiser + sunscreen + retinoid at night** costs roughly **$20–40 per month** and captures essentially all of the evidence-based benefit.
- **Weight loss changes the face more than any cream** — but rapid/extreme loss can hollow the face and age appearance. Slow loss plus protein and resistance training preserves facial volume better (Low-Moderate evidence; largely inference).
- **Red-light/LED devices, microcurrent, facial exercises, collagen supplements, and "stem cell" creams have weak-to-absent evidence** and are the main places men waste money.
- **Acne in adult men is treatable and evidence-based:** benzoyl peroxide, adapalene, salicylic acid, azelaic acid; see a clinician for persistent/scarring acne. Treat it — scarring is permanent.

## 2. What the evidence establishes

### 2.1 Sun protection (the foundation)
- **Nambour trial** (randomised, Australia): daily sunscreen vs discretionary use for 4.5 years → **24% less progression of skin ageing** in the daily-use group (Hughes 2013, Ann Intern Med — High).
- **10-year follow-up**: daily sunscreen use was associated with **about half the melanoma incidence** (Green 2011, J Clin Oncol — High; small number of events but a randomised design).
- **Photoaging is caused mainly by UVA** (deep, collagen-degrading) and UVB (burning); **both are covered by "broad-spectrum" SPF 30–50**.
- Practical rules: **SPF 30–50, broad-spectrum, every morning, ~2 mg/cm² (about a teaspoon and a half for the face and neck), reapply with prolonged sun exposure.** Sunscreen is not optional if you use a retinoid (retinoids increase photosensitivity).

### 2.2 Retinoids (the active ingredient worth having)
- **Tretinoin** (prescription in most countries) restores collagen formation in photodamaged skin (Griffiths 1993, NEJM — Moderate-High) and improves photoaging in vehicle-controlled trials (Weiss 1988, JAMA — Moderate-High).
- **Adapalene 0.1%** is OTC in many markets; it is a third-generation retinoid with good tolerability and evidence in acne (Reynolds 2024 acne guideline — High for acne), and is used off-label for photoaging.
- **Retinol** (cosmetic) is 10–20× weaker; a double-conjugated retinoid cream performed comparably to 1% retinol and 0.025% tretinoin in one trial (McDaniel 2017 — Low-Moderate, industry-linked).
- **How to use:** pea-sized amount, at night, on dry skin, 2–3 nights/week initially → build to nightly as tolerated. Moisturiser on top ("retinoid sandwich") reduces irritation. Expect 8–12 weeks for texture/pigmentation change; 6+ months for wrinkles.
- **Side effects:** irritation, dryness, flaking, initial purge in acne. **Avoid in pregnancy** (household relevance), and use sunscreen daily.

### 2.3 Moisturisers and barrier care
- Moisturisers improve skin hydration, barrier function and appearance — **but there is no good evidence that they prevent wrinkles long-term** (Low/Insufficient).
- **Ceramide-containing, fragrance-free moisturisers** are cheap and effective; "anti-aging" moisturisers cost more for negligible added benefit.
- **Cleansing:** a gentle, non-soap or syndet cleanser once or twice daily is enough. Harsh soaps and scrubs damage the barrier (Moderate). Men's skin is thicker and oilier on average, but that does not justify abrasive products.

### 2.4 Acne (adult men)
- **First-line OTC:** benzoyl peroxide 2.5–5% (antibacterial), adapalene 0.1% (comedolytic), salicylic acid 2% (mild), azelaic acid 15–20% (acne + pigmentation).
- **Guideline-level statement:** topical combination therapy is more effective than monotherapy for inflammatory acne; antibiotic monotherapy should be avoided; oral isotretinoin is for severe/scarring acne under dermatology care (Reynolds 2024, JAAD — High).
- **Diet:** observational meta-analyses link **dairy (especially skim milk) and high-glycaemic-index diets** to acne (Juhl 2018; Aghasi 2019 — Low-Moderate, observational). A 4–8 week dairy reduction trial is reasonable, cheap, and low-risk.
- **Do not pick.** Scarring is permanent; scarring costs hundreds to thousands to treat later.

### 2.5 Pigmentation
- **Sunscreen is the treatment** — without it, nothing else works.
- **Azelaic acid** has systematic-review support across acne, rosacea and melasma, and compared favourably to hydroquinone in one meta-analysis (King 2023 — Moderate; Albzea 2023 — Low-Moderate).
- **Niacinamide** reduces facial hyperpigmentation (Kimball 2010, Br J Dermatol — Moderate).
- **Hydroquinone** (2–4%) is effective but prescription/restricted in many countries; risk of ochronosis with long-term unmonitored use.
- **Tranexamic acid (topical/oral)** has growing evidence for melasma; oral requires medical supervision.

### 2.6 Devices and supplements
| Intervention | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Red-light/LED devices | Small, short, often industry-funded trials; a 2023 study reported improvements in skin-aging signs (Couturaud 2023); a 2025 trial examined combined yellow/red/infrared LED (Yi 2025) — **Low** | **Optional at best**; expensive per unit of benefit |
| Microneedling (at home) | Some evidence for scars/hair when done properly; **infection and scarring risk if done wrong** | Clinic-only if at all |
| Microcurrent/facial toning devices | Very weak | **Skip** |
| Facial exercises | One small 20-week study reported improved fullness in middle-aged women (Alam 2018, JAMA Dermatol — Low) | Not evidence for reshaping bone; **skip** |
| Collagen supplements | Meta-analyses show small improvements in skin hydration/elasticity in short trials (Myung 2025 — Low-Moderate) | Optional, expensive per benefit |
| Vitamin C serum | Modest antioxidant/depigmenting effect; unstable formulations | Optional |
| "Stem cell"/"growth factor"/"DNA repair" creams | No outcome evidence | **Skip** |
| IV glutathione / skin whitening injections | No adequate evidence; real risks (renal, hepatic, infection) | **Avoid** |

### 2.7 Lifestyle inputs with real skin relevance
- **Smoking** accelerates wrinkles and wound-healing delay (Moderate/High).
- **Sleep deprivation** impairs skin barrier recovery in controlled studies (Kwon 2025, Ann Dermatol — Low-Moderate).
- **Alcohol** → dehydration, flushing, poorer sleep (Low-Moderate).
- **Diet:** high sugar/ultra-processed intake is plausibly pro-glycation but human evidence for wrinkles is weak (Insufficient). High fruit/vegetable intake is associated with better skin appearance (Low).
- **Hydration:** normal hydration helps skin turgor; drinking extra water beyond thirst does not "hydrate" skin measurably (Low).
- **Weight loss:** improves facial definition; **rapid/extreme loss** can hollow cheeks and deepen nasolabial folds (Low-Moderate; see file 15).

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

**The 4-product routine (≈$20–40/month):**
1. **Morning:** gentle cleanser (or just water) → **moisturiser with SPF 30–50** (or separate sunscreen). Reapply if outdoors >2 h.
2. **Evening:** gentle cleanser → **retinoid (adapalene 0.1% or tretinoin if prescribed)** 2–3 nights/week building to nightly → **plain moisturiser**.
3. **Weekly:** nothing extra required. No scrubs, no 10-step routines.
4. **If acne:** add benzoyl peroxide 2.5–5% in the morning (or a BPO/adapalene combination).
5. **If pigmentation:** add azelaic acid 15–20% (or niacinamide 4–5%) and be obsessive about sunscreen.

**Limitations:** retinoids take 3–6 months; results are modest compared to procedures; irritation is common in the first month; tretinoin requires a prescription in most countries.

**What is "fastest" for appearance overall** is not skincare at all — it is **fat loss to a healthy body fat, sleep, and a haircut that suits you** (file 15).

## 7. Practical protocol

### Beginner (weeks 1–4)
- AM: water rinse → SPF 30+ moisturiser.
- PM: gentle cleanser → moisturiser.
- Add adapalene 2 nights/week in week 3.

### Building (weeks 5–12)
- Adapalene up to 3–4 nights/week as tolerated; moisturiser on top.
- If acne: BPO 2.5% in the morning on acne-prone areas.
- Never use a scrub + retinoid on the same day.

### Maintenance (month 3+)
- Retinoid 4–7 nights/week.
- SPF every single morning (this is the one non-negotiable).
- Moisturise as needed; treat flares with BPO/azelaic acid.

### Shaving-friendly advice
- Shave **after** (not before) the shower if you get irritation; use a sharp blade and shave with the grain; a fragrance-free moisturiser post-shave; avoid alcohol-based aftershaves on irritated skin.

### What to do about a specific concern
| Concern | Action |
|---|---|
| Dullness/texture | Retinoid + consistent SPF (8–12 weeks) |
| Fine lines | Retinoid + SPF; stop smoking; sleep |
| Pigmentation/sun spots | SPF + azelaic acid/niacinamide (or prescribed hydroquinone) |
| Acne | BPO + adapalene; reduce dairy for 6 weeks if you want a trial; clinician if scarring |
| Redness/rosacea | Gentle routine, azelaic acid, sun protection; see clinician |
| Under-eye bags | Sleep, sodium/alcohol reduction, treat allergy; nothing topical fixes fat herniation (surgery does) |
| Dryness/flaking | Fewer actives, thicker moisturiser, reduce retinoid frequency |

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**Two products, two minutes a day: SPF moisturiser in the morning, retinoid at night (plus a cleanser if you get oily).** That is ~90% of the evidence-based benefit and less than $25/month.

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Retinoids:** avoid in pregnancy and breastfeeding (household relevance: partners); increased sun sensitivity — sunscreen mandatory; do not layer with strong acids initially.
- **Benzoyl peroxide:** bleaches towels and clothing; irritation; rare allergy.
- **Salicylic acid:** avoid with aspirin allergy; not for large-area use in children.
- **Hydroquinone:** restricted in many countries; risk of ochronosis with prolonged unsupervised use.
- **Sunscreen:** choose one you will actually wear; some chemical filters are regulated differently by country (EU vs US); mineral (zinc/titanium) is fine if you prefer.
- **Skin cancer:** any new/changing/bleeding mole, non-healing spot, or asymmetric pigmented lesion → see a doctor. **Do not treat suspicious lesions with creams.**
- **Isotretinoin:** prescription-only, requires monitoring (liver, lipids, pregnancy prevention).
- **Avoid:** skin-lightening injections, unregulated "peels", at-home deepneedling, and any product claiming to "reverse" ageing.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Buying serums before sunscreen** — backwards order of value.
2. **Not using enough sunscreen** (or only on sunny days).
3. **Using 5 actives at once** — irritation, then blame on "sensitive skin".
4. **Expecting results in 2 weeks** — retinoids need 3–6 months.
5. **Skipping moisturiser with a retinoid** → irritation → quitting.
6. **Scrubs/exfoliating acids daily** — barrier damage.
7. **$300 LED mask instead of $15 adapalene.**
8. **Ignoring acne until it scars.**
9. **Smoking while buying "anti-aging" creams.**
10. **Believing collagen drinks replace sunscreen/retinoid.**

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Sunscreen daily, SPF 30–50, broad-spectrum.** Non-negotiable.
2. **Retinoid at night** (adapalene OTC or tretinoin Rx), built up slowly.
3. **Gentle cleanser + basic ceramide moisturiser.**
4. **Treat acne early** with BPO/adapalene/azelaic acid; get help if scarring.
5. **No smoking, 7–9 h sleep, moderate alcohol.**
6. **Lose fat slowly** to protect facial volume (file 15).
7. **Buy nothing else** until the above are habitual — then, if you want, add niacinamide/azelaic acid/vitamin C.
