# 17 — Expensive Drugs, Clinics & Affordable Alternatives

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**. This file answers: *what do expensive interventions actually do, what happens when you stop, and what can and cannot be reproduced cheaply?*

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **GLP-1 and dual/triple-agonist drugs are genuinely transformative and cannot be replaced by lifestyle.** Semaglutide 2.4 mg produces ~**15%** weight loss (STEP 1), tirzepatide up to ~**21%** (SURMOUNT-1), and phase-3/2 agents (CagriSema, retatrutide) report **~23–24%**. Lifestyle produces ~**5–8%**. Honest framing: drugs roughly **double to triple** what lifestyle achieves, and no food, fibre or supplement reproduces that.
- **Everything reverses on discontinuation.** Stopping semaglutide after 20 weeks led to regain of roughly **two-thirds** of lost weight within a year (STEP 4); stopping tirzepatide after 36 weeks produced **+14%** regain over 52 weeks (SURMOUNT-4). These drugs are, biologically, **chronic treatments**.
- **The clinically important benefits are not just weight**: SELECT showed a **20% reduction in major cardiovascular events** with semaglutide in adults with obesity and established cardiovascular disease but no diabetes (2023 — High). That is a genuine outcome benefit that lifestyle cannot fully match in magnitude.
- **Muscle loss is a real cost**: a substantial fraction (roughly a quarter to a third in trial body-composition substudies) of the weight lost is lean mass, which is why high protein + resistance training is *more* important on these drugs, not less.
- **Bariatric surgery** produces the largest and most durable weight loss (~25–30% maintained over 10–20 years in SOS) with real risks and lifelong nutritional monitoring.
- **What is genuinely reproducible cheaply:** appetite control (partially), food-volume and protein strategies, resistance training and protein for muscle preservation, sleep, walking, self-monitoring, and — critically — the **maintenance behaviours** that determine what happens after any drug or surgery.
- **What is not reproducible:** the magnitude and speed of pharmacological weight loss, the cardiovascular outcome benefit of semaglutide, and the durability of surgery.
- **Never obtain prescription medications outside medical supervision.** Compounded/unregulated GLP-1 products have caused dosing errors and hospitalisations; "research chemical" peptides are not medicines.

## 2. What the evidence establishes

### 2.1 GLP-1 receptor agonists and multi-agonists

| Agent | Trial & design | Key result | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Semaglutide 2.4 mg s.c. weekly** | STEP 1, n=1,961, 68 wk, no diabetes | **−14.9%** vs −2.4% placebo; ≥15% loss in ~50% (Wilding 2021) | High |
| **Semaglutide 2.4 mg — maintenance** | STEP 4 (withdrawal design) | Stopping → regain of roughly **two-thirds** of lost weight in 1 year (Rubino 2021) | High |
| **Semaglutide 2.4 mg — cardiovascular outcomes** | SELECT, n=17,604, obesity + CVD, no diabetes | **20% lower MACE** (HR 0.80) (Lincoff 2023) | High |
| **Tirzepatide (GIP/GLP-1) 5/10/15 mg** | SURMOUNT-1, n=2,539, 72 wk | Up to **−20.9%** at 15 mg vs −3.1% placebo (Jastreboff 2022) | High |
| **Tirzepatide — maintenance** | SURMOUNT-4 (withdrawal), 88 wk | Continued treatment −5.5% further; placebo switch **+14.0%** (Aronne 2024) | High |
| **Retatrutide (GLP-1/GIP/glucagon) 12 mg** | Phase 2, 48 wk | **−24.2%** (Jastreboff 2023) | Moderate (phase 2) |
| **CagriSema (cagrilintide + semaglutide)** | REDEFINE-1 phase 3, 68 wk | **−22.7%** (topline; semaglutide arm −16.1%) | Moderate (topline/news) |
| **Liraglutide 3.0 mg daily** | SCALE programme | ~−8% (older agent, daily injection) | High |
| **Orlistat 120 mg TID** | Multiple RCTs | ~−3% placebo-subtracted; GI side effects | High |
| **Phentermine/topiramate ER** | CONQUER, n=2,487 | **−9.8%** vs −1.2% at 56 wk (Gadde 2011) | High |
| **Naltrexone/bupropion ER** | COR-I, n=1,742 | **−6.1%** vs −1.3% at 56 wk (Greenway 2010) | High |

**Mechanism, honestly stated:** GLP-1 agonists reduce appetite centrally and slow gastric emptying; GIP/glucagon co-agonism adds effects on satiety and possibly energy expenditure. The result is that people simply eat substantially less, without the compensatory hunger that defeats most diets.

**Risks (important):** nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation (very common); gallbladder disease; pancreatitis (rare); dehydration and kidney injury; **lean-mass loss**; possible gastroparesis; anaemia and micronutrient issues with prolonged poor intake; thyroid C-cell tumour warning (rodent data; human relevance uncertain); contraindications in pregnancy and with certain conditions (e.g., MEN2/medullary thyroid carcinoma history). Long-term (>4 year) safety data are still accumulating.

**Practical realities:** cost (very high, and often not reimbursed), supply interruptions, injection burden, requirement for dose titration, and **lifelong treatment expectation** if the goal is sustained weight loss.

### 2.2 Bariatric surgery
- **Swedish Obese Subjects (SOS)**: sustained weight loss ~25–30% at 10–20 years, reduced diabetes incidence, and reduced cardiovascular events in some analyses (Sjöström 2012 — Moderate-High; Carlsson 2012 — High for diabetes prevention).
- **Risks**: perioperative complications, anastomotic leaks, hernias, micronutrient deficiencies (B12, iron, folate, calcium, vitamin D, thiamine), dumping syndrome, gallstones, need for lifelong supplementation and monitoring, and **weight regain in a significant minority over 5–10+ years** (Moderate).
- **Who it is for:** severe obesity (typically BMI ≥40, or ≥35 with serious comorbidities) after failure of supervised non-surgical treatment. It is a medical decision, not an optimisation.
- **Lifestyle equivalent:** none. But surgery patients who add protein, resistance training and monitoring maintain far better results — the behaviours in this library are what protect the surgical investment.

### 2.3 Clinical obesity programmes and "metabolic" testing
- Structured multidisciplinary programmes (dietitian + clinician + behavioural support) outperform self-directed attempts, mainly by improving adherence and follow-up (High, guideline level; NICE NG246; Obesity Canada CMAJ 2020).
- **Indirect calorimetry (RMR) testing**: prediction equations carry meaningful error (±10% commonly); a single test rarely changes management, because the correct adjustment is always empirical (2–3 weeks of scale data). **Moderate value at best**; useful mainly in extremes (very low body weight, suspected hypometabolism, research).
- **DEXA body composition**: useful feedback, no therapeutic value. A tape measure and photos cover 90% of the decision-making.
- **Continuous glucose monitors in non-diabetics**: no proven weight-loss benefit; interesting but not an obesity treatment (Insufficient).
- **Gut microbiome testing**: no validated actionable output for weight or longevity (Insufficient).

### 2.5 The affordability question: what can and cannot be reproduced

**Taxonomy used in this library — apply it to every claim you read:**

| Category | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Similar mechanism** | Touches the same pathway, unknown clinical magnitude | Fibre slows gastric emptying ~ like GLP-1 (but the effect size is not comparable) |
| **Partial effect** | Reproduces a fraction of the outcome | Protein + fibre + volume reduce intake 300–700 kcal/day vs GLP-1's far larger, centrally driven reduction |
| **Similar practical outcome** | Ends in the same place by different means | A structured deficit + training reaches 5–8% loss; a drug reaches 15–21% |
| **Clinically equivalent** | Trial-proven equivalence | **Nothing in lifestyle is clinically equivalent to semaglutide/tirzepatide for weight loss** |

**Mapping table:**

| Drug/clinical effect | Cheap intervention | Equivalence verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Appetite suppression (GLP-1) | Protein 2 g/kg, 30–40 g fibre, volume foods, sleep, no liquid calories | **Partial effect.** Meaningful but far smaller in magnitude |
| Slowed gastric emptying | Viscous fibre (psyllium), protein, soup/salad preloads | **Similar mechanism, small effect** |
| Reduced food reward/craving | Sleep, removing trigger foods, structured meals, no alcohol | **Partial effect** |
| 15–21% weight loss | Nothing (lifestyle: 5–8%) | **Not equivalent** |
| Cardiovascular risk reduction (SELECT) | BP control, statins if indicated, smoking cessation, exercise, diet | **Different mechanism, comparable-or-better for some outcomes** — but not a substitute if you meet drug criteria |
| Muscle preservation | Protein + resistance training | **Essential on and off the drug** |
| Post-drug maintenance | Files 06 & 07 protocols | **The only thing that determines the outcome** |
| Surgical weight loss (25–30%) | None | **Not equivalent** |

### 2.6 If you are considering a GLP-1 drug (responsible guidance)
1. **Eligibility is medical**: BMI thresholds (commonly ≥30, or ≥27–30 with comorbidities depending on country), and local rules (e.g., NICE TA875/TA1026 in England restrict semaglutide to specialist services and tirzepatide to defined BMI/comorbidity criteria).
2. **Get it through a clinician**, with baseline bloods, blood pressure, and a plan for muscle preservation (protein + resistance training) and for maintenance.
3. **Plan the exit before you start.** The withdrawal trials show what happens without a plan.
4. **Do not buy compounded or grey-market semaglutide/tirzepatide.** Dosing errors and hospitalisations have been reported; purity and sterility are not assured.
5. **Do not use these drugs to reach a cosmetic goal at normal BMI** — the risk/benefit is unfavourable and supply diversion harms patients who need them.

## 3. Evidence quality

- **High:** efficacy of semaglutide/tirzepatide; regain on discontinuation; cardiovascular benefit of semaglutide in SELECT; efficacy of phentermine/topiramate and naltrexone/bupropion; bariatric surgery's durable weight loss.
- **Moderate:** lean-mass fraction of drug-induced loss (substudies vary); retatrutide/CagriSema (phase 2/3 topline); long-term (>5 years) drug safety.
- **Low/Insufficient:** hCG, injections for fat loss, metabolic testing's clinical utility, microbiome testing, "biological age" testing.

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

**If you want the fastest safe result without drugs:** file 20 (aggressive-but-safe transformation) — a 20–25% deficit, 2.2–2.6 g/kg protein, 3–4 resistance sessions, 10–12k steps, 7–9 h sleep, one structured meal pattern, weekly-average monitoring. Expect **0.75–1.0%/week**, i.e. **8–12 kg in 3 months** for a heavier man.

**If you meet medical criteria for pharmacotherapy:** the fastest safe result is **drug + the lifestyle protocol in this library**, because that combination preserves muscle, builds the maintenance skill set, and reduces the regain that follows discontinuation.

**If you have severe obesity with comorbidities:** a structured clinical programme (and consideration of surgery) is the fastest safe route; self-directed dieting at BMI 40+ has a poor long-term record.

## 6. Free and cheap alternatives

- **Instead of a commercial programme:** a self-managed protocol + monthly weigh-in with a friend/clinician + the file 06 maintenance system.
- **Instead of RMR/metabolic testing:** estimate + 2–3 weeks of empirical adjustment.
- **Instead of meal replacements:** a repeated 3-meal template with a protein anchor (files 08, 26).

- **Instead of a longevity clinic:** BP monitor, annual bloods, gym, sunscreen, sleep (file 12).
- **Instead of expensive fibre/appetite products:** psyllium and protein (files 05, 09).

## 7. Practical protocol (for a man on, or considering, pharmacotherapy)

1. **Before starting:** baseline weight, waist, BP, HbA1c, lipids, liver/kidney function; discuss contraindications, cost, and the exit plan.
2. **Protein: 2.0–2.6 g/kg/day** (higher end if appetite is suppressed and intake is low) — the single most important co-intervention.
3. **Resistance training 3×/week** — non-negotiable on GLP-1 drugs.
4. **Micronutrients/fibre:** ensure ≥25 g fibre, adequate fluid, and consider a multivitamin if intake is very low.
5. **Monitor:** weight, waist, strength (to detect muscle loss), and symptoms (nausea, constipation, reflux).
6. **Taper plan:** before stopping, have 8–12 weeks of stable weight on the maintenance protocol (file 06) and a defined correction rule.
7. **After stopping:** weigh weekly, keep protein/training/steps, expect hunger to return — the 2-kg rule is your protection.

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **GLP-1/dual agonists:** contraindicated in pregnancy/breastfeeding, personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2, and in severe gastroparesis; use caution with pancreatitis history, gallbladder disease, diabetic retinopathy (rapid glucose improvement), and in patients on insulin/sulfonylureas (hypoglycaemia).
- **Surgery:** requires lifelong nutritional monitoring; never a "quick fix"; complications can be serious.
- **Phentermine** is a controlled stimulant: cardiovascular effects, insomnia, dependence potential.
- **Topiramate:** cognitive side effects, kidney stones, teratogenicity.
- **Naltrexone/bupropion:** contraindicated with opioid use, seizure history, uncontrolled hypertension, eating disorders.
- **Orlistat:** GI effects, fat-soluble vitamin malabsorption, rare liver injury.
- **Never** buy prescription weight-loss drugs without a prescription; never use someone else's medication; never use "research chemical" GLP-1s or peptides.
- **Do not use drugs to pursue leanness below a healthy body fat** — the risks (muscle loss, malnutrition, gallbladder disease, disordered eating) outweigh cosmetic gains.

## 10. Products and tools

- **Prescription only:** semaglutide (Wegovy/Ozempic — different indications), tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound), liraglutide (Saxenda), phentermine/topiramate (Qsymia), naltrexone/bupropion (Contrave), orlistat (Xenical; a low-dose version is available OTC in some countries).
- **Cheap, evidence-based adjuncts:** protein powder/food, psyllium, a food scale, resistance bands, a home BP monitor (file 18).
- **Not recommended purchases:** hCG, "lipotropic" injections, body wraps, "metabolism boosters", clinic memberships whose main product is a supplement stack.

*This library does not provide dosing guidance for prescription medicines and does not link to pharmacies selling them.*

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Assuming a supplement can substitute for a GLP-1 drug** — it cannot.
2. **Assuming a GLP-1 drug removes the need for lifestyle** — muscle loss and regain say otherwise.
3. **Starting a drug without a discontinuation plan.**
4. **Buying compounded/grey-market GLP-1s** to save money — the leading cause of avoidable harm in this space.
5. **Paying for metabolic testing** and expecting a personalised metabolic solution.
6. **Using hCG, injections, wraps or "cavitation"** — no fat-loss effect.
7. **Choosing surgery without trying a structured programme** — unless medically indicated.
8. **Ignoring protein and training while on medication** — the classic pathway to "skinny fat".
9. **Stopping the drug suddenly without maintenance structures in place.**
10. **Believing the drug is a cure** — it is a treatment that works while it is taken.

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Understand the honest hierarchy:** lifestyle 5–8% · older drugs 3–10% · GLP-1 15% · dual agonist 21% · surgery 25–30%.
2. **Nothing cheap replaces a GLP-1 drug**; but **nothing expensive replaces protein, training and maintenance behaviours.**
3. **If eligible and able, use medication with lifestyle** — not instead of it.
4. **Plan the exit on day one** (files 06/07).
5. **Refuse** hCG, injections, wraps, "metabolism" testing packages, and unregulated GLP-1 products.
6. **Spend your money on protein, shoes, a gym, a food scale and a BP monitor** before spending it on a clinic.
