# 08 — Nutrition & Protein

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Protein is the highest-leverage nutrient for a man trying to change his body**: it protects muscle in a deficit, is the most satiating macronutrient, and has a small thermic cost. Targets: **1.6–2.2 g/kg** at maintenance; **2.0–2.6 g/kg** in a deficit; **≥1.2 g/kg** even for older or sedentary men who are not training.
- **Everything else is negotiable.** Low-carb, low-fat, Mediterranean, vegetarian, and omnivorous patterns all produce similar fat loss when protein and calories are equated (High). The best diet is the one you can execute for years.
- The eating pattern with the most consistent health-outcome evidence is **high in fibre (25–30+ g/day), high in minimally processed foods, rich in vegetables/legumes/fruit, with unsaturated fats and limited ultra-processed food and sugar-sweetened drinks** (High).
- **Ultra-processed food increases spontaneous intake** — ~500 kcal/day in a controlled inpatient trial (Hall 2019 — Moderate-High). You do not need to be perfect; you need to make the *default* food minimally processed.
- **Cheapest protein per gram** in most markets: eggs, milk, plain yoghurt, chicken/thighs, canned fish, lentils/beans, and (where available) textured vegetable protein. Whey powder is a *convenience* product, not the cheapest protein.
- **No universal calorie target exists.** Estimate maintenance as body weight (kg) × 22–30 kcal depending on activity, calibrate against 2–3 weeks of real weight data, and adjust.

## 2. What the evidence establishes

### 2.1 Protein: amount
- Meta-regression across 49 RCTs: hypertrophy benefits plateau at **~1.6 g/kg/day** (95% CI 1.03–2.20) (Morton 2018, BJSM — High).
- In an energy deficit, higher protein improves fat-free-mass retention (Cava 2017; Weinheimer 2010 — Moderate) and physique-sport guidance recommends **2.3–3.1 g/kg fat-free mass** during aggressive dieting (Helms 2014 — Low-Moderate).
- Older adults show blunted anabolic response and benefit from **1.2–1.6+ g/kg** with resistance training (Moderate).
- Very high protein (>3 g/kg) offers no further benefit for body composition and is unnecessary (Moderate).

### 2.2 Protein: distribution and source
- **Distribution:** 3–4 feedings of ~0.4 g/kg (≈30–40 g) is at least as good as skewed patterns; total daily intake dominates (Moderate).
- **Source:** animal and dairy proteins have higher leucine/essential-amino-acid density; **soy and potato proteins** perform comparably in trials; most plant proteins need ~25–50% more grams to match an animal source (Moderate). For vegetarians, use combinations (legumes + grains) and consider a soy/pea isolate.
- **Protein quality is nearly irrelevant if total intake is high enough** — that is the practical simplification.

### 2.3 Carbohydrate, fat and diet pattern
- **Carbohydrate vs fat** for fat loss: no meaningful difference when protein and calories are matched (Ge 2020; Johnston 2014 — High).
- **Fibre** at 25–30+ g/day is associated with lower all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, lower body weight, and better lipids (Reynolds 2019, Lancet — High).
- **Mediterranean-style pattern** reduced major cardiovascular events by ~30% in a large randomised trial (PREDIMED 2018, NEJM — High, with acknowledged randomisation/conduct criticisms).
- **Saturated fat** should be moderated (<10% energy) and replaced with unsaturated fat; **trans fats** avoided (guideline consensus; Moderate).
- **Added sugar and sugar-sweetened beverages** are the clearest dietary targets: liquid calories are poorly compensated for and associated with weight gain (Moderate-High).

### 2.4 Meal timing and structure
- Total intake dominates; timing is secondary. The *reliable* timing effects are: **eating late at night increases intake**, **protein at breakfast reduces later hunger**, and **meal regularity** supports adherence (Low-Moderate).
- **Meal frequency** (3 vs 6 meals) has no meaningful effect on fat loss when calories/protein are matched (Moderate).

### 2.5 Practical nutrition facts worth knowing
- **Alcohol:** 7 kcal/g; suppresses fat oxidation temporarily; disrupts sleep; increases next-day intake. The GBD analysis concluded **no level of consumption improves health** (GBD 2016 Alcohol Collaborators, Lancet 2018 — Moderate).
- **Fiber sources that actually work** and are cheap: oats, lentils, beans, psyllium, frozen vegetables, cabbage, carrots, apples, berries (frozen).
- **Water:** thirst is a poor guide when busy; keep a bottle visible.
- **Caffeine:** up to ~400 mg/day is safe for most adults; avoid after ~2pm if sleep suffers.

## 4. Effectiveness (expected magnitude)

- Protein 2 g/kg vs 1 g/kg during a 20% deficit: ~2–4 kg more fat-free mass retained over 12–16 weeks (Moderate, derived from trial ranges).
- Fibre from ~15 g to ~35 g/day: better satiety, ~1–3 mmHg lower systolic BP, small LDL reduction, and consistent weight-management benefit (Moderate-High).
- Replacing ultra-processed with minimally processed foods at the same "diet": ~300–500 kcal/day lower spontaneous intake (Moderate-High).
- Mediterranean pattern: ~30% lower major CV events over ~5 years in a high-risk population (High).

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

**The "one-page diet" that produces the fastest safe result:**

1. **Protein:** 2.0–2.4 g/kg/day, in 3–4 feedings of ≥30 g.
2. **Fibre:** 30–40 g/day (see file 09 for cheap sources).
3. **Vegetables:** 400–600 g/day, minimum 2 colours per meal.
4. **Starch:** 1–2 cupped-hand portions per meal, timed around training if convenient.
5. **Fat:** 0.6–1.0 g/kg/day, mostly unsaturated (olive oil, nuts, fish, avocado).
6. **No liquid calories** except milk.
7. **Minimally processed default:** if it has a long ingredient list and a health claim on the front, it is probably not the default.
8. **Two flexible meals per week** (inside the weekly calorie budget).

**Limitations:** this is a template, not a prescription. Men with kidney disease, gout, food allergies, diabetes on medication, or eating disorders need individualisation by a clinician or dietitian.

## 6. Free and cheap alternatives

| Expensive product | Cheap equivalent | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Whey isolate ($/g protein is high) | Milk, eggs, plain yoghurt, cottage cheese, chicken, canned tuna, lentils | Cheapest protein sources in nearly every market |
| "Greens" powders | Frozen mixed vegetables, cabbage, carrots | Far cheaper, more fibre |
| Protein bars | Boiled eggs, yoghurt, milk, tuna | Bars are candy with protein |
| Meal-replacement shakes | Milk + whey/cottage cheese + banana (blended) | ~1/3 the price |
| "Metabolic" supplements | Nothing | No evidence |
| Grass-fed/organic premium (when budget is tight) | Conventional protein and produce | Nutritional difference is small; **wash produce** |

**Cost-per-gram-protein reality check (typical high-income-market prices):** lentils/beans ≈ $0.01–0.02/g; eggs ≈ $0.04/g; milk ≈ $0.03/g; chicken ≈ $0.025–0.04/g; canned tuna ≈ $0.05/g; whey powder ≈ $0.04–0.08/g. *Prices vary hugely by country — calculate with your local prices, and remember that protein powder's real benefit is convenience, not cost.*

## 7. Practical protocol

### Daily template (85 kg man, ~2,100 kcal deficit day)
| Meal | Contents | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | 3 eggs + 200 g Greek yoghurt + 40 g oats + berries | ~40 g |
| Lunch | 200 g chicken/fish + 200 g rice or potato + 250 g vegetables + olive oil | ~50 g |
| Snack | 300 ml milk + 1 fruit (or 30 g whey) | ~15 g |
| Dinner | 200 g lean beef/fish/tofu + 300 g vegetables + 100 g legumes | ~55 g |
| **Total** | ~35 g fibre, 2.0–2.4 g/kg protein | **~160 g** |

### If you hate cooking (the low-effort version)
- Rotisserie chicken or pre-cooked chicken packs, canned tuna/salmon, frozen vegetables (microwave), pre-cooked rice pouches, eggs, cottage cheese, bagged salad, frozen berries, milk.
- **Assembly, not cooking**: 10 minutes per meal, no recipe required.

### Vegetarian/vegan adjustments
- Use **soy** (tofu, tempeh, edamame), **seitan**, **lupin**, **pea/soy isolate**, and **legumes + grains**.
- Target protein ~10–20% higher than the omnivore numbers; consider 3–5 g creatine (vegetarians have lower muscle creatine — file 16).
- Watch B12 (supplement), iron, zinc, iodine, and omega-3 (algae oil).

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**The 4-item grocery rule:** every week buy (1) a protein for each meal (eggs/dairy/meat/fish/legumes), (2) frozen vegetables, (3) a fruit, (4) a fibre base (oats/legumes). Repeat the same 5 meals. Change two meals per week for variety. This is the entire nutrition plan for most men.

---

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Chronic kidney disease:** do not raise protein without medical supervision.
- **Gout/hyperuricaemia:** limit organ meats, shellfish, beer; dairy and plant proteins are protective.
- **Food allergies:** check labels on protein powders (milk, soy, egg, gluten).
- **Eating disorders:** avoid rigid macro rules; seek professional support.
- **Diabetes on insulin/sulfonylureas:** carbohydrate changes require dose adjustment with your clinician.
- **Older adults/frail:** do not restrict protein or total intake aggressively; sarcopenia risk.
- **Pregnancy/household:** not applicable to men, but note that energy restriction is inappropriate in pregnancy.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Counting calories before fixing protein and fibre** — hunger makes counting impossible.
2. **Protein only at dinner.**
3. **Assuming "clean eating" equals a deficit** — olive oil, nuts and nut butters are calorically dense and easy to over-eat.
4. **Drinking calories** (juice, lattes, beer, smoothies).
5. **Eliminating entire food groups** you enjoy, then bingeing on them.
6. **Chasing the perfect diet** instead of the repeatable one.
7. **Ignoring ultra-processed food** while counting calories to the gram — the environment beats arithmetic.
8. **Believing carbs are uniquely fattening** (High-quality evidence says otherwise).
9. **Buying premium protein sources while under-eating total protein** — the amount matters more than the label.
10. **Not eating vegetables because "fibre supplements" exist** — supplements are a top-up, not a replacement.

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Hit protein first** (2.0–2.4 g/kg in a deficit, 1.6–2.2 at maintenance).
2. **Fibre to 30–40 g/day** with whole foods; psyllium as a cheap top-up.
3. **Make minimally processed food the default**, not the exception.
4. **No liquid calories.**
5. **Pick a diet pattern you can eat for 5 years** — Mediterranean-style, high-protein, is the best-supported default.
6. **Estimate, then calibrate:** formula → 2–3 weeks of scale trend → adjust.
7. **Shop cheap:** eggs, dairy, legumes, frozen vegetables, canned fish, chicken. That is a complete, high-protein, high-fibre, low-cost diet.
