# 21 — Maintenance After Weight Loss (Post-Diet Playbook)

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**. Companion to files 06 and 07 — this is the step-by-step playbook for the 6 months after you hit your goal.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- The **first 12 weeks after a diet ends are the highest-risk period** for regain, because hunger is still elevated while structure and monitoring usually stop.
- **Maintenance calories are lower than they were before the diet** at the same body weight (adaptive thermogenesis, reduced spontaneous movement) — expect roughly **10–20% below** your pre-diet maintenance for a while, and plan accordingly (Moderate).
- **Do not "reverse diet" aggressively or "eat big to reset metabolism"** — there is no evidence that controlled overfeeding permanently raises metabolic rate. Increase calories **gradually (100–200 kcal/week)** until weight is stable, and stop there.
- **The four things that must not change:** protein intake, resistance training, daily walking, and weekly weighing.
- **Diet breaks during the loss phase** (1–2 weeks at maintenance every 8–12 weeks) are a reasonable adherence tool and may improve maintenance outcomes (Low-Moderate). They are optional, not magic.
- **Success is 60–80% of the loss held at 2 years**, not 100%. Aim for that and you will have beaten the odds; aim for perfection and you will likely lose more than you keep.
- For men who used GLP-1 medication, this playbook is **even more important**, because regain on discontinuation is the documented norm (STEP 4; SURMOUNT-4).

---

## 2. What the evidence establishes

- **Hunger persists:** appetite hormones remain altered at 1 year post-loss (Sumithran 2011 — Moderate-High).
- **Energy expenditure is below prediction** after loss (Leibel 1995 — High in controlled settings; Fothergill 2016 — Low-Moderate at 6 years).
- **Continued intervention maintains weight:** Look AHEAD held 4.7% vs 2.1% at 8 years with continuing support (High); DPP held long-term diabetes prevention benefits despite partial regain (High).
- **Structured maintenance programmes** (self-monitoring, continued contact, activity goals) slow regain in randomised trials (de Freitas 2024 — Moderate).
- **Higher protein and lower glycaemic index** during maintenance improved weight-loss maintenance in the Diogenes trial (Larsen 2010 — Moderate; see search link in file 06).
- **Continued pharmacotherapy maintains the loss; stopping reverses it** (STEP 4; SURMOUNT-4 — High).

---

## 3. Evidence quality

**High** for the biology of regain and the effect of continuing intervention. **Moderate** for specific maintenance behaviours (protein, self-weighing, activity dose). **Low** for "reverse dieting" specifics and diet-break timing (physiologically plausible, thin evidence).

---

## 4. Effectiveness (expected magnitude)

| Strategy | 2-year outcome |
|---|---|
| No plan, "back to normal eating" | Regain of ~50% of loss |
| Weighing weekly + protein + training + steps | Hold ~70–80% of loss |
| All of the above + monthly accountability + written trigger | Hold ~80%+ (best realistic) |
| Continued GLP-1 | Hold ~100% while continued; ~2/3 regain within a year of stopping |

---

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

**The 12-week transition, in order:**

| Week | Action |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Keep everything identical to the diet except: add 100 kcal/day (from carbohydrate), stop food logging (keep weighing) |
| 3–4 | Add another 100 kcal/day; introduce 2 flexible meals/week |
| 5–6 | Add another 100 kcal/day if weight still falling >0.25%/week; check 7-day average |
| 7–8 | Stabilise: when weight is flat for 2 weeks, that is your maintenance intake — write it down |
| 9–10 | Add a third flexible meal; keep protein, training, steps unchanged |
| 11–12 | Set the maintenance range (current ±1.5 kg), write the 2-kg rule, schedule a monthly review |

**If weight starts climbing during transition:** do not panic-cut. Check adherence for 2 weeks; if the average is up >1 kg, revert to the last calorie level that held stable.

---

## 6. Free and cheap alternatives

Nothing needs to be purchased. The "maintenance product" is a **written page**: your range, your trigger, your three non-negotiables, your weekly review day. A photo of that page on your phone is the entire system.

---

## 7. Practical protocol

### Daily (5 min)
- Weigh (or ≥4×/week), log it.
- Protein at every meal (≥1.6 g/kg).
- 8,000–12,000 steps.
- No liquid calories; ≤1–2 drinks, ideally fewer.
- 7–9 h sleep; kitchen closed 3 h before bed.

### Weekly (10 min)
- 2–3 resistance sessions.
- Look at the 7-day average: inside range → do nothing. Above range top for 2 weeks → 2-week correction (file 06 §7B).
- One grocery run with the 4-item list.

### Monthly (20 min)
- Waist + photos.
- Review: training sessions completed, average steps, alcohol units, sleep average.
- One "audit meal": eat out and check that you can hold your habits socially.

### Quarterly
- Re-read files 06 and 07 (5 minutes) to keep the rules salient.
- Reassess goal: recomposition, a small further loss, or pure maintenance.

### Annual
- Blood pressure, bloods (file 25), and a fitness benchmark (e.g., 5 km walk time, number of push-ups, or a 1-rep-max proxy).

---

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**Weigh weekly. Protein at every meal. Lift twice. Walk daily. Correct at +2 kg.** That is the permanent, five-minute-a-week version of this entire library.

---

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Do not use drugs, diuretics, laxatives or "detox" to correct a small regain** — see file 23.
- **Avoid very low body fat maintenance** (<8–10%): hormonal, sleep, immune and libido costs; and it makes maintenance psychologically brutal.
- **Watch for disordered eating patterns** (rigid rules, guilt, secret eating, compensatory exercise). Maintenance should reduce distress, not increase it.
- **Older men:** prioritise protein + resistance training to protect muscle/bone; avoid repeated loss-regain cycles.
- **If weight rises rapidly despite unchanged habits**, see a clinician (fluid, medication effects, endocrine).

---

## 10. Products and tools

Scale, tape measure, a notes app or paper log. Optional: a step counter (phone). Nothing else is needed, and no subscription improves the outcome.

---

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Ending the diet and the structure on the same day.**
2. **"Reverse dieting" by +800 kcal/week** — indigestion, bloating, rapid regain, confusion.
3. **Stopping training** because "the diet is over".
4. **Dropping protein to "normal" levels.**
5. **Abandoning weighing** — regain becomes invisible for months.
6. **Treating flexible meals as "cheat days"** that undo the week.
7. **Not writing the range/trigger down** — you will not act in the moment without it.
8. **Judging by how clothes feel** rather than data.
9. **Restarting a crash diet at the first 1 kg blip.**
10. **Planning nothing for holidays/travel/illness** — the four predictable disruptors.

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Transition over 8–12 weeks**, adding 100–200 kcal/week.
2. **Keep protein, training, walking, weighing** — permanently.
3. **Write your range and trigger** (goal ±1.5 kg; correct at +2 kg for 2 weeks).
4. **Aim to hold 70–80%** of the loss; that is success.
5. **Review monthly, correct early, never crash.**
6. **If you used medication:** build this system *before* stopping, not after.
