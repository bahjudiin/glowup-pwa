# 07 — Hydration, Electrolytes and Digestion

*Phase 2 module, file 7 of 12. Fibre as an appetite tool is in `02` §3.4; psyllium as a product in `09` §4; fasting-related digestive changes follow `03`.*

---

## 1. Purpose

This file covers the boring, high-impact plumbing of fat loss: **water, salt, potassium, magnesium, fibre, bowel function and the daily weight fluctuations** that make people quit. It also gives a clear verdict on psyllium — the only supplement in this module with a genuine evidence base for both constipation and cholesterol.

Two recurring themes:
1. **Most people need less intervention than the supplement industry claims**, and **more fibre than they eat**.
2. **Water and electrolytes have a safe range with real upper limits** — "more" is not "better," and unsupervised high-dose electrolytes are a genuine hazard.

---

## 2. Hydration without the hype

### 2.1 What is actually established
- **Water has no calories, and pre-meal water can reduce intake.** A systematic review of water intake and energy intake/weight status found evidence that water (and replacing caloric beverages with water) supports lower energy intake ([Daniels & Popkin 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20796216/)); in one randomised trial, 500 ml before meals produced about **2 kg greater weight loss** over 12 weeks ([Dennis 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19661958/)).
- **Liquid calories are poorly compensated**, which is why replacing juice/soft drinks with water is one of the most reliable single changes available ([Cassady 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22258267/)).

### 2.2 What is not established
- "8 glasses a day" as a universal rule.
- That drinking more water "speeds metabolism" in any meaningful way.
- That cold water burns significant calories.
- That you must drink a specific amount to "flush fat."

### 2.3 Practical hydration rules
1. **Drink to thirst, plus a glass before each main meal.**
2. **Target pale-straw urine**, not clear.
3. **Drink more with heat, sweating, manual work, and fasting** — Mogadishu heat is a real modifier.
4. **Do not drink litres in an hour.** Rapid large-volume water intake without sodium can dilute serum sodium. Exercise-associated hyponatraemia is documented and its consensus statement is explicit that over-drinking (not dehydration) is the main cause in endurance settings ([Hew-Butler 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26227507/)).
5. **Water is not a meal.** Drinking 1.5 L before eating to "fill the stomach" causes discomfort and does not meaningfully extend satiety compared with a normal pre-meal glass.

## 3. Sodium, potassium, magnesium — what's useful, unnecessary, dangerous, or obtainable from food

| Electrolyte | Role | Useful? | Unnecessary? | Dangerous? | Obtainable from food? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sodium** (salt) | Fluid balance, nerve/muscle function; lost in sweat | **Yes** — in heat/manual labour, long fasts, or heavy sweating, a pinch of salt in water is reasonable | Yes — salt tablets/electrolyte powders taken on a normal diet with no sweating | **Yes** — high-dose salt loading raises BP; salt water "flushes" are dangerous | Yes — salt used in cooking |
| **Potassium** | Cellular function, blood pressure, muscle contraction | **Yes** — from food. Higher potassium intake is part of a blood-pressure-friendly pattern | Yes as a supplement for healthy people | **Yes** — potassium supplements, especially with kidney disease, ACE inhibitors, ARBs or spironolactone, can cause dangerous hyperkalaemia. **Do not self-supplement.** | Yes — potato (535 mg/100 g), spinach (558), white beans (561), tomato paste (1014), banana, orange, lentils |
| **Magnesium** | Cofactor, neuromuscular function | **Sometimes** — dietary adequacy matters. Supplement evidence for cramps/sleep is weak and inconsistent | Largely yes for most people | **Yes** in kidney disease; high doses cause diarrhoea | Yes — nuts, seeds, legumes, whole grains, greens |
| **"Electrolyte packets"** | Convenience | Rarely — only for heavy sweating, long fasts, or illness | Usually yes for 16:8 fasting | Yes if overused with a normal diet | Not needed |
| **Bicarbonate / "alkaline water"** | Nothing relevant to fat loss | No | Yes | Not usually | No |

**The blunt summary:** on a normal diet you get plenty of sodium, potassium and magnesium from food. **Electrolyte products exist to solve a problem most dieters do not have.** Use salt when you sweat heavily or fast > 24 h; use food for potassium and magnesium; do not take potassium or magnesium supplements without a reason and, for potassium, without medical input.

## 4. Fibre, constipation, bloating, gas, diarrhoea

### 4.1 The fibre target and the adaptation problem
- **25–29 g/day** is the range associated with the best outcomes in the largest systematic review, with benefits continuing above 30 g/day ([Reynolds 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30638909/)).
- The problem is **speed of increase**. Going from 10 g to 35 g in a week reliably produces gas, bloating and either constipation or loose stools.
- **Rule:** +5 g per week, with water increasing alongside.

### 4.2 Constipation (very common on a deficit)
Causes: total food volume down, fibre down, water down, dairy up, iron supplements, low activity, hypothyroidism (medical), medications.
**What works:**
- **Soluble, gel-forming fibre** — psyllium is the best-evidenced single agent: it improves stool frequency and consistency in chronic constipation ([Suares 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21332763/)), and dietary fibre interventions improve symptoms generally ([Christodoulides 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27170558/)).
- **Fluid** with the fibre (psyllium without water can worsen things).
- **Movement** (walking).
- **Kiwi/prunes** have trial support in constipation (not verified in this project's citation set — treat as low-cost trial-and-see).
**What doesn't:** "detox" teas (often stimulant laxatives — **do not use regularly**), excess insoluble bran without fluid, and coffee as a strategy.

### 4.3 Bloating and gas
Mostly from **fermentable carbohydrates** (beans, lentils, cabbage, onion) and swallowed air (eating fast, carbonated drinks). Practical sequence:
1. **Halve the portion** of the culprit food; keep it in the diet at a tolerated dose (tolerance often improves in 2–4 weeks).
2. **Soak and rinse legumes**; cook them thoroughly; discard soaking water.
3. **Cook cabbage/broccoli** instead of eating large raw volumes.
4. **Eat slower** (also helps intake).
5. **Reduce/stop carbonated drinks and sugar alcohols** (sorbitol, xylitol, maltitol — common in "sugar-free" products).
6. If symptoms are severe, persistent, or accompanied by weight loss, blood, or pain — **see a doctor.** That is not "bloating from fibre."

### 4.4 Diarrhoea
On a deficit, usual causes: too much fibre too fast, sugar alcohols, excess caffeine, high-fat meals after fasting, food/water hygiene, magnesium supplements, and (importantly) infection. **Persistent diarrhoea is a medical issue, and in a hot climate dehydration follows quickly.**

### 4.5 Digestive changes during fasting
- **Less frequent bowel movements** are expected when you eat less; this is not automatically constipation unless stools are hard/painful.
- **Breaking a long fast with a large fatty or high-fibre meal commonly causes cramps and urgent diarrhoea.** Break gently (`04`).
- **Coffee on an empty stomach** stimulates gastric acid and colonic motility in some people — fine for some, reflux/diarrhoea for others.
- **Gallbladder:** repeated prolonged fasting increases gallstone risk; this is a reason not to make 36–48 h fasts a routine.

## 5. Water retention, glycogen, and why the scale lies

| Phenomenon | What happens | Magnitude | Time course |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Glycogen depletion** | Each gram of glycogen holds ~3 g water; low-carb/low-calorie days deplete glycogen | 1–3 kg of scale weight | First 3–7 days; reverses with a carb meal |
| **Sodium load** | A salty meal retains water | 0.5–1.5 kg | 24–72 h |
| **New exercise** | Muscle damage → inflammation and water | 0.5–2 kg | 3–10 days after starting |
| **Constipation** | Retained stool | 0.3–1 kg | Until resolved |
| **Menstrual cycle** (if applicable to your household/partner context) | Hormonal water shifts | 0.5–2 kg | Cyclical, predictable |
| **Fasting/refeed** | Water and glycogen drop then rebound | 1–2 kg | 1–3 days after the fast ends |
| **Actual fat gain** | Requires a sustained surplus | ~0.1–0.5 kg/week max realistically | Slow |

**The consequence for measurement:** a 1.5 kg overnight rise is **not** fat. Judge progress on the **7-day average of daily weights** plus **waist circumference weekly** (`08` §4).

## 6. Psyllium — the honest assessment

**What it is:** the husk of *Plantago ovata* seed, a gel-forming soluble fibre.

**Evidence, by outcome:**

| Outcome | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Constipation** | Improves stool frequency/consistency; among the better-evidenced fibre supplements ([Suares 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21332763/); [Christodoulides 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27170558/)) | **Good** |
| **LDL cholesterol** | Meta-analysis of RCTs shows a modest, consistent lowering of LDL and non-HDL cholesterol ([Jovanovski 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30239559/)) | **Good** (small effect) |
| **Glycaemia** | Modest improvements in some trials (see [Xiao 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31919936/)) | **Moderate** |
| **Weight / body fat** | Small effects at best; a meta-analysis in diabetic patients found effects on weight/BMI/lipids/glucose but the weight effect is small ([Xiao 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31919936/)) | **Weak for weight** |
| **Appetite** | Plausible via viscosity/gastric emptying; less consistent than food-based fibre | **Weak–moderate** |

**How to use it:** start with **3–5 g once daily** in a large glass of water (≥ 250 ml), then increase to 5–10 g/day if needed. Take it **30–60 minutes before a meal** if the goal is appetite, or at a consistent time if the goal is bowel regularity.

**Contraindications and interactions (important):**
- **Do not take with dry mouthfuls or without fluid** — oesophageal obstruction and choking are documented risks.
- **Avoid** with known oesophageal stricture, swallowing difficulty, bowel obstruction, or severe constipation of unknown cause.
- **Separate from medicines by ~2 hours** — psyllium can reduce absorption of some drugs (notably certain thyroid medications, lithium, digoxin, carbamazepine, and iron). **If you take any daily medication, confirm timing with a pharmacist.**
- **Start low** — it causes gas/bloating in the first week.
- **Do not use it as a meal replacement.**

## 7. What's useful vs unnecessary vs dangerous (quick reference)

| Category | Useful | Unnecessary | Dangerous |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water | Pre-meal glass; drinking to thirst; more in heat | Forcing 4+ litres; "water fasting" beyond 24–48 h unsupervised | Rapid large-volume intake → hyponatraemia |
| Sodium | Pinch of salt in heat/long fasts; normal cooking salt | Salt tablets on a normal diet; "salt water flush" | High-dose salt loading with hypertension/heart or kidney disease |
| Potassium | Food sources | Supplements for healthy people | Potassium supplements with kidney disease/ACEi/ARB/spironolactone |
| Magnesium | Food sources | "Magnesium for sleep/cramps" as a reliable fix | High doses in kidney disease |
| Fibre | Gradual increase to 25–35 g/day | Fibre gummies/"detox" fibres | Stimulant laxatives as a routine |
| Probiotics | Possibly after antibiotics, or specific IBS strains | General "gut health" products for fat loss | Unregulated products in immunocompromised people |
| Digestive enzymes | Specific medical conditions | Fat-loss use | — |
| Laxatives | Constipation, short-term, under advice | "Cleanses" | **Chronic use, use for weight control** — causes dependence and electrolyte loss |

## 8. Summary

### 8.1 Strongest verified findings
1. **Water instead of caloric drinks reduces energy intake**, and pre-meal water produced ~2 kg extra loss in one RCT ([Daniels 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20796216/); [Dennis 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19661958/)).
2. **Over-drinking water can cause exercise-associated hyponatraemia** — documented in a formal consensus statement ([Hew-Butler 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26227507/)). More is not better.
3. **Psyllium improves constipation and modestly lowers LDL** ([Suares 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21332763/); [Jovanovski 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30239559/)); its weight effect is small.
4. **25–29 g/day fibre** is the best-supported intake range ([Reynolds 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30638909/)).
5. **Most scale jumps are water, glycogen, sodium or stool — not fat** (§5).

### 8.2 Most practical actions
1. Water before meals; pale-straw urine; more in heat.
2. Salt only when sweating heavily or fasting long; potassium and magnesium from food.
3. Fibre +5 g/week to 25–35 g/day, with water.
4. Psyllium 5 g/day if constipated (with a full glass of water, 2 h from medication).
5. Weigh daily, judge on the 7-day average; measure waist weekly.

### 8.3 Cheapest effective options
Water, salt from the kitchen, potatoes/spinach/beans for potassium, lentils and cabbage for fibre, walking. Psyllium (~$0.17/serving, `09`) is the only purchase with a real case.

### 8.4 What is unnecessary
Electrolyte powders for routine fasting; alkaline water; "detox" teas; colon cleanses; probiotic supplements for weight loss; magnesium for sleep as a certainty; potassium supplements.

### 8.5 What is unsupported or misleading
- "You need 4 litres of water a day to lose fat" — no.
- "Salt water flushes toxins" — no; it is a laxative effect and an electrolyte risk.
- "Bloating means your diet isn't working" — usually it means fibre increased too fast or a FODMAP load.
- "Psyllium absorbs fat calories" — no.
- "You must supplement electrolytes on 16:8" — no.

### 8.6 Important risks
Hyponatraemia from over-drinking or salt manipulation; hyperkalaemia from potassium supplements with kidney disease or RAAS-blocking drugs; oesophageal obstruction from dry psyllium; drug binding by psyllium; laxative dependence; persistent diarrhoea/constipation as a sign of underlying disease; gallstones with repeated prolonged fasting.

### 8.7 Concise action plan
1. Put a glass of water beside your plate at every meal; drink it before you eat.
2. Add one fibre source per week (lentils → oats → cabbage → guava) until ~30 g/day.
3. Use salt in cooking; add a pinch to water only when sweating heavily.
4. If constipated for > 3 days: psyllium 5 g + water + a walk. If > 1 week or painful, see a doctor.
5. Stop all "detox"/laxative products permanently.
6. Record weights daily for 2 weeks and calculate the weekly average — then stop reacting to single days.
