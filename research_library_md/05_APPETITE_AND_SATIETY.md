# 05 — Appetite Suppression & Satiety (Low Cost)

> Part of the **Men's Transformation Research Library**.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- The four **reliable, cheap** levers for hunger are, in order of evidence strength: **protein**, **fibre (especially viscous/soluble fibre)**, **food volume / low energy density**, and **sleep**. Together they typically remove **300–700 kcal/day** of spontaneous intake for a man — the same order of magnitude as many appetite-suppressant products, without the cost or risk.
- **Protein** is the strongest single dietary lever: raising protein to ~30% of energy caused a spontaneous ~440 kcal/day reduction in intake and ~5 kg weight loss over 12 weeks without deliberate restriction (Weigle 2005 — Moderate).
- **Fibre** at 25–30+ g/day is associated with lower body weight and better health outcomes (Reynolds 2019, Lancet — High for health outcomes; Moderate for weight).
- **Energy density** (kcal per gram of food) is one of the most consistent predictors of how much people eat; adding water-rich foods (soup, salad, vegetables, fruit) lowers intake without counting (Rolls' programme of laboratory RCTs — Moderate-High).
- **Sleep extension** cut objectively measured intake by ~270 kcal/day in overweight adults (Tasali 2022 — Moderate-High). This is the cheapest "appetite suppressant" in existence.
- **Caffeine** has a small, unreliable effect on appetite; **water before meals** produces a modest, real effect; **eating slowly** reduces intake within a meal.
- **Appetite-suppressant supplements** (garcinia, raspberry ketone, hoodia, "detox" products, stimulant blends) have **no meaningful evidence** and real risk. Nothing over the counter is remotely equivalent to a GLP-1 drug (see files 17 and 23).

## 2. What the evidence establishes

### 2.1 Protein
- Ad libitum high-protein diets produce sustained reductions in appetite and spontaneous intake (Weigle 2005, AJCN — Moderate) and higher protein intakes raise satiety per calorie in controlled feeding studies (Halton 2004, J Am Coll Nutr — review, Moderate).
- **Practical magnitude:** adding 30–50 g/day of protein typically displaces 150–400 kcal/day of other intake.
- Practical threshold: **≥30 g protein at breakfast** reduces later-day intake more than the same protein at dinner (Low-Moderate; timing studies are mixed).

### 2.2 Fibre
- Dose-response meta-analysis of 185 cohorts + 58 trials: greatest risk reduction at **25–29 g/day** of fibre, with additional benefit above 30 g (Reynolds 2019, Lancet — High). Higher fibre intake is associated with **lower body weight**.
- Soluble/viscous fibres increase perceived satiety and reduce short-term energy intake (Salleh 2019, Foods — Moderate but small studies).
- **Psyllium** specifically increased satiety in controlled studies (Brum 2016, Appetite — Moderate) and improves glycaemic control; it is the cheapest well-evidenced fibre supplement.
- **Glucomannan** (a highly viscous fibre) produced **less than 1 kg** additional weight loss in short trials vs placebo, with larger lipid effects and frequent GI side effects (Sood 2008, AJCN meta-analysis — Low-Moderate). It is optional, not a "natural GLP-1".

### 2.3 Food volume and energy density
- Across laboratory RCTs, people eat a **fairly constant weight of food**; lowering energy density (more water/air/fibre per gram) lowers intake without hunger (Rolls; summarised in Rolls 2025, Physiol Behav — Moderate-High).
- Practical examples with good evidence: **soup or salad as a first course** reduces total meal intake; **fruit and vegetables** as the first thing eaten reduce intake of the energy-dense part of the meal.
- Beverage calories are poorly compensated for: liquid calories increase total intake more than solid calories (Flood 2006, J Am Diet Assoc — Moderate). **Rule: don't drink calories.**

### 2.4 Sleep
- Sleep extension to ~8.5 h in short-sleeping overweight adults reduced objectively measured energy intake by **~270 kcal/day**, with no change in activity (Tasali 2022, JAMA Intern Med — Moderate-High).
- Short sleep raises ghrelin and lowers leptin, increasing hunger and appetite (Spiegel 2004, Ann Intern Med — Moderate).

### 2.5 Behavioural and environmental
- **Eating rate:** faster eating is associated with higher obesity risk in a large Japanese cohort meta-analysis (Ohkuma 2015, Int J Obes — Moderate) and slower eating reduces within-meal intake in controlled studies (Andrade 2008 — Low-Moderate).
- **Water before meals:** 500 ml before each meal plus a hypocaloric diet produced about **2 kg greater weight loss over 12 weeks** than diet alone in middle-aged/older adults (n=48) (Dennis 2010, Obesity — Moderate; small trial).
- **Menu labelling/calorie information** has small effects on calories ordered (Sinclair 2014; Cantu-Jungles 2017 — Low-Moderate).
- **Coffee/caffeine:** modest reduction in intake in some studies, no effect in others; useful mainly as a low-calorie replacement for sugary drinks (Schubert 2014, Appetite — Low).
- **Food environment** is the highest-leverage behaviour change for most men: what is in the house, what is visible, what is pre-portioned. No willpower required.

### 2.6 What does not work
| Intervention | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Garcinia cambogia, raspberry ketone, forskolin, CLA, "detox" teas | **No meaningful evidence**; some hepatotoxicity reports for green-tea extract |
| Stimulant "thermogenics" | Small, transient effect; cardiovascular risk; banned substances in some products |
| Hoodia, appetite-suppressant "herbal" blends | No reliable evidence; safety concerns |
| Very high water intake as an appetite tool | Weak; some benefit pre-meal only |
| Chewing gum / small distractions | Trivial effect |
| Apple cider vinegar | Small glycaemic effect; no meaningful weight effect (umbrella review, 2026 — low) |

## 5. Fastest reasonably safe approach

**The hunger-control stack, in order of implementation:**
1. **Protein 2.0–2.4 g/kg/day**, ≥30–40 g at breakfast.
2. **Fibre 30–40 g/day** from whole foods; **psyllium 5 g** before the hungriest meal if short.
3. **Fill half the plate with vegetables/fruit; start every meal with the high-volume, low-calorie component** (soup, salad, vegetables).
4. **No liquid calories.**
5. **Sleep 7.5–9 h** with a fixed wake time.
6. **Eat at a table, from a plate, without screens**; keep serving dishes off the table; do not stock trigger foods.
7. **Coffee/tea** as appetite-neutral substitutes for snacks.
8. **500 ml water** 15–20 min before main meals.

None of these is as powerful as a GLP-1 receptor agonist — but they are additive, free, and safe, and they are what makes the deficit bearable.

## 7. Practical protocol

### The "full-plate" template (no counting)
- **Half the plate:** non-starchy vegetables or salad, eaten **first**.
- **Quarter:** protein, palm-and-a-half sized (30–50 g protein).
- **Quarter:** starch (potato, rice, bread, pasta) — or fruit.
- **Fat:** one thumb of oil/nuts/cheese.
- **Drink:** 500 ml water before the meal; zero-calorie drinks during.
- **Speed:** put the fork down between bites; aim for 20+ minutes per meal.

### Daily fibre plan (to ~35 g)
- 40–60 g oats or 2 slices dense wholegrain bread (~6–8 g)
- 1 portion legumes (lentils/chickpeas/beans, 150 g cooked ~6–8 g)
- 2 portions fruit (~5–8 g)
- 300 g+ vegetables (~6–8 g)
- Optional: 5 g psyllium in water before the hungriest meal (~4 g)

### Hunger troubleshooting
| Symptom | Most likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Ravenous at 4pm | Protein/fibre too low at lunch; poor sleep | Bigger protein lunch; psyllium; fix sleep |
| Cannot stop eating at night | Under-eating during the day; no wind-down routine | Larger breakfast/lunch; earlier last meal; brush teeth after dinner |
| Constant cravings for sweets | Large deficit + low protein + restriction | Reduce deficit; add fruit/dairy; stop "saving" calories for junk |
| Hunger fine but no weight loss | Intake creeping (oils, drinks, bites, weekends) | Two-week food log; weigh food briefly |

## 8. Low-maintenance version

**Three rules, forever:** (1) protein at every meal; (2) vegetables first, half the plate; (3) no liquid calories. Add **sleep 7.5+ h** and **psyllium only when hunger spikes**. That is 90% of the achievable satiety benefit with essentially no tracking.

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- **Fibre:** increase gradually over 2–3 weeks and increase fluids; rapid fibre loading causes bloating, gas, and (rarely) obstruction in people with strictures or prior bowel surgery. **Contraindicated** in bowel obstruction, severe dysphagia, and some post-surgical states. Psyllium should always be taken with a full glass of water.
- **Glucomannan:** can cause oesophageal obstruction if taken with insufficient fluid; avoid with swallowing disorders and before bed.
- **High protein:** requires medical advice in chronic kidney disease; adequate hydration needed; kidney stones (calcium oxalate) may warrant attention to oxalate sources and fluid.
- **Caffeine:** limit to <400 mg/day (roughly 4 cups of coffee); avoid in uncontrolled hypertension, arrhythmia, anxiety disorders, and after mid-afternoon if sleep is affected.
- **Weight-loss supplements:** avoid stimulants, diuretics, laxatives, and proprietary blends. Green tea extract has caused liver injury. Avoid anything marketed as "legal speed".
- **Do not use** appetite suppression to mask an eating disorder, and do not use "hunger is weakness" framing — persistent severe hunger is a sign the plan is wrong.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Trying to win hunger with willpower** instead of protein, fibre, volume and sleep.
2. **Low protein at breakfast**, then fighting hunger all day.
3. **Drinking calories** — juice, lattes, beer, shakes.
4. **Fibre loading overnight** — bloating and abandonment.
5. **Buying appetite-suppressant supplements** — money for nothing, occasionally harm.
6. **Confusing thirst with hunger** (mild effect) or, more commonly, **snacking out of habit/boredom** rather than hunger.
7. **Keeping trigger foods in the house** and relying on restraint.
8. **Under-sleeping** and blaming "weak discipline".
9. **Skipping meals then overeating at night** — a predictable cycle, fixable with meal structure.

---

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Protein first:** 2.0–2.4 g/kg/day, front-loaded at breakfast.
2. **Fibre to 30–40 g/day**; psyllium (cheap) when you're short.
3. **Volume first at every meal** — vegetables/soup/salad before the calorie-dense food.
4. **Zero liquid calories.**
5. **Sleep 7.5–9 h** — the free appetite suppressant.
6. **Design the environment** (what's in the kitchen, what's on the counter, what's pre-portioned).
7. **Buy nothing** marketed as an appetite suppressant.
