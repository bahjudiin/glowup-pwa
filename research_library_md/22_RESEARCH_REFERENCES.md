## 2. What the evidence establishes

### 2.1 How to use this index

1. Find the PMID in the list below and open it: `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/<PMID>/`.
2. Check the **publication type** (RCT, meta-analysis, review, animal) — the library's grades assume you know which you are reading.
3. For guidelines, use the canonical URL in §3.

## 3. Evidence quality

Grades are attached to claims in the topic files, not here. The distribution of sources is deliberate: the highest-cited anchors in this library are **meta-analyses, large RCTs and clinical guidelines**, and animal/mechanistic papers are cited only to explain plausibility.

## 7. Practical protocol

1. Identify the claim you are checking. 2. Find its row above. 3. Open the PMID. 4. Confirm the study type and whether the outcome measured is the one you care about. 5. If it is a review, ask what the underlying trials showed.

## 9. Safety and contraindications

- A citation does not equal a recommendation. Some cited trials describe interventions (GLP-1 drugs, bariatric surgery, prescription retinoids, finasteride, testosterone) that require medical supervision.
- Animal and in-vitro studies are cited only for plausibility and must not be used to justify self-treatment.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. Citing a paper you have not opened.
2. Treating a review as a trial, or a trial as a guideline.
3. Ignoring the population studied (sex, age, BMI, training status).
4. Using an animal study to settle a human question.
5. Thinking a PMID makes a claim true — check design, size, duration and funding.

## 12. Final recommendations
