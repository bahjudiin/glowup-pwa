## 1. The purchasing principle

> **Free → cheap food → cheap tools → supplements last.**

Anything on this page that costs money has to justify itself against a free alternative. Most items fail that test. The five that pass are marked **✅ Worth buying** and the rest are listed so you can consciously *not* buy them.

**Ordering note for Somalia:** iHerb ships internationally but with long transit times, customs uncertainty and shipping costs that can exceed the product price; Amazon US/UK shipping to Mogadishu is usually expensive and unreliable. **Prices below are retail prices in USD as verified in the Phase-1 research session — they are not delivered prices to Somalia.** For anything heavy (whey, 2 lb psyllium), the *delivered* cost may be double. Where a local or regional equivalent exists (eggs, chicken, a cheap plastic scale from a phone-accessory shop, a cloth eye mask), buy locally.

**Link policy:** iHerb product URLs below were **verified live on 1 Nov 2026** during Phase-1 research, with prices marked **✅ verified**. Items without a verifiable product page are given as **clearly-labelled marketplace search links** — never as invented product links. No affiliate links are used.

## 5. Priority 4 — Supplements (last, and few)

Only products with real human evidence relevant to *this* module are listed. Phase-1 files `16` and `18` contain the wider supplement review — these are the only ones that survive.

### 5.1 Psyllium husk — **✅ Worth buying** (the best-value supplement in this module)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Constipation, modest LDL reduction, small appetite/fibre-gap help |
| **Evidence** | **Good** for stool frequency/consistency ([Suares 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21332763/)); **good but small** for LDL ([Jovanovski 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30239559/)); **weak** for weight ([Xiao 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31919936/)) |
| **Best value option** | NOW Foods Psyllium Husk Powder 12 oz — **✅ $11.30 verified 1 Nov 2026**: <https://www.iherb.com/pr/now-foods-psyllium-husk-powder-12-oz-340-g/8934> |
| **Budget/bulk option** | Same brand 24 oz — **✅ $21.31** (~$0.16/serving): <https://www.iherb.com/pr/now-foods-psyllium-husk-powder-24-oz-680-g/21133> · Micro Ingredients Organic 2 lb — **✅ $28.99**: <https://www.iherb.com/pr/micro-ingredients-organic-psyllium-husk-powder-2-lb-907-g/149845> · Viva Naturals Organic 24 oz — **✅ $23.39**: <https://www.iherb.com/pr/viva-naturals-organic-psyllium-husk-powder-24-oz-680-g/113541> |
| **Cost per serving** | ~$0.16–0.17 per 5 g serving |
| **Safety** | Must be taken with a full glass of water; avoid with swallowing disorders/oesophageal stricture/bowel obstruction; **separate from medication by ~2 h** (thyroid meds, lithium, digoxin, carbamazepine, iron); start at 3–5 g/day |
| **Free alternative** | Lentils, oats, okra, cabbage, guava — cheaper per gram of fibre, plus protein and micronutrients |

### 5.2 Whey / protein powder — **◻ Conditional**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Convenience for hitting 110–140 g protein/day |
| **Evidence** | Protein itself is well-supported; **the powder is not the evidence** — total protein is ([Morton 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)) |
| **Best value option** | California Gold Nutrition Sport Whey Protein Isolate, unflavoured, 5 lb — **✅ $105.00 verified**: <https://www.iherb.com/pr/california-gold-nutrition-sport-whey-protein-isolate-unflavored-5-lb-2-27-kg/76479> (~$1.40 per 30 g protein) |
| **Budget option** | 1 lb size — **✅ $30.80** (worse per serving). Plain **whey concentrate** is usually cheaper per gram of protein — marketplace search: <https://www.iherb.com/search?kw=whey+protein+concentrate> |
| **Cheaper alternatives** | Eggs, chicken, goat, fish, lentils, milk — **always cheaper per gram of protein in this market** |
| **Safety** | Fine for most; avoid with milk-protein allergy; lactose intolerance → isolate or non-dairy |
| **Verdict** | Buy **only** if you genuinely cannot eat enough protein from food, or need a shelf-stable option for work/travel |

### 5.3 Creatine monohydrate — **◻ Conditional (training only)**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Training performance and lean-mass support during resistance training |
| **Evidence** | Moderate and specific to training/lean mass — **not a fat-loss supplement** |
| **Best value** | California Gold Nutrition Creatine 1 kg — **✅ $32.15** (~$0.16/day): <https://www.iherb.com/pr/california-gold-nutrition-sport-pure-creatine-monohydrate-unflavored-2-2-lbs-1-kg/143718> · NOW Foods Micronized 500 g — **✅ $22.05**: <https://www.iherb.com/pr/now-foods-sports-micronized-creatine-monohydrate-1-1-lbs-500-g/687> · Optimum Nutrition 600 g — **✅ $27.99**: <https://www.iherb.com/pr/optimum-nutrition-micronized-creatine-powder-unflavored-1-32-lb-600-g/68616> |
| **Safety** | Long-established safety at 3–5 g/day in healthy people; **avoid/ask a doctor** with kidney disease; causes ~1 kg water-weight gain (which will look like a "stall" on the scale) |
| **Verdict** | Only if you are doing resistance training consistently. Buy plain monohydrate; ignore HCl/ethyl-ester/"buffered" versions |

### 5.4 Vitamin D3 — **◻ Conditional**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Correcting deficiency; not a fat-loss aid |
| **Evidence** | Deficiency correction is well supported; fat-loss claims are not |
| **Best value** | NOW D-3 2000 IU 240 softgels — **✅ $8.83** (~$0.04/day): <https://www.iherb.com/pr/now-foods-vitamin-d-3-high-potency-2-000-iu-240-softgels/16272> · CGN 90 ct — **✅ $4.92**: <https://www.iherb.com/pr/california-gold-nutrition-vitamin-d3-50-mcg-2-000-iu-90-fish-gelatin-softgels/77548> |
| **Safer approach** | **Get a blood level first** if possible. Clothing and indoor work make deficiency plausible here, but blind high-dose supplementation is not ideal |
| **Verdict** | Reasonable low-cost option; do not expect it to affect weight |

### 5.5 Electrolytes — **❌ Usually not worth buying**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Replacing sodium/potassium/magnesium lost in heavy sweat or long fasts |
| **Evidence** | Legitimate in endurance/heat/long-fasting contexts; **no evidence of benefit on a normal diet with normal sweating** |
| **Risk** | Over-use with a normal diet adds unnecessary sodium; high-dose potassium products are hazardous with kidney disease or ACE inhibitors/ARBs/spironolactone; over-drinking fluids with or without electrolytes causes hyponatraemia ([Hew-Butler 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26227507/)) |
| **Cheaper option** | A pinch of salt in water + food potassium (potato, spinach, beans, tomato paste) |
| **Verdict** | Buy only if you do heavy physical work in heat or fast > 24 h regularly. Even then, salt + food is enough |

### 5.6 What this file deliberately does **not** recommend

| Product class | Why not |
|---|---|
| Fat burners / "thermogenics" | Unproven or unsafe; stimulant risks; no durable effect |
| Appetite suppressant supplements (garcinia, hoodia, saffron, raspberry ketone, 5-HTP) | Evidence absent or weak; some have safety concerns |
| "GLP-1 booster" / "natural Ozempic" products | **No food or supplement has been shown to produce GLP-1-drug-level weight loss.** Marketing only |
| Detox/cleansing products, slimming teas | Laxative effects, electrolyte loss, no benefit |
| Testosterone boosters | Not supported; Phase-1 `16` covers this |
| Collagen for skin/fat loss | Modest evidence for skin elasticity at best; irrelevant to fat loss |
| Ketone esters/"exogenous ketones" | No fat-loss benefit |
| BCAA/EAA when protein is adequate | Redundant |
| Meal replacement shakes as a diet strategy | No advantage over food; worse satiety per calorie |
| "Autophagy activator" supplements | No human outcome evidence |
| Waist trainers, sweat suits, sauna belts | Dehydration, not fat loss — and hazardous |

## 7. Summary

### 7.1 Strongest verified findings
1. **Psyllium** improves constipation and modestly lowers LDL ([Suares 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21332763/); [Jovanovski 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30239559/)); weight effects are small ([Xiao 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31919936/)).
2. **Protein powder is not magic** — the benefit is total protein ([Morton 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)), and food protein is cheaper here.
3. **Creatine supports training, not fat loss.**
4. **Water preloading is free and has trial support** ([Dennis 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19661958/)).
5. **Electrolyte products solve a problem most dieters do not have**, and over-use carries real risk ([Hew-Butler 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26227507/)).

### 7.2 Most practical actions
Buy a kitchen scale. Use eggs/chicken/lentils instead of powder. Add psyllium only if constipated. Use salt + food for electrolytes. Skip everything else.

### 7.3 Cheapest effective options
All of Priority 1 and 2 above; psyllium at ~$0.16/serving is the only supplement with a clear cost-justified role.

### 7.4 What is unnecessary
Fat burners, appetite-suppressant supplements, "natural GLP-1" products, detox teas, collagen for fat loss, exogenous ketones, BCAAs, body-fat scales, smart rings, CGMs (non-diabetic), waist trainers.

### 7.5 What is unsupported or misleading
- Any supplement marketed as producing drug-like GLP-1 effects.
- "Detox" products — the liver and kidneys do this.
- Products promising fat loss "without diet or exercise."
- Verified-looking prices for products shipped to Somalia: **none of the prices here include international shipping, customs, or local markup.**

### 7.6 Important risks
Fake or adulterated supplements (buy sealed, from a reputable seller); psyllium choking/drug-binding; potassium/magnesium with kidney disease; stimulant fat burners with hypertension or heart disease; long international shipping times with expiry-sensitive products; customs seizure.

### 7.7 Concise action plan
1. Buy a **kitchen scale** and **6 containers**. Stop there for a month.
2. Reallocate any supplement budget to eggs/chicken/fish.
3. If constipated: add **psyllium 5 g/day** with a full glass of water, 2 h from any medication.
4. If training consistently: **creatine 3–5 g/day** (plain monohydrate).
5. If you sweat heavily or fast > 24 h: **a pinch of salt in water**, not a product.
6. Verify every price locally before buying — and note whether it was **Verified local price**, **Estimated**, or **Unknown**.
